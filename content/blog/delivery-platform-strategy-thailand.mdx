I had a client last year -- a Thai-Italian restaurant in Ari doing solid dine-in numbers -- who told me he had "tried delivery" and it did not work. When I asked what he meant, he said he had signed up for GrabFood, uploaded his full dine-in menu, and waited. Three months later he was averaging 8 orders a day, losing money on most of them, and ready to quit.

The problem was not delivery. The problem was that he treated a platform listing like a passive storefront. Upload and forget. That approach might have worked in 2020 when platforms were subsidizing everything to acquire merchants, but in 2026 -- with a duopoly controlling 86% of the market and commissions sitting at 25-35% -- delivery requires an active, deliberate strategy or it will quietly drain your margins.

After we rebuilt his delivery operation -- trimmed the menu to 14 items, repriced for platform economics, launched on LINE MAN, set up a dedicated prep station, and ran a 6-week promotional push -- his delivery orders climbed to 45 per day with a 22% net margin per order. Not spectacular. But 45 profitable orders a day on top of his dine-in revenue changed his monthly P&L completely.

This article is the full playbook. I will cover the two remaining platforms and one Thai-owned alternative, break down their commission structures and tools, walk through delivery menu optimization, and lay out the operational systems that separate restaurants making money on delivery from those bleeding it.

## The Platform Landscape in 2026

Thailand's food delivery market hit roughly 86 billion THB in 2025 and is projected to reach 93-95 billion THB in 2026. After [Foodpanda's exit in May 2025](/blog/thailand-food-delivery-2026), the market consolidated into two dominant players and one smaller Thai-owned challenger.

**GrabFood** holds approximately 46% market share nationally, with even higher penetration in Bangkok. It absorbed most of Foodpanda's customer base and has the largest rider fleet in the country.

**LINE MAN Wongnai** holds approximately 40% market share. Its integration with LINE -- Thailand's dominant messaging app with 54 million active users -- gives it a unique discovery and engagement advantage, while the Wongnai review platform feeds restaurant visibility.

**Robinhood** holds roughly 3-4% market share. Developed by SCB (Siam Commercial Bank), it launched in 2020 with a zero-commission model to undercut the major platforms. The commission structure has since changed, but it remains the cheapest platform option for merchants.

The remaining 10-14% is split across direct channels (LINE OA ordering, restaurant websites), niche aggregators, and restaurant-operated delivery.

<Callout type="info">
Being on a single platform in 2026 is leaving 40-54% of the delivery market untouched. The incremental cost of managing a second or third platform listing is minimal -- menu setup, tablet management, and occasional promotion adjustments. Unless you have a specific reason to be exclusive (a negotiated commission reduction, for example), list on both major platforms from day one.
</Callout>

## GrabFood: The Market Leader

### Commission Structure

**Standard commission:** 30% of order subtotal. This is the baseline rate for most merchants signing up today.

**Preferred partner commission:** 35%. Higher commission buys you priority search placement, badge visibility, and inclusion in curated collections. Only worthwhile if the volume increase exceeds the 5% commission premium -- which it often does for restaurants in competitive zones like Thonglor or Silom.

**High-volume negotiated rate:** 25-28%. Available to merchants consistently doing 100+ orders per day or chains with 10+ outlets. You need to ask; Grab will not offer this proactively.

**Self-delivery rate:** 15-18%. You provide your own delivery riders, Grab handles the platform, ordering interface, and payment processing. This only makes sense within a 3 km radius with reliable riders -- beyond that, delivery times spike and ratings suffer.

**Payment terms:** Weekly settlement every Monday for the previous week. Grab deducts commission, advertising spend, and any promotional charges before paying you.

### Merchant Tools

**GrabMerchant app:** The core dashboard for managing your listing. You can update menu items, adjust prices, toggle availability, view order analytics, and manage promotions. The analytics are decent -- you can see top-selling items, peak order times, average preparation time, and customer ratings by period.

**GrabAds:** Sponsored search placement within the app. Cost ranges from 3-8 THB per click depending on location and competition. Conversion rates (click to order) typically run 8-15%. At a 10% conversion rate and 5 THB per click, your cost per acquired order is 50 THB -- affordable if your average order value is above 180 THB.

**Promo Manager:** Self-serve tool for setting up discounts, free delivery, buy-one-get-one, and bundle promotions. You set the discount and Grab shows it prominently in search results. Some promotions are co-funded (Grab pays 30-50% of the discount), especially during mega-sale events.

### Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strengths:** Largest customer base, most reliable rider fleet, strongest brand recognition, best analytics dashboard, largest advertising inventory.

**Weaknesses:** Highest standard commission among the three platforms, less flexible on commission negotiation for small merchants, customer support can be slow for merchant-side issues.

## LINE MAN Wongnai: The Local Contender

### Commission Structure

**Standard commission:** 27-30% of order subtotal. The baseline sits slightly lower than Grab.

**High-rated merchant rate:** 25%. LINE MAN offers commission reductions for restaurants maintaining 4.5+ star ratings with consistent order volumes. This is not automatic -- you need to ask your account manager, but they are more flexible than Grab on commission for SMEs.

**Self-delivery rate:** 12-15%. The lowest self-delivery commission among the three platforms, reflecting LINE MAN's push to reduce logistics costs.

**Large chain rate:** 22-25%. Negotiable for chains with significant volume.

**Payment terms:** Weekly settlement, similar to Grab.

### Merchant Tools

**LINE MAN Merchant Center:** Dashboard for menu management, order tracking, and performance analytics. Functional but less polished than GrabMerchant. The key advantage is integration with Wongnai -- your Wongnai restaurant profile feeds directly into your LINE MAN listing, so reviews, photos, and ratings on Wongnai boost your visibility on LINE MAN.

**Wongnai advertising:** Sponsored listings on Wongnai's restaurant review platform, which is deeply integrated with LINE MAN search. Cost: 2-6 THB per click, generally cheaper than GrabAds because of lower competition for inventory.

**LINE ecosystem integration:** LINE MAN can push promotions through LINE's messaging platform, reaching 54 million Thai users. LINE OA (Official Account) integration lets you broadcast promotions to your followers and convert them directly into LINE MAN orders. This cross-platform synergy is LINE MAN's biggest competitive advantage.

### Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strengths:** Lower standard commission, Wongnai review integration drives organic discovery, LINE ecosystem reach (54 million users), more flexible on commission negotiation for smaller operators, strong among Thai consumer segments.

**Weaknesses:** Slightly smaller rider fleet than Grab (leading to longer delivery times in some areas), lower average order values (160-200 THB vs Grab's 180-220 THB), merchant tools are less sophisticated.

<Callout type="tip">
LINE MAN's lower commission does not automatically mean higher net revenue per order. Grab's larger customer base often generates more total orders and higher average order values. Run the numbers for your specific restaurant: track net revenue per order on each platform for 30 days, then allocate your advertising budget toward whichever platform generates more profit per baht spent.
</Callout>

## Robinhood: The Thai Alternative

### Commission Structure

**Standard commission:** 10-15% of order subtotal. Robinhood launched with zero commission and has gradually introduced fees, but it remains dramatically cheaper than Grab or LINE MAN.

**Advertising and promotion fees:** Robinhood monetizes through optional advertising rather than commission. Featured placement costs 1,000-5,000 THB per campaign, and promoted listings start at 1-3 THB per click.

**Payment terms:** Bi-weekly settlement.

### Why Consider Robinhood

At 10-15% commission versus 27-35% on the major platforms, the math per order is significantly better. On a 200 THB order:

- **GrabFood (30%):** You receive 140 THB
- **LINE MAN (27%):** You receive 146 THB
- **Robinhood (12%):** You receive 176 THB

That 30-36 THB difference per order is pure margin. At 20 Robinhood orders per day, you save 18,000-21,600 THB per month compared to the same volume on Grab.

### Why Robinhood Is Not a Primary Platform

**Small customer base.** Robinhood's 3-4% market share means far fewer potential orders. You will not build your delivery revenue on Robinhood alone.

**Smaller rider fleet.** Fewer riders means longer delivery times, which means lower customer satisfaction and fewer repeat orders.

**Limited merchant tools.** The dashboard is basic compared to Grab or LINE MAN. Analytics are minimal, and advertising options are limited.

**Brand awareness gap.** Most Bangkok consumers open Grab or LINE MAN by default when ordering food. Robinhood requires active consumer choice, which limits order volume.

### The Right Role for Robinhood

Treat Robinhood as a margin-improvement supplement, not a primary channel. List on Robinhood alongside Grab and LINE MAN. Every order that comes through Robinhood instead of a major platform is 30-36 THB more in your pocket. Even 10-15 Robinhood orders per day adds 9,000-16,200 THB per month in margin improvement with zero additional effort beyond the initial setup.

## Direct Ordering via LINE OA

The fourth "platform" is not a platform at all -- it is your own ordering channel, and it offers the best economics of any delivery option.

**How it works:** You create a LINE Official Account (free for basic, 1,200-4,000 THB/month for upgraded features), build a menu using LINE's rich menu or integrate a third-party ordering system (FoodStory, Ocha, or a custom LIFF app), and customers order by messaging your LINE OA.

**Effective commission equivalent:** 5-15% all-in (ordering system fee + delivery logistics). Compare that to 27-35% on platforms.

**Delivery logistics options:**
- **Own riders:** 15,000-20,000 THB per rider per month (salary, fuel, insurance). Best within a 3 km radius.
- **Lalamove/Skootar:** 40-80 THB per delivery. Viable for 10-30 orders per day.
- **LINE MAN delivery-only:** You can use LINE MAN's rider fleet without their platform commission through a logistics-only arrangement.

**The catch:** No built-in traffic. Grab and LINE MAN have millions of users browsing for food. Your LINE OA only reaches people who have already added you. Building that audience requires consistent marketing -- QR codes on every delivery package, social media pushes, in-store signage, and a reason (lower prices, loyalty rewards, exclusive items) for customers to order direct.

## Optimizing Your Delivery Menu

A delivery menu is not a copy of your dine-in menu. It is a separate product designed for a different cost structure, a different eating context, and a different set of constraints. Here is how to build one that makes money.

### Fewer Items, Higher Margin

Your delivery menu should have 12-20 items. Not 40. Not 60. Twelve to twenty.

A focused menu reduces kitchen complexity, speeds up preparation time (which improves your platform ranking), decreases ingredient waste, and simplifies inventory management. Delivery customers make purchasing decisions in 30-60 seconds of scrolling, so a concise menu with clear categories and compelling photos converts better than an overwhelming list.

**What to include:**
- 6-10 mains (your bestsellers that travel well, repriced for delivery economics)
- 2-4 sides and add-ons (high margin, encourage larger order values)
- 2-3 drinks (Thai iced tea, soft drinks, juices -- high margin, low prep time)
- 1-2 desserts (items that hold up in transit)

**What to exclude:**
- Anything with food cost above 35% of the delivery price (after commission, you are losing money)
- Crispy items that go soggy in 20-30 minutes (unless you invest in vented packaging)
- Elaborate plated dishes that look terrible in a box
- Very low-priced items under 80 THB (the kitchen time and packaging cost are not justified)
- Items requiring immediate consumption (souffles, delicate salads, anything temperature-critical)

### Delivery-Friendly Dishes

Think about what happens to your food during a 20-40 minute journey in a delivery bag on the back of a motorcycle in Bangkok traffic. Design around that reality.

**Travels well:** Rice dishes with curry or stir-fry (sauce separate), fried chicken, burgers (wrapped properly), pasta with sauce on the side, grain bowls, bao buns, spring rolls in a vented container, sealed soups, congee.

**Needs engineering to travel:** Noodle dishes (sauce separate, noodles slightly undercooked so they do not turn to mush), salads (dressing separate, kept cold), fried items (vented container to maintain crispness).

**Avoid for delivery:** Tempura, delicate sashimi presentations, anything with a crispy bottom that will steam and soften in a sealed container, multi-element plates that require assembly.

### Pricing for Platform Economics

Platform commission changes the math on every item. A dish that is profitable at your dine-in price may lose money on delivery after 30% commission and packaging costs.

**The repricing formula:** Take your dine-in price, add 15-25%, and confirm the result still hits a contribution margin of at least 50% after commission and packaging. Both Grab and LINE MAN allow you to set platform-specific prices that differ from your dine-in menu, and customers expect delivery prices to be slightly higher.

**Example: Green curry with rice**
- Dine-in price: 160 THB
- Food cost: 52 THB (32.5%)
- Dine-in contribution margin: 108 THB (67.5%)

On GrabFood at the same 160 THB price:
- Revenue after 30% commission: 112 THB
- Food cost: 52 THB
- Packaging: 14 THB
- Contribution margin: 46 THB (28.7% of menu price)

Repriced to 195 THB for delivery:
- Revenue after 30% commission: 136.50 THB
- Food cost: 52 THB
- Packaging: 14 THB
- Contribution margin: 70.50 THB (36.2% of menu price)

That repricing moves the dish from barely viable to comfortably profitable. Your customer pays 35 THB more and still lands within the 150-250 THB delivery sweet spot.

For a complete framework on analyzing every menu item's delivery profitability, see our [menu engineering guide](/blog/menu-engineering-food-delivery-thailand).

### Bundle Strategy

Delivery bundles increase average order value while giving the customer a perception of savings. They are one of the most effective levers for improving delivery unit economics.

**Structure:** Main + side + drink at 10-15% below the sum of individual items.

**Example:** "Delivery Set A: Green curry + jasmine rice + Thai iced tea" -- individual total 275 THB, bundle price 239 THB. The customer saves 36 THB (13% discount). Your blended food cost on the bundle is 70 THB (29.3%), and after Grab's 30% commission (71.70 THB) and packaging (16 THB), your contribution margin is 81.30 THB (34% of bundle price).

I have seen bundles increase average order value by 25-40% on delivery platforms. The key is making the bundle the obvious choice -- place it at the top of your platform listing, photograph it as a complete meal, and price the individual items just high enough that the bundle feels like genuine value.

## Commission Negotiation Tactics

You cannot change the platforms' listed commission rates. But you can negotiate, and the leverage you bring depends on your volume and how you position the conversation.

### What the Platforms Care About

Before negotiating, understand what matters to Grab and LINE MAN's account teams:

**Order volume.** More orders means more commission revenue. A restaurant doing 100 orders per day at 30% commission generates more for the platform than one doing 20 orders at 35%.

**Customer retention.** Platforms track reorder rates. A restaurant with high reorder rates is more valuable because it keeps users on the platform. If customers love your food and keep coming back, the platform benefits.

**Rating and speed.** High-rated, fast restaurants improve the platform's overall customer experience. Platforms will offer better terms to keep 4.7+ rated restaurants happy.

**Promotional participation.** Platforms want merchants who participate in mega-campaigns, seasonal promotions, and co-funded discounts. Willingness to participate is a bargaining chip.

### Negotiation by Volume Tier

**Under 30 orders per day:** Minimal leverage. Focus on maximizing new-merchant discounts (typically 20-25% commission for 1-3 months), maintaining high ratings for LINE MAN's tiered commission, and participating in every co-funded promotion. Do not waste time trying to negotiate custom terms at this volume.

**30-100 orders per day:** Request a dedicated account manager (usually assigned at 50+ orders/day). At contract renewal (typically 12 months), present your volume data, customer rating, and promotional participation record, then ask for a 2-3 percentage point reduction. Frame it as a partnership: "I want to invest more in platform advertising and promotions, but I need commission relief to make the numbers work."

**100+ orders per day:** You are in the top 5-10% of merchants. Negotiate to 25-28% commission, request free advertising credits (10,000-20,000 THB/month), push for priority placement during mega-campaigns, and negotiate faster payment terms. Come with data: monthly order volume, average order value, reorder rate, and customer rating. If you have multiple outlets, negotiate a group rate.

**Multi-brand operators:** If you run 2-4 brands from a [cloud kitchen](/blog/cloud-kitchen-bangkok), your combined volume across all brands gives you more leverage than each brand individually. Negotiate a master commission rate that applies to all your brands.

<Callout type="warning">
Never threaten to leave a platform unless you genuinely will. Saying "I will go exclusive with LINE MAN" when 70% of your delivery revenue comes from Grab is not credible. The platform's account managers know your numbers. Instead, offer something in return for better terms -- higher promotional spend, participation in new features, willingness to trial new tools. Make the negotiation collaborative, not adversarial.
</Callout>

### The Exclusivity Trade-Off

Both platforms occasionally offer commission reductions of 3-5 percentage points in exchange for exclusivity or "preferred platform" status. I generally advise against full exclusivity because it cuts your addressable market by 40-46%. But a soft arrangement -- 70-80% of your promotional budget directed to one platform, first access to new features, co-branded marketing -- can yield meaningful concessions without locking you out of the other platform entirely.

## Managing Multiple Platforms Simultaneously

Running delivery across two or three platforms creates operational complexity. Here is how to manage it without your kitchen descending into chaos during peak hours.

### Tablet and Order Management

Each platform requires its own tablet or device for receiving orders. That means 2-3 tablets on your kitchen counter, each dinging with incoming orders, each requiring separate acceptance and management.

**Solution 1: Aggregator POS.** Systems like FoodStory, Ocha, or Loyverse can aggregate orders from multiple platforms into a single interface. Monthly cost: 1,500-5,000 THB. This is worth the investment once you are doing 30+ delivery orders per day across platforms.

**Solution 2: Dedicated delivery station.** Assign one staff member to manage all delivery tablets, accept orders, print tickets, and coordinate with the kitchen. This person also handles packaging, rider handoff, and order accuracy checks. At 40+ delivery orders per day, this role pays for itself in fewer errors, faster preparation, and better ratings.

**Solution 3: Kitchen display system.** For high-volume operations (80+ orders per day), a kitchen display system that pulls orders from all platforms into a single screen with priority queuing. Cost: 5,000-15,000 THB for setup plus monthly software fees. But the efficiency gains at volume are substantial.

### Menu Consistency Across Platforms

Your menu does not need to be identical on every platform, but core items and pricing should be consistent. Customers who order from you on Grab and later find your LINE MAN listing will notice if the same dish costs 20% more on one platform.

**What should be consistent:** Core menu items, pricing (within a 5% range), food photography, brand presentation.

**What can differ:** Platform-specific promotions, exclusive bundles designed for each platform's user behavior, and promotional pricing during platform-specific campaigns.

### Promotion Coordination

Running promotions on multiple platforms simultaneously can overload your kitchen. Stagger promotions -- run a Grab campaign Monday through Wednesday, a LINE MAN campaign Thursday through Saturday -- to spread the volume. Simultaneous promotions on all platforms during the same peak hour will result in orders you cannot fulfill on time, which tanks your ratings on every platform at once.

## Delivery-Only vs Hybrid Model

Should you go delivery-only (cloud kitchen) or run delivery alongside dine-in?

### Delivery-Only (Cloud Kitchen)

**When it works:** You are testing a concept with minimal capital, operating a multi-brand strategy from a shared kitchen, or building a delivery-native brand that does not need a storefront.

**Economics:** No dine-in revenue as a safety net, so platform commission is your single largest cost. Net margins per order are 10-20% after commission, food cost, packaging, rent, and labor. Profitability requires volume -- typically 50+ orders per day for a single brand, 100+ for multi-brand.

**Startup cost:** 500,000-2,000,000 THB for shared kitchen; 1-3 million THB for standalone.

For the full cloud kitchen playbook, see our [cloud kitchen guide](/blog/cloud-kitchen-bangkok).

### Hybrid Model (Dine-In + Delivery)

**When it works:** You have an existing restaurant with kitchen capacity during off-peak hours, or you are opening a new restaurant and want delivery revenue from day one.

**Economics:** Dine-in provides the higher-margin base revenue (no commission, higher average check), while delivery provides incremental volume during slow periods and exposure to customers who may later dine in. The blended margin across dine-in and delivery is healthier than either channel alone.

**The capacity question:** If your kitchen can handle 80 dine-in covers per service and 30 delivery orders per hour during peak, you are fine. If adding 30 delivery orders during a full dine-in service means food quality or speed drops for either channel, you have a capacity problem. Solve it with a dedicated delivery prep station, staggered delivery promotion timing (push delivery during 2-5 PM when dine-in is slow), or limiting delivery order acceptance during dine-in peaks.

My recommendation for most restaurant operators: start hybrid. Delivery adds revenue without requiring a separate location, and you can test demand levels, menu performance, and operational workflows before committing to a standalone delivery operation.

## Photography Requirements

Your listing photos are the single biggest factor in a customer's decision to order. On a delivery platform, you have 2-3 seconds of attention as someone scrolls past your listing. Bad photos mean no clicks. No clicks mean no orders. No orders mean no data, which means the algorithm buries you further.

**Professional food photography:** Budget 8,000-20,000 THB per brand for a shoot covering 15-20 dishes. Use a food photographer with delivery-platform experience -- the composition needs to work as small thumbnails on a phone screen, which is a different discipline from magazine food photography.

**Photo specifications:**
- Bright, overhead shots (45-degree or directly above) on a clean, solid background
- Food should fill 70-80% of the frame
- Show the dish as it arrives in the delivery container, not plated on fine china
- Minimum resolution: 1200x800 pixels (Grab and LINE MAN both require this)
- Avoid filters, heavy post-processing, or unrealistic color saturation -- customers feel deceived when the delivered food looks nothing like the photo

**Update frequency:** Refresh your photos every 6-12 months or whenever you change menu items. Seasonal refreshes (new photos, new backgrounds, promotional banners) give your listing a visual bump that can increase click-through rates by 10-15%.

**DIY option:** If budget is tight, use a modern smartphone with good natural lighting (near a window, between 10 AM and 2 PM), a white or light-colored surface, and minimal styling. Not as good as professional, but far better than dark, blurry phone photos taken under fluorescent kitchen lighting.

## Promotion Strategies by Platform

### GrabFood Promotions

**Free delivery:** Grab subsidizes part of the delivery fee; you may contribute 10-20 THB per order. High impact on order volume, moderate cost. Best for acquiring new customers.

**Percentage discount (15-25% off):** Co-funded with Grab during mega-campaigns (Grab covers 30-50% of the discount). Run these during slow periods (Monday-Wednesday) to fill capacity.

**Bundle deals:** "Order 300 THB, get a free side." Increases average order value while the "free" item costs you 15-25 THB in food cost. Excellent ROI.

**Sponsored search:** Paid placement at the top of category and keyword results. Budget 500-2,000 THB per day during promotional periods. Track conversion rates and pause campaigns with cost-per-order above 60 THB.

### LINE MAN Promotions

**LINE OA broadcast + LINE MAN coupon:** Send a coupon to your LINE OA followers that is redeemable on LINE MAN. This combination leverages LINE MAN's strongest asset -- LINE ecosystem integration. Cost: the coupon value plus 0.15-0.25 THB per broadcast message (on upgraded LINE OA plans).

**Wongnai-featured placement:** Being featured on Wongnai's "recommended" lists drives significant discovery on LINE MAN. This is partially organic (high Wongnai ratings help) and partially paid (sponsored placement campaigns). Budget 5,000-15,000 THB per campaign.

**Flash sales:** Time-limited promotions (2 hours during lunch or dinner) with 20-30% discounts. LINE MAN pushes these to users in your delivery radius. High-impact for generating volume spikes and testing demand.

### Robinhood Promotions

Robinhood's promotional tools are more limited, but the lower commission means you can afford deeper discounts from your own margin. A 15% discount on Robinhood still leaves you with better per-order economics than full-price on Grab.

## Handling Reviews and Ratings

Your platform rating is the second most important factor (after photos) driving organic order volume. A 4.5-star restaurant gets roughly 3x the organic visibility of a 4.0-star restaurant on both Grab and LINE MAN.

### Rating Management

**Respond to every negative review within 24 hours.** Acknowledge the issue, apologize briefly, and state what you will do to fix it. Both platforms show your response publicly, and future customers read it.

**Fix recurring complaints immediately.** If three customers in a week mention soggy packaging, fix the packaging today, not next month. One pattern of complaints can drop your rating from 4.6 to 4.3 in two weeks, and recovering those 0.3 points takes months.

**Include a review card in every order.** A simple card saying "Enjoying your meal? Rate us 5 stars!" costs 0.50 THB and nudges satisfied customers to leave reviews. Without the nudge, most happy customers say nothing while unhappy customers complain loudly.

**Do not incentivize reviews.** Offering free items or discounts for 5-star reviews violates both platforms' terms and can get your listing suspended.

### Common Delivery Complaints and Fixes

**"Food arrived cold."** Upgrade to insulated containers or foil-lined packaging. Separate hot and cold items. Designate a holding station with heat lamps for orders waiting for riders.

**"Missing items."** Implement a double-check process: the cook prepares the order, a second person verifies it against the ticket before sealing the bag. At scale, this one step reduces missing-item complaints by 70-80%.

**"Packaging leaked."** Switch to sealed containers with rubber gaskets for curries, soups, and sauced dishes. Never rely on snap-fit lids for liquids -- they open during transport. The extra 2-3 THB per container saves you negative reviews.

**"Took too long."** Improve kitchen prep speed (pre-portioned ingredients, pre-filled sauce containers, dedicated delivery station) or increase your estimated preparation time on the platform. A realistic 18-minute estimate that you consistently meet is better than an optimistic 10-minute estimate that you routinely miss.

<Callout type="tip">
Track your rating weekly on a simple spreadsheet: date, GrabFood rating, LINE MAN rating, number of new reviews, common complaint themes. This takes 5 minutes per week and gives you a trend line. When you see a dip, investigate immediately. Ratings are a lagging indicator -- by the time you notice a drop, the underlying problem has been happening for days.
</Callout>

## When to Consider Going Direct-Only

Some restaurants eventually reach a point where the platform model no longer makes sense and they cut ties entirely. This is rare, but it happens. Here are the conditions where it works:

**Strong brand with loyal following.** You have 5,000+ LINE OA followers, an active social media presence, and customers who seek you out specifically. Your brand is the draw, not the platform's discovery function.

**High average order value (350+ THB).** At higher order values, the commission premium per order is larger. A 30% commission on a 400 THB order is 120 THB -- that is enough to fund your own delivery rider for the order with margin to spare.

**Dense delivery radius.** Your customers are concentrated within a 3-5 km radius, making your own delivery logistics efficient and fast.

**Established reorder rate.** 40%+ of your delivery orders are from repeat customers. These customers do not need the platform for discovery -- they already know you.

For most restaurants, the right answer is not going direct-only but building a mixed channel: 60-70% platform (for discovery and new customers) and 30-40% direct (for repeat customers and margin). For the complete analysis, see our [delivery market article](/blog/thailand-food-delivery-2026).

## Putting It All Together: A 90-Day Platform Launch Plan

If you are starting delivery from scratch or resetting a failing delivery operation, here is the sequence:

**Week 1-2: Foundation**
- Audit your dine-in menu for delivery-viable items (travel well, margin above 35% after commission)
- Design a 14-18 item delivery menu with 2-3 bundles
- Set delivery-specific prices (15-25% above dine-in)
- Hire a food photographer and schedule a shoot (8,000-15,000 THB)
- Set up a dedicated delivery prep station in your kitchen

**Week 3-4: Launch**
- Sign up on GrabFood, LINE MAN, and Robinhood simultaneously
- Upload menus, photos, and complete your merchant profiles (description, operating hours, address)
- Set up a LINE OA and include the QR code on all delivery packaging
- Order branded packaging (minimum 500 units per item type, budget 10,000-20,000 THB)
- Brief kitchen staff on delivery workflow, packaging standards, and quality checks

**Month 2: Accelerate**
- Activate platform advertising on Grab and LINE MAN (15,000-25,000 THB combined budget)
- Run a "new restaurant" promotion (free delivery or 15% off first order) for 2 weeks
- Monitor ratings daily and fix any recurring complaints immediately
- Track per-order economics on each platform weekly
- Adjust menu if certain items are consistently low-rated or low-margin

**Month 3: Optimize**
- Analyze 60 days of data: top items, peak hours, platform mix, ratings, and cost per order
- Negotiate with your Grab and LINE MAN account managers (request ad credits, commission review)
- Begin shifting repeat customers to LINE OA ordering (insert QR code cards, offer 10% direct order discount)
- Evaluate adding or removing menu items based on performance data
- Set monthly targets for order volume, average order value, and contribution margin

By the end of 90 days, you should have a clear picture of your delivery P&L, your best-performing platform, and a plan for scaling volume while protecting margins.

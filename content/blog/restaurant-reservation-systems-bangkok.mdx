A client of mine in Thonglor -- 45-seat modern Thai, solid reviews, 800-1,200 THB average check -- told me last year that he was losing around 120,000 THB per month to no-shows. He knew this because he had started counting: an average of 3-4 tables per night that were booked but never filled, each representing two covers at his average spend. On Friday and Saturday nights the number climbed to 5-6 tables. He had turned away walk-in guests to hold those empty seats, and by the time it was clear the reservation was not coming, the walk-in traffic had moved on.

He was managing bookings through a paper diary and his personal LINE account. No confirmation system, no deposit mechanism, no way to flag repeat offenders, and no data on how bad the problem actually was until he sat down with a calculator one Sunday.

That conversation is why I now push every restaurant above a certain size and price point to think seriously about reservation management. Not because the software is exciting -- it is not -- but because the gap between a system and no system is often the difference between 8% and 12% of your revenue evaporating into empty chairs.

This article covers the reservation platforms available in Thailand, how to pick one that fits your operation, the no-show problem and what actually works to control it, managing walk-ins alongside reservations, and using reservation data to build a smarter business.

## Why Bangkok Restaurants Need a Reservation System

Bangkok has a walk-in culture. Compared to cities like Tokyo or New York, where booking ahead is default behavior, Bangkok diners often decide where to eat 30 minutes before they arrive. This creates a specific set of problems that reservation systems need to address.

**No-show rates are higher here.** Global restaurant no-show averages hover around 10-15%. In Bangkok, I see 15-25% at restaurants that do not use confirmation or deposit systems. The combination of a spontaneous dining culture, easy access to alternatives (there is always another restaurant next door), and limited social friction around canceling means diners treat reservations as soft holds rather than firm commitments.

**Peak demand is extremely concentrated.** Bangkok's dinner rush hits hard between 18:30 and 20:30. A 50-seat restaurant might turn away 15-20 groups during that 2-hour window while holding empty tables for no-shows. Without a system to manage this, you are making staffing and prep decisions based on reservation counts that do not reflect reality.

**Walk-in management matters more here.** Because of the walk-in culture, a reservation system that only handles bookings and ignores walk-in flow is incomplete. You need to manage both streams, and the best systems help you balance reservations against expected walk-in demand.

**LINE is the communication layer.** In Thailand, restaurant communication happens through LINE, not email or phone. Any reservation system that relies heavily on email confirmations is fighting the local communication norm. The systems that work best here either integrate with LINE or operate through it natively.

**Group dining is frequent and high-value.** Thai dining culture skews toward larger groups -- 4-8 people is common for dinner, and groups of 10-20 for celebrations are routine. Managing group reservations, especially for set menus or special packages, requires coordination that a paper diary handles poorly.

<Callout type="info">
Not every restaurant needs a reservation system. If you run a casual restaurant with an average check under 300 THB, minimal wait times, and no table turn pressure, the cost and complexity of a booking system may not be justified. This article is aimed at restaurants where table allocation directly impacts revenue -- typically 300+ THB average check, 40+ seats, and regular wait times during peak hours.
</Callout>

## Reservation Platforms Available in Thailand

The market here is different from Europe or the US, where OpenTable and Resy dominate. Thailand has a mix of regional platforms, global entrants, and local workarounds. Each has a different model.

### Chope

**Overview:** Chope is the most established restaurant reservation platform in Southeast Asia, headquartered in Singapore with strong coverage in Bangkok. They list hundreds of Bangkok restaurants and have built brand recognition with the dining public.

**How it works:** Diners book through the Chope app or website. The restaurant receives bookings through Chope's merchant dashboard. Chope provides a table management system alongside the booking platform.

**Pricing model:**
- **Commission-based:** Chope charges per seated diner, typically 20-50 THB per cover, depending on the restaurant tier and negotiated rate
- **No monthly subscription** on the basic plan -- you pay only for covers that Chope sends you
- **Premium features** (advanced table management, CRM tools, marketing placement) are available on paid tiers, negotiated directly

**Strengths:**
- Largest diner base in Southeast Asia -- genuine discovery channel that brings new customers
- Built-in confirmation system (email + push notification) that reduces no-shows
- Table management tools are competent, not best-in-class but functional
- Chope deals and promotions can drive volume during slow periods
- Reporting shows booking patterns, no-show rates, and diner demographics

**Weaknesses:**
- Commission model means you pay for every cover, including diners who would have come anyway through your own channels
- You do not own the customer relationship -- Chope owns the diner data and markets to them directly
- Limited LINE integration, which matters in Thailand
- The platform is diner-centric, not restaurant-centric -- features are designed to benefit the booking experience, not necessarily your operational needs
- Cancellation and no-show policies are controlled partly by the platform, reducing your flexibility

**Best for:** Restaurants that want a discovery channel and can absorb the per-cover commission. Particularly useful for new restaurants building awareness, or established restaurants in tourist areas where Chope's international user base brings incremental covers.

### Hungry Hub

**Overview:** Hungry Hub is a Thai-founded platform that combines reservation booking with fixed-price dining packages. It is particularly strong in Bangkok's mid-range to upper-range segment and has built a loyal user base around its "all-you-can-eat" and set-menu deals.

**How it works:** Restaurants create packages (set menus, buffet deals, special occasion sets) on the platform. Diners prepay through Hungry Hub when booking. The prepayment model means no-shows are near zero -- diners have already paid.

**Pricing model:**
- **Commission:** 15-25% of the package price, depending on the deal structure and restaurant negotiation
- **Prepayment:** Diners pay in advance through the platform, and Hungry Hub settles with the restaurant after the dining date (typically within 7-14 days)
- **No monthly fee** for basic listing

**Strengths:**
- Prepayment eliminates no-shows almost entirely -- the biggest operational advantage of any platform on this list
- Strong Thai user base, especially for special occasions and group dining
- Package model can fill seats during slow periods without discounting your regular menu
- Good for promoting set menus, seasonal packages, and celebration deals
- LINE notification integration for booking confirmations

**Weaknesses:**
- Commission is high relative to other platforms -- 15-25% of the package price, not per cover
- The package model does not work for all restaurant types -- a la carte-focused fine dining may not fit naturally
- Settlement delay (7-14 days) affects your cash flow
- Hungry Hub's brand is associated with deals and value, which may not align with premium positioning
- You are selling packages, not just table space -- requires menu engineering for the platform specifically

**Best for:** Restaurants that want to fill seats during slow periods, promote set-menu packages, or solve the no-show problem through prepayment. Particularly effective for group dining, celebrations, and buffet-style operations.

<Callout type="tip">
Hungry Hub's prepayment model is the closest thing to a guaranteed no-show solution. If your no-show rate exceeds 20% and you are losing significant revenue to empty tables, listing specific time slots or packages on Hungry Hub while keeping your core reservations on another platform gives you a blended approach -- guaranteed covers from Hungry Hub and flexibility from your primary booking system.
</Callout>

### Quandoo

**Overview:** Quandoo is a Berlin-based global reservation platform with operations in Asia, including Thailand. It is smaller than Chope in the Bangkok market but offers a clean booking experience and international reach.

**Pricing model:**
- **Commission per cover:** Typically 25-60 THB per seated diner, depending on restaurant tier
- **Monthly subscription option:** Some markets offer a flat monthly fee instead of per-cover commission -- check current Thai pricing
- **Table management tools** included in the platform

**Strengths:**
- International user base that brings tourists and expat diners
- Integration with Google Reserve (diners can book directly from Google Maps)
- Clean merchant dashboard with decent reporting
- Automated confirmation and reminder messages (email and SMS)

**Weaknesses:**
- Smaller diner base in Bangkok compared to Chope and Hungry Hub
- Limited Thai-language features and support
- Less local brand recognition -- Thai diners are less likely to search for restaurants on Quandoo than on Chope or Hungry Hub
- The commission model has the same cost concerns as Chope

**Best for:** Restaurants in tourist-heavy areas (Sukhumvit, Silom, Khaosan, riverside) that want international exposure and Google Reserve integration.

### Google Reserve

**Overview:** Not a standalone platform but rather Google's booking integration that appears in Google Maps and Search results. When a diner searches for your restaurant and a "Reserve a Table" button appears in your Google Business Profile, that is Google Reserve.

**How it works:** Google partners with booking platforms (Quandoo and others) to enable bookings. You do not work with Google directly -- you sign up with a Google Reserve partner, and their booking widget appears in your Google listing.

**Pricing model:** Depends on the partner platform. Quandoo's Google Reserve integration carries their standard commission.

**Strengths:**
- Massive exposure -- your booking button appears where diners are already looking (Google Maps)
- Zero friction for the diner, which increases conversion from search to booking
- No separate app download required for the diner

**Weaknesses:**
- You must use a partner platform, which adds their commission
- Limited customization of the booking experience
- Not widely adopted in Thailand yet -- many Bangkok restaurants do not have Google Reserve enabled
- No direct relationship with Google for support or features

**Best for:** Any restaurant already using Quandoo or another Google Reserve partner. The incremental cost is zero (it uses your existing platform), and the visibility is free.

### LINE OA Booking

**Overview:** LINE Official Account is not a reservation platform -- it is a communication tool. But in Thailand, many restaurants use LINE OA as their primary booking channel, managing reservations through chat messages.

**How it works:** Diners add your restaurant's LINE OA, send a message to book, and your staff confirms via chat. Some restaurants automate part of this with LINE chatbot features or form-based booking through LINE's rich menu.

**Pricing model:**
- **LINE OA Free plan:** Up to 200 messages/month, basic chat
- **LINE OA Light plan:** 500 messages/month, approximately 300-500 THB/month
- **LINE OA Pro plan:** Unlimited messages, approximately 1,500-2,500 THB/month
- **No commission per cover** -- you own the customer relationship entirely

**Strengths:**
- Free or extremely low cost compared to commission-based platforms
- Native to how Thai diners communicate -- LINE is the default messaging app in Thailand
- You own the customer data and the ongoing relationship
- Rich menu and chatbot features can semi-automate the booking process
- Easy to send confirmations, reminders, and post-visit follow-ups
- Integrates with your broader LINE OA marketing (promotions, loyalty, announcements)

**Weaknesses:**
- No built-in table management -- you need a separate system or a spreadsheet to track table allocation
- Manual process unless you invest in chatbot automation (which requires technical setup)
- No discovery channel -- diners need to already know your restaurant and have added your LINE OA
- Confirmation and reminder messages require staff effort to send (unless automated)
- No built-in no-show management, deposit collection, or credit card hold functionality
- Difficult to analyze booking data without separate tracking

**Best for:** Small to mid-sized restaurants with a loyal customer base who are already using LINE OA for marketing. Works well as a complementary booking channel alongside a platform like Chope or Hungry Hub.

<Callout type="info">
Many successful Bangkok restaurants use a hybrid approach: a platform like Chope or Hungry Hub for discovery and new customer bookings, plus LINE OA for regular customers who prefer booking through chat. The platform brings new diners; LINE OA manages the relationship with returning ones. Your [marketing strategy](/blog/restaurant-marketing-thailand) should specify which channel handles which customer segment.
</Callout>

### Phone and Walk-In (The Non-System)

Still the default at the majority of Bangkok restaurants. The host answers the phone, writes the booking in a diary, and hopes the guest shows up. Walk-ins are managed by eye -- the host scans the room, estimates availability, and seats or queues guests accordingly.

**When this works:** Restaurants under 30 seats, casual concepts with average checks under 300 THB, and operations where wait times rarely exceed 10 minutes. At this scale, the overhead of a booking system outweighs its benefits.

**When this breaks:** Above 40 seats, when no-shows start costing real money, when peak-hour queues exceed 20 minutes, or when you start tracking table turn times and realize your seating efficiency is 60-70% when it should be 85-90%.

## Comparing Systems: What to Prioritize

Features matter differently depending on your restaurant type. Here is what to weight most heavily based on your operation.

### For Fine Dining (80+ seats, 1,500+ THB average check)

**Priority 1 -- No-show protection.** At your price point, every empty table is a significant revenue loss. You need deposit collection, credit card holds, or a prepayment mechanism. Hungry Hub's prepayment model handles this for package bookings, while dedicated table management systems like TableCheck offer credit card hold functionality.

**Priority 2 -- Guest profiles and CRM.** [Fine dining](/blog/fine-dining-bangkok) lives on repeat guests and personalized service. A system that tracks dietary preferences, seating history, special occasion dates, and spending patterns lets your team deliver recognition that turns first-time visitors into regulars.

**Priority 3 -- Table management and turn optimization.** With high-value tables and longer dining times (90-120 minutes for fine dining), precise table allocation and turn management directly affect your nightly capacity and revenue ceiling.

### For Casual Dining (40-80 seats, 400-800 THB average check)

**Priority 1 -- Walk-in and reservation balance.** Casual dining gets heavier walk-in traffic relative to reservations. Your system needs to allocate tables between pre-booked and walk-in guests without leaving seats empty or creating excessive wait times.

**Priority 2 -- Waitlist management.** When you are full, a digital waitlist with SMS or LINE notifications lets walk-in guests explore the area instead of standing at your door. They come back when their table is ready, and your host is not managing a crowd.

**Priority 3 -- Speed and simplicity.** Your host may be handling 30-50 seating decisions per hour during peak. The system needs to be fast and intuitive -- not a 10-step process to seat a party of two.

### For Group-Focused Restaurants (Set menus, celebrations, private dining)

**Priority 1 -- Package and set-menu management.** Hungry Hub excels here. If group bookings with set menus are a significant share of your revenue, the platform's package model aligns naturally with your operation.

**Priority 2 -- Communication tools.** Group organizers need to coordinate with your team on menus, dietary requirements, seating arrangements, and timing. A system that centralizes this communication prevents the "I sent you a LINE message about the nut allergy but nobody told the kitchen" problem.

**Priority 3 -- Deposit collection.** Group no-shows hurt more than individual no-shows. A 12-person reservation that does not show up is 12 covers lost in a single slot. Deposits or prepayment are non-negotiable for groups above 6.

## The No-Show Problem: What Works in Thailand

No-shows are the single biggest revenue leak that reservation systems can address. But the solutions that work in Europe or the US do not always translate to the Thai market, where cultural norms around cancellation are different.

### Why No-Show Rates Are High in Bangkok

**Last-minute decision culture.** Thai diners often book multiple restaurants and decide on the night which one they actually feel like attending. The others get ghosted. This is changing slowly with younger demographics, but it remains common.

**Low perceived consequence.** Unlike cities where restaurants track and block no-show offenders, most Bangkok restaurants have no memory of who stood them up. Diners know this.

**Easy alternatives.** Bangkok has an extraordinary density of restaurants. If a diner's plans change, there is always somewhere else to eat within walking distance. The opportunity cost of skipping a reservation is near zero for the diner.

**Communication gaps.** When a diner books via phone and later decides not to come, calling to cancel feels like a confrontation. Many diners simply avoid the call. LINE-based booking makes cancellation easier (a quick message), which counterintuitively reduces no-shows by making cancellation less awkward.

### Strategies That Work

**Confirmation messages 24 hours before.** A simple LINE message or SMS asking the guest to confirm reduces no-shows by 20-30% in my experience. The key is making it easy to confirm or cancel -- a one-tap "Confirm" or "Cancel" button, not a request to call back. Many guests genuinely forgot about the booking and will cancel honestly when reminded, freeing the table for someone else.

**Confirmation messages 2-3 hours before.** A second touch-point on the day of the reservation catches same-day cancellations and gives you a 2-3 hour window to fill the table from your waitlist or walk-in flow.

**Deposits for peak periods.** Collecting a deposit of 500-1,000 THB per person for Friday and Saturday dinner reservations reduces no-shows significantly. In Thailand, this needs to be handled carefully:
- **Frame it as a reservation guarantee**, not a penalty. "To secure your table, we ask for a 500 THB deposit per guest that is applied to your bill upon arrival."
- **PromptPay transfer** is the easiest collection method. Send your QR code via LINE when the booking is confirmed.
- **Refund policy should be clear:** full refund for cancellations 24+ hours before, 50% for same-day cancellations, no refund for no-shows. Put this in writing.

**Credit card holds.** Some platforms and dedicated table management systems allow you to hold a credit card at booking. If the guest does not show, you charge a fee. This is less common in Thailand than in Western markets, and some Thai diners resist providing card details for a restaurant booking. Use this selectively -- for high-demand time slots, special events, or [fine dining](/blog/fine-dining-bangkok) where the expectation is established.

**Overbooking calculations.** If your historical no-show rate is 20%, mathematically you can overbook by 15-18% and still have enough tables for everyone who shows up. This is airline logic applied to restaurants, and it works -- until the one night when everyone shows up. Start conservative: if your no-show rate is 20%, overbook by 10%. Track results weekly and adjust.

<Callout type="warning">
Overbooking is a calculated risk, not a permanent strategy. If your overbooking rate results in even one guest being turned away after they honored their reservation, the reputational damage outweighs the revenue gain. Always have a contingency -- a bar seat with a complimentary drink, a nearby partner restaurant where you can redirect, or the ability to set up an extra table in a pinch.
</Callout>

**Waitlist backfill.** When a no-show becomes apparent (15-20 minutes past reservation time), immediately release the table to your waitlist. A digital waitlist with LINE or SMS notification means you can fill the table within minutes. Without a waitlist system, you are relying on a walk-in happening to arrive at exactly the right moment.

**No-show tracking.** Keep a record of guests who no-show. After two no-shows, require a deposit for future bookings. After three, require full prepayment or decline the reservation. This sounds harsh, but repeat no-show offenders cost your restaurant thousands of baht per year each. Platforms like Chope track this data automatically; with LINE OA booking, you need to maintain your own list.

## Managing Walk-Ins Alongside Reservations

In Bangkok, walk-in traffic is not an afterthought -- it is often the majority of your covers. A reservation system that fills every table with bookings and leaves no room for walk-ins is as broken as one that ignores reservations entirely.

### The Allocation Split

The right balance between reserved and walk-in tables depends on your concept and location.

**Fine dining in a destination location:** 70-80% reserved, 20-30% walk-in buffer. Your guests plan ahead, and your revenue per table justifies holding seats for confirmed bookings.

**Casual dining on a busy street:** 40-50% reserved, 50-60% walk-in buffer. Your location generates foot traffic, and walk-in guests are a significant revenue source. Over-reserving means turning away immediate customers for potential no-shows.

**Mid-range in a mall or mixed-use building:** 50-60% reserved, 40-50% walk-in. Mall traffic is unpredictable, and your booking volume may not fill the restaurant consistently.

The exact split should be data-driven. Track your walk-in-to-reservation ratio for 4-6 weeks, note your no-show rate, and calculate the optimal allocation that maximizes total seated covers across both channels.

### Hold Times and Release Policies

**Standard hold:** 15 minutes past reservation time. If the guest has not arrived or communicated, the table releases to the walk-in queue. Communicate this clearly at booking: "We hold your table for 15 minutes after your reserved time."

**Extended hold for VIP or large groups:** 20-30 minutes for parties of 6+ or guests with high lifetime value. These bookings represent more revenue, so the hold cost is justified.

**Late notification:** If a guest messages that they are running late, extend the hold. The issue is not lateness -- it is uncertainty. A guest who communicates is not a no-show risk; a silent absence is.

### Digital Waitlist Management

When tables are full, a digital waitlist turns potential lost customers into queued revenue.

**How it works:** Walk-in guests give their name and phone number. They receive a LINE message or SMS with their queue position and estimated wait time. When their table is ready, they get a notification. They can browse nearby shops, grab a drink at a bar, or wait in the area instead of standing at your door.

**Why it matters:**
- Guests who leave the door but stay in the area have a 70-80% return rate when notified. Guests who are told "about 30 minutes" and left standing at the door have a 40-50% return rate.
- Your host is freed from managing a physical queue and can focus on seating and service.
- The data -- average wait time, queue abandonment rate, peak queue length -- feeds into your staffing and table management decisions.

**Simple implementation:** Even without dedicated software, your host can use a LINE group or simple messaging to notify queued guests. It is manual but functional for restaurants handling 10-15 waitlist entries per night.

## Integrating Reservations with POS and CRM

A reservation system becomes significantly more powerful when it connects to your [POS](/blog/restaurant-pos-systems-thailand) and customer relationship data. The integration turns booking data into business intelligence.

### What Integration Enables

**Spend tracking by guest.** When your POS links a check to a reservation, you know exactly how much each guest spends per visit. Over time, you build a spend profile: Guest A averages 2,800 THB per visit, always orders wine, and comes every 6 weeks. Guest B averages 600 THB and visits once a year. You treat them differently -- not because one is better than the other, but because the service approach and marketing investment should match the guest's pattern.

**Visit frequency tracking.** Your reservation data tells you who comes once and who comes regularly. A guest who visited 3 times in 2 months and then stopped for 4 months is a re-engagement opportunity. A targeted LINE message with a personal touch ("We have not seen you since March -- we just launched a new summer menu we think you would enjoy") costs nothing and can recover a lapsed regular.

**Table preference and seating history.** Some guests prefer specific tables, areas, or seat types. Tracking this and proactively seating them in their preferred spot is a small detail that creates disproportionate loyalty. This is especially valuable in [fine dining](/blog/fine-dining-bangkok), where personal recognition is a core part of the experience.

**Dietary and allergy records.** Recording dietary restrictions and allergies in the guest profile means your kitchen is prepared before the guest asks. "I see we have Mrs. Chen at table 7 tonight -- she is allergic to shellfish, flag that for the kitchen" is the kind of preparation that transforms a good restaurant into a great one.

### How to Implement

**If your POS has built-in reservation features:** FoodStory and some StoreHub configurations include basic reservation and guest tracking. The data lives in one system, which is the simplest approach. The reservation functionality is not as deep as a dedicated platform, but the integration is seamless.

**If you use a separate platform:** Export reservation data weekly and cross-reference with POS sales data. This is manual but doable. Assign a staff member to maintain a guest database (even a Google Sheet) that combines booking history with spend data.

**Dedicated table management software:** Systems like TableCheck, Eat App, or SevenRooms offer deep CRM features alongside reservation management. These are premium solutions (3,000-8,000+ THB/month) aimed at high-end restaurants where guest relationship management justifies the cost.

## Using Reservation Data for Marketing

Your booking data is a marketing asset that most Bangkok restaurants ignore entirely. The information you collect through reservations -- contact details, visit dates, group sizes, special occasions -- enables targeted marketing that generic social media blasts cannot match.

### Birthday and Anniversary Tracking

When a guest books for a birthday or anniversary, record the date. Eleven months later, send a personalized LINE message: "Happy early birthday! We would love to host you again this year -- here is a complimentary dessert on us when you book." The cost is one dessert (50-100 THB). The return is a full table booking, often for a group, at your regular prices.

This only works with a [loyalty system](/blog/restaurant-loyalty-programs-thailand) or CRM that stores the data and triggers the outreach. Build this into your reservation workflow from day one -- it takes 10 seconds to record an occasion date but creates marketing opportunities for years.

### Visit Frequency Segmentation

Divide your guest database into segments based on visit frequency:

- **New guests:** First visit. Focus on collecting their LINE OA contact and ensuring an excellent experience.
- **Returning guests (2-4 visits):** Building a habit. Send targeted promotions for their next visit within 2-3 weeks of their last.
- **Regulars (5+ visits):** Your core revenue base. Offer early access to new menus, special events, or exclusive off-menu dishes. These guests respond to recognition, not discounts.
- **Lapsed guests (no visit in 3+ months):** Re-engagement candidates. A personal message works better than a mass promotion.

### Spending Pattern Analysis

If your POS links to reservation data, you know what each guest spends. Use this to:

- **Identify high-value guests** who deserve VIP treatment (complimentary amuse-bouche, priority seating, birthday recognition)
- **Spot upselling opportunities** -- a guest who always orders the second-cheapest wine might respond to a sommelier recommendation
- **Calculate customer lifetime value** to determine how much you can afford to spend on acquisition and retention
- **Target promotions** based on spend level -- a 20% discount to a guest who spends 4,000 THB per visit has a different ROI than the same offer to a guest who spends 500 THB

## Premium Reservations and Special Occasion Packages

Bangkok's dining scene increasingly supports premium reservation models where guests pay for elevated experiences rather than just a table.

### Chef's Table and Omakase Bookings

If you offer a chef's table or omakase experience, manage these bookings separately from regular reservations. These require:

- **Full prepayment** -- at price points of 3,000-10,000+ THB per person, no-show losses are unacceptable
- **Menu communication** in advance, including allergy and preference collection
- **Limited availability** that creates urgency and justifies the premium
- **Confirmation at 48 hours and 24 hours** before the experience

Hungry Hub is particularly effective for marketing these packages, while direct LINE OA booking with PromptPay prepayment keeps the commission in-house.

### Celebration Packages

Birthday dinners, anniversaries, proposal setups, and corporate events are high-value bookings that justify dedicated handling.

**What to offer:**
- Pre-set menus at fixed price points (3-4 options from mid to premium)
- Room or area decoration (balloons, flowers, signage) at an add-on cost
- Cake and champagne packages
- Custom menu cards with the guest of honor's name
- Photography (even a Polaroid station costs minimal)

**How to manage:**
- Create a dedicated booking form (Google Form linked from your LINE OA rich menu) that captures group size, occasion, dietary requirements, budget, and special requests
- Assign one staff member as the event coordinator for groups above 8
- Require deposits of 30-50% at booking, balance on the night
- Confirm all details 48 hours before via LINE

These packages represent 2-5x the revenue of a standard table booking and, when executed well, generate social media exposure and word-of-mouth referrals that [bring new diners](/blog/restaurant-marketing-thailand) without advertising spend.

## Group Booking Management

Group dining is a regular feature of Bangkok's restaurant scene -- corporate dinners, family celebrations, friend groups, and social gatherings fill restaurants with parties of 6-20+ guests. Managing these bookings well is both a revenue opportunity and an operational challenge.

### The Coordination Challenge

A group of 12 is not the same as 6 tables of 2. It requires:

- **Table configuration** -- can you push tables together, or do you have a private area?
- **Menu coordination** -- is the group ordering a la carte (slow, chaotic) or a set menu (efficient, predictable)?
- **Timing alignment** -- groups arrive in waves. If the booking is for 19:00, expect 8 people at 19:15 and the rest by 19:30.
- **Bill management** -- one check, split checks, or a combination? Clarify before the night, not after dessert.
- **Special requests** -- "Two of us are vegetarian, one is allergic to seafood, and we want a birthday cake at 20:30."

### Group Booking Workflow

**At booking:** Collect group size, contact person, occasion, dietary requirements, preferred seating (private room, outdoor, main dining), and budget range. Ask whether they want a set menu (recommend it for groups above 8 -- it speeds service and improves kitchen efficiency).

**48 hours before:** Confirm final headcount, menu selection, and any special arrangements. The contact person should have confirmed with all guests. Adjust table setup and kitchen prep based on the final number.

**Day of:** Brief your floor team on the group booking -- table assignment, menu, special requests, and timing. A 30-second briefing prevents the confusion of discovering a 15-person group arriving when only the host expected it.

**After the event:** Follow up via LINE with a thank-you message. If the booking was for a celebration, send any photos taken. Ask for a Google review. Group organizers who have a positive experience become repeat group bookers -- they are the person in their social circle who "knows a great restaurant" for every occasion.

<Callout type="tip">
For groups above 10, always recommend a set menu with 2-3 tier options. This is not just about kitchen efficiency -- it improves the group's experience. Large-group a la carte ordering takes 20-30 minutes, creates order errors, and delays food. A set menu gets everyone eating at the same time and lets your kitchen batch production. Frame it as a better experience, not a restriction.
</Callout>

## Reservation Systems for Different Restaurant Types

### Fine Dining

**Recommended setup:** Dedicated table management software (TableCheck, SevenRooms, or Eat App) as your primary system, plus Chope listing for discovery. LINE OA for regular guest bookings.

**Why:** Fine dining needs deep CRM, guest preference tracking, credit card hold capability, and table management sophistication that platform-only solutions do not provide. The monthly cost (3,000-8,000+ THB) is easily justified at 1,500+ THB average check on a 40-80 seat restaurant.

**Non-negotiable features:** Credit card hold or deposit collection, guest profile with allergy and preference records, seating assignment with server section management, automated confirmation at 48 and 24 hours, and [POS integration](/blog/restaurant-pos-systems-thailand) for spend tracking.

### Casual Dining

**Recommended setup:** LINE OA as the primary booking channel, plus Hungry Hub for package deals during slow periods. Google Reserve via Quandoo if you want international visibility.

**Why:** Casual dining does not need deep CRM or credit card holds. What it needs is speed, simplicity, and walk-in management. LINE OA is free and native to how your customers communicate. Hungry Hub fills slow periods with guaranteed prepaid covers.

**Non-negotiable features:** Easy confirmation and reminder messages (LINE OA handles this), waitlist management during peak hours (even manual via host station), and a clear allocation between reserved and walk-in tables.

### Izakaya, Bar-Restaurants, and Late-Night Concepts

**Recommended setup:** LINE OA for bookings, with a clear policy on no reservations after 21:00 (walk-in only for late-night). Minimum-spend requirements for reserved tables during peak hours.

**Why:** These concepts thrive on atmosphere and spontaneity. Over-reserving kills the energy. Reserve specific tables or areas (booths, private rooms) while keeping bar seating and high-tops for walk-ins.

**Key consideration:** For bar-forward concepts, consider a minimum-spend reservation instead of a per-person deposit. "Table for 4 on Friday at 20:00, minimum spend 4,000 THB" ensures revenue without locking guests into a food package.

### Hotels and Resort Restaurants

**Recommended setup:** Integration with the hotel's property management system (PMS) is the priority. Many hotels use Opera PMS, which integrates with platforms like SevenRooms. For in-house guests, the PMS handles booking flow. For external diners, Chope or Quandoo provides a public booking channel.

**Key consideration:** Hotel restaurants need to balance in-house guest priority with external diner revenue. A system that flags in-house guests and gives them priority seating while still accepting external bookings maximizes both occupancy and guest satisfaction.

## The LINE OA Booking Alternative: A Deeper Look

Given that LINE is Thailand's dominant communication platform, it deserves a detailed discussion as a reservation tool.

### Setting Up LINE OA for Reservations

**Step 1: Create a booking-focused rich menu.** Design your LINE OA rich menu with a prominent "Book a Table" button that links to a booking form (Google Form, Typeform, or LINE's built-in form feature).

**Step 2: Build a simple booking form.** Capture: name, phone number, date, time, party size, and occasion (if any). Keep it short -- more than 6 fields and completion rates drop.

**Step 3: Create response templates.** Prepare confirmation, reminder (24-hour and 2-hour), and post-visit thank-you message templates that your staff can send with one tap.

**Step 4: Set up a tracking spreadsheet.** Until you invest in dedicated software, a Google Sheet with columns for date, time, guest name, party size, status (confirmed/canceled/no-show), and notes is sufficient. Your host updates this in real time during service.

### LINE OA Booking Automation

For restaurants handling 20+ bookings per day via LINE, manual management becomes unsustainable. Consider:

**LINE chatbot booking:** Third-party LINE chatbot services (ChatCompose, BotStar, or local Thai providers) can automate the booking conversation -- collecting details, checking availability against your calendar, confirming the booking, and sending reminders. Setup costs 5,000-15,000 THB with ongoing fees of 500-2,000 THB/month.

**LINE x Google Calendar integration:** Use Zapier or Make (formerly Integromat) to automatically create Google Calendar events when a booking form is submitted. This gives you a visual schedule without dedicated software. Setup is free on basic tiers.

<Callout type="info">
LINE OA is best thought of as a communication layer, not a reservation system. It handles the guest interaction brilliantly -- booking, confirmation, reminders, post-visit engagement. But it does not manage table allocation, turn times, or waitlists. Pair LINE OA with a simple table management process (even a paper floor plan with time slots) to cover both sides.
</Callout>

## When You Do Not Need a Reservation System

Not every restaurant benefits from booking software. Here are the signals that a system would add complexity without proportional value.

**Your average wait time is under 10 minutes.** If diners rarely wait, there is no seating problem to solve. A reservation system manages scarcity -- if there is no scarcity, there is no need.

**Your average check is under 300 THB.** At this price point, the commission from a platform like Chope (20-50 THB per cover) represents 7-17% of the check. The math does not work. Use LINE OA for the few bookings you receive and focus your investment elsewhere.

**Your concept is counter-service or fast casual.** No tables to reserve. No turns to manage. A queue management system might help during peak hours, but a reservation platform is irrelevant.

**You are doing fewer than 20 covers per night during peak hours.** At this volume, your host can manage seating by memory and a diary. The operational overhead of a system outweighs the benefit.

**Your location generates heavy foot traffic and most customers are one-time visitors.** Tourist-area restaurants with high walk-in, low-repeat traffic get little value from CRM features. A simple waitlist is more useful than a full reservation system.

## Staff Training on Reservation Management

A reservation system only performs if the staff using it follow consistent procedures. The common failure point is not the technology -- it is the team.

### Host Stand Training

Your host is the most critical person in the reservation workflow. Train them on:

**System operation:** Every function they will use during service -- checking bookings, seating walk-ins, managing the waitlist, recording no-shows, and releasing held tables. Practice during a mock service before going live.

**Decision framework for peak hours:** When all reserved tables are full and walk-ins are queuing, the host needs a clear priority system. Who gets seated first -- the next reservation arriving in 10 minutes or the walk-in party of 4 that has been waiting 20 minutes? Define the rules so the host is not making stressful judgment calls during a Friday rush.

**Phone and LINE booking protocol:** How to take a reservation, what information to collect, what confirmation message to send, and how to handle special requests. Create a script and laminate it at the host stand.

**No-show and late arrival protocol:** When to call or message a late reservation, when to release the table, and how to log the no-show. Do not leave this to individual judgment -- "We always call at 10 minutes, release at 15 minutes unless the guest has communicated."

### Floor Team Awareness

Your servers need to know:

- Which tables are reserved for the next turn and when those guests are expected
- The current waitlist status, so they can manage guest expectations at their tables (gently encouraging dessert decisions when the next group is waiting)
- Any special requirements for upcoming reservations (birthday setup, dietary restrictions, VIP flags)

A 2-minute pre-shift briefing covering tonight's reservations, special events, and VIP guests costs nothing and prevents miscommunication during service.

## Measuring Reservation System ROI

You are paying for this system -- either through platform commissions, software subscriptions, or staff time. Make sure the investment returns.

### Key Metrics to Track

**No-show rate (before and after).** This is the primary metric. If your system reduces no-shows from 20% to 8%, calculate the revenue difference. On a 50-seat restaurant doing 1,200 THB average check with 80% reservation rate, dropping no-shows from 20% to 8% recovers roughly 57,600 THB per month in recaptured revenue (40 reserved seats x 12% improvement x 1,200 THB).

**Covers per service.** Are you seating more guests per night with the system than without it? Better table management and waitlist efficiency should increase total covers by 5-15%.

**Table turn time.** How long between one party leaving and the next being seated? A good system reduces this gap from 10-15 minutes (host guessing and scanning) to 3-5 minutes (system alerts host that a table is cleared and next reservation is waiting).

**[Staff](/blog/restaurant-staffing-thailand) time on booking management.** How many minutes per day does your team spend on reservation-related tasks (taking bookings, confirming, managing the floor plan)? A system should reduce this, freeing your host to focus on guest experience rather than logistics.

**Platform acquisition cost.** If you use Chope or Hungry Hub, calculate the cost per new customer acquired. If Chope charges 35 THB per cover and 40% of your Chope bookings are first-time guests, your acquisition cost is 87.50 THB per new customer (35 THB / 0.40). Compare this to what you spend on other [marketing channels](/blog/restaurant-marketing-thailand) per new customer.

**Repeat booking rate.** What percentage of guests who book once return within 90 days? A system with CRM and follow-up capability should increase this rate over time. Track it monthly.

<Callout type="tip">
Run the numbers quarterly. A reservation system that costs 3,000 THB/month in software plus 15,000 THB/month in Chope commissions needs to generate at least 18,000 THB/month in recovered no-show revenue, incremental covers, or operational savings to break even. In my experience, the break-even point is reached within the first month for restaurants with pre-system no-show rates above 15%.
</Callout>

## My Recommendations

**If you are a fine dining restaurant losing money to no-shows:** Invest in a dedicated table management system with credit card hold capability. The software cost (3,000-8,000 THB/month) pays for itself within 1-2 weeks of reduced no-shows at your price point. List on Chope for discovery but manage your core bookings in-house.

**If you are a casual dining restaurant that wants to manage peak hours better:** Start with LINE OA booking and a Google Sheet tracking system. It is free and covers 80% of what you need. Add Hungry Hub for slow-period packages and a digital waitlist (even manual via LINE messages) for peak-hour walk-in management.

**If you are a new restaurant building awareness:** List on Chope and Hungry Hub for the discovery benefit, accepting the commission as a marketing cost. As your direct booking volume grows through LINE OA and repeat guests, reduce platform dependence.

**If you run a bar or late-night concept:** LINE OA for reservations with a minimum-spend policy for premium tables during peak hours. No platform needed unless you specifically want the marketing exposure.

**If you are a group-focused or event-heavy restaurant:** Hungry Hub is your best platform fit. Supplement with direct LINE OA booking for corporate and repeat group clients where you want to avoid commission.

The reservation system you choose is less important than the discipline of using it consistently. A paper diary with a rigorous confirmation process will outperform a 5,000 THB/month software system that nobody maintains. Start with the simplest tool that addresses your biggest problem -- usually no-shows -- and add sophistication as your operation demands it.

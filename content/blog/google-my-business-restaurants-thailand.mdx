A client in Sukhumvit called me last year about a problem she could not explain. Her restaurant had been open eight months, food was solid, Wongnai rating sat at 4.3, and Instagram was pulling in new customers every week. But she kept hearing the same thing from walk-ins: "We almost went somewhere else because we couldn't find you on Google Maps."

I checked her Google Business Profile. The map pin was placed 200 meters from her actual entrance, on the wrong side of the soi. Her listed hours showed "Closed" on Mondays, even though she had been open seven days a week since month two. There were zero photos uploaded by the business -- just three blurry customer shots of pad thai under fluorescent lighting. And her business category was set to "Thai Massage."

Thai Massage.

That one profile was costing her an estimated 15-20 walk-ins per week, based on the foot traffic patterns we measured after fixing it. At an average check of 650 THB, that is 40,000-52,000 THB in lost monthly revenue from a free tool that takes about two hours to set up properly.

Google My Business (now officially called Google Business Profile, though everyone still says GMB) is the most neglected piece of restaurant marketing infrastructure in Thailand. Most owners either skip it entirely, set it up once and never touch it again, or make errors that actively drive customers to their competitors. This guide covers the complete process of getting it right -- from verification to ongoing optimization -- with specific advice for the Thai market.

## Why Google Business Profile Matters for Thailand Restaurants

Here is a question I hear from Bangkok restaurant owners more than I should: "Thais use Wongnai, not Google. Why should I bother?"

They are half right. Thai diners do prefer Wongnai for restaurant discovery and reviews, and that platform deserves serious attention (I cover it in my [complete guide to restaurant marketing in Thailand](/blog/restaurant-marketing-thailand)). But Google serves a different function in Thailand's dining ecosystem, and ignoring it means ignoring two massive customer segments.

### The Tourist Search Pipeline

Thailand welcomed over 35 million international tourists in 2025, with Bangkok consistently ranking as one of the world's most-visited cities. These tourists do not use Wongnai. They use Google Maps.

When a tourist in Silom opens their phone and types "best Thai restaurant near me," Google's local pack -- that box of three businesses with map pins that appears at the top of search results -- determines where they eat. If your restaurant is not appearing in that local pack, you do not exist for the tourist market.

The math is straightforward. A restaurant in a tourist-heavy area like Sukhumvit, Silom, Sathorn, or Old Town can attribute 20-40% of its covers to tourist walk-ins. For a 60-seat restaurant doing two turns on a busy night, that is 24-48 covers driven by people who found you on Google Maps. Lose that traffic, and you are leaving 600,000-1,500,000 THB per month on the table.

### The Expat and Corporate Market

Bangkok has roughly 300,000 registered expats, and the real number including unregistered long-stay visitors is considerably higher. This demographic skews heavily toward Google over Wongnai -- English-speaking expats search Google in English, read Google Reviews in English, and make dining decisions using Google Maps navigation.

Corporate lunch and dinner bookings follow a similar pattern. When an EA at a multinational company searches "restaurant for business dinner Sathorn," Google is the first stop.

### Thai Users Are Shifting

Here is the part most owners miss: Thai Google usage for restaurant searches is growing. Google Maps is pre-installed on every Android phone (which dominates the Thai market at 60%+ market share), and younger Thai diners increasingly use Google Maps for navigation even when they discover a restaurant on Instagram or Wongnai. A 2025 ETDA digital behavior report showed that 41% of Thai smartphone users aged 18-35 used Google Maps to search for restaurants at least once a month, up from 28% two years prior.

Wongnai is still dominant for Thai restaurant discovery. But Google is where people navigate, check hours, call to book, and read reviews when they are already nearby and hungry. Those are high-intent actions -- the customer is not browsing, they are deciding. If your profile is incomplete or inaccurate at that moment, you lose them.

## Setting Up Your Google Business Profile in Thailand

### Step 1: Claim or Create Your Listing

Go to [business.google.com](https://business.google.com) and search for your restaurant name. There are three scenarios:

**Your business already exists on Google Maps.** This is common -- Google auto-generates business listings from public data sources, customer contributions, and web crawling. If your restaurant appears, click "Claim this business" and follow the verification process.

**A previous owner or tenant has a listing at your address.** You will need to either request ownership transfer (if you can contact them) or report the listing to Google and create a new one. This can take 2-4 weeks to resolve.

**No listing exists.** Click "Add your business" and fill in the required information: business name, category, address, phone number, and website.

<Callout type="warning">
Use your restaurant's exact legal operating name. Do not stuff keywords into your business name (e.g., "Siam Kitchen - Best Thai Food Bangkok Sukhumvit"). Google penalizes keyword-stuffed names and may suspend your listing. Your business name should match what is on your signage and business registration.
</Callout>

### Step 2: Verification

Google needs to confirm that you are the actual owner of this business at this location. In Thailand, the verification methods available are:

**Phone verification** is the fastest method, usually available for most restaurants. Google calls or texts your listed phone number with a 6-digit code. Takes about 5 minutes.

**Postcard verification** is sometimes required for new businesses or addresses that Google cannot verify digitally. Google mails a postcard to your business address with a verification code. In Thailand, this takes 7-14 business days, sometimes longer. The postcard goes to your registered business address -- make sure your staff knows to look for it and not throw it away.

**Video verification** is increasingly common for new businesses in Thailand. You record a short video showing your restaurant's signage, interior, and surrounding street to confirm you are physically present at the claimed address. Upload it through the Google Business Profile dashboard.

**Email verification** is available for businesses that already have an established web presence with a matching domain.

My recommendation: try phone verification first. If it is not available, video verification is typically faster than waiting for a postcard. I have had clients wait three weeks for a postcard that never arrived because it was delivered to the building's management office and discarded.

### Step 3: Complete Your Profile

Once verified, fill in every field Google offers. Completeness directly affects your local search ranking. Google's own documentation states that businesses with complete profiles are 2.7 times more likely to be considered reputable and 70% more likely to attract location visits.

**Essential information:**

- **Business name** -- exact match to your signage
- **Primary category** -- "Thai Restaurant," "Japanese Restaurant," "Italian Restaurant," etc. (more on category strategy below)
- **Address** -- exact, including floor number if in a mall or building
- **Service area** -- leave blank for dine-in restaurants, set a radius for delivery-only
- **Hours** -- precise hours for every day, including holiday hours
- **Phone number** -- the number your staff actually answers
- **Website** -- your website or Instagram if no website
- **Menu link** -- direct link to your menu (PDF or webpage)
- **Reservation link** -- link to your booking system or LINE Official Account
- **Order link** -- link to your Grab Food or LINE MAN page

**Attributes to enable:**

Google offers dozens of restaurant-specific attributes. Enable everything that applies:
- Dine-in / Takeout / Delivery
- Outdoor seating
- Wi-Fi
- Parking
- Wheelchair accessible
- Accepts credit cards / accepts mobile payments
- Halal / Vegetarian options / Vegan options
- Good for groups / Good for kids
- Live music / Happy hour
- Serves alcohol / Serves cocktails / Beer / Wine

These attributes appear in your listing and are used as filters when customers search. A customer searching "restaurant with outdoor seating near me" will only see restaurants that have enabled the outdoor seating attribute.

## Category Strategy: Getting This Right

Your primary category is the strongest ranking signal for your Google Business Profile. Get it wrong, and you are invisible for the searches that matter.

### Primary Category

Choose the most specific category available that accurately describes your restaurant. Google offers hundreds of restaurant subcategories. Some relevant ones for Thailand:

- Thai Restaurant
- Japanese Restaurant
- Italian Restaurant
- Seafood Restaurant
- Vegetarian Restaurant
- Sushi Restaurant
- Ramen Restaurant
- Pizza Restaurant
- Steakhouse
- Cafe
- Bar & Grill
- Fine Dining Restaurant
- Buffet Restaurant

**Do not choose "Restaurant" as your primary category.** This is the generic parent category and it is far too broad. A "Thai Restaurant" categorization will rank higher for "Thai food near me" than a generic "Restaurant" categorization.

### Secondary Categories

You can add up to 9 secondary categories, and you should use them. A modern Thai restaurant that also has a bar and serves brunch could set:

- **Primary:** Thai Restaurant
- **Secondary:** Bar, Brunch Restaurant, Asian Restaurant

A Japanese restaurant with a sushi counter and ramen:

- **Primary:** Japanese Restaurant
- **Secondary:** Sushi Restaurant, Ramen Restaurant

Each additional category makes you eligible to appear in more search queries. But only add categories that genuinely apply -- Google's review team can remove listings that use misleading categories.

<Callout type="tip">
Check what categories your top-performing competitors use. Search for your target keyword on Google Maps, find the top 3 restaurants in the local pack, and look at their profiles. You can see their primary category displayed below their business name. If they are using a category you missed, add it.
</Callout>

## Optimizing for Google Maps and Local Pack Ranking

The "local pack" is the box of three Google Maps results that appears above organic search results when someone searches for a restaurant near a specific location. Appearing in this box is worth more than any organic ranking below it -- studies show the local pack captures 44% of all clicks on local search results pages.

Google uses three primary factors to determine local pack rankings:

### 1. Relevance

How well does your profile match what the searcher is looking for? This is where your categories, business description, services, and attributes matter. A complete profile with accurate categories will rank higher for relevant queries than a sparse profile.

**Your business description** should be 750 characters (Google's limit) and naturally include the terms your customers search for. Do not keyword-stuff, but do mention your cuisine type, neighborhood, signature dishes, and dining experience.

Good description: "Modern Thai restaurant in Thonglor serving creative takes on traditional Thai dishes. Our menu features sustainable seafood, organic produce from Chiang Mai, and a curated list of natural wines. Private dining room available for groups of 8-20. Open for lunch and dinner daily."

Bad description: "Best Thai food Bangkok Thonglor restaurant near BTS top rated five star amazing food must try best restaurant Thailand."

### 2. Distance

How close is your restaurant to the searcher's location? You cannot change this, which is why optimizing the other two factors is so important -- they are the levers you can pull.

One thing you can control: make sure your map pin is in the exact right location. I have audited dozens of restaurant profiles in Bangkok where the pin was placed on the wrong soi, on the wrong side of the street, or in the middle of a building's parking structure. Walk outside your restaurant, open Google Maps, and verify that the pin matches your actual entrance. If it is off, you can request a pin correction through your Google Business Profile dashboard.

### 3. Prominence

How well-known and well-reviewed is your business? This factor includes:

- **Review count and rating** -- more reviews with higher ratings boost your ranking
- **Review velocity** -- steady new reviews signal an active, relevant business
- **Web presence** -- mentions of your restaurant on other websites (food blogs, review sites, news articles, directory listings)
- **Google Business Profile activity** -- regular posts, photo uploads, and Q&A responses signal to Google that you are an active business

Of these, reviews are the most impactful, and I will cover review strategy in detail below.

## Managing Reviews: Thai and English

Reviews are the engine of your Google Business Profile. A restaurant with 200 reviews averaging 4.5 stars will rank higher and convert more profile visitors into actual diners than a restaurant with 15 reviews averaging 4.8 stars. Volume and quality both matter, and in Thailand, you need to manage reviews in two languages.

### Getting More Reviews

**Ask at the right moment.** The best time to ask for a Google review is when a customer has just expressed satisfaction -- after a compliment to the server, after taking a photo of the food, or after a birthday celebration goes well. Train your staff to read these moments and say: "We are glad you enjoyed it. If you have a moment, a Google review would mean a lot to us."

**Make it frictionless.** Generate a short review link from your Google Business Profile dashboard (Settings > Get more reviews). Print this link as a QR code and place it on table tents, on the bill folder, and near the exit. The fewer taps between "I should leave a review" and actually submitting one, the more reviews you get.

**Follow up via LINE.** If you have a LINE Official Account (and you should -- it is the best retention channel in Thailand), send a gentle follow-up message to recent diners: "Thank you for visiting! We would love your feedback on Google: [review link]." Time this 2-4 hours after their visit.

**Target the right customers.** Tourists are more likely to leave Google reviews than Thai diners, who default to Wongnai. Foreigners are already in the Google ecosystem and often leave reviews unprompted if the experience was notable. Place English-language review prompts (QR codes, table tents) visibly in areas where tourists tend to sit.

### Responding to Reviews

Respond to every review within 48 hours. Every single one -- positive and negative, Thai and English. This is non-negotiable for three reasons: Google's algorithm favors businesses that actively engage with reviews, potential customers read your responses before deciding to visit, and it signals that you care about the dining experience beyond the transaction.

**Responding to positive reviews:**

- Thank the reviewer by name
- Reference something specific from their review ("glad you enjoyed the massaman curry")
- Invite them back with a specific reason ("our new seasonal menu launches next week -- hope to see you again")
- Keep it 2-3 sentences, warm but not over-the-top

**Responding to negative reviews:**

- Acknowledge the issue without being defensive
- Apologize for the specific experience, not in vague generalities
- Explain what you have done or will do to address it
- Offer to continue the conversation offline ("please contact us at [phone/LINE] so we can make this right")
- Never argue, never blame the customer, never make excuses

**Language strategy:** Respond in the language the review was written in. If a review is in Thai, respond in Thai. If in English, respond in English. If a review is in Chinese, Japanese, or Korean (common in tourist areas), respond in English with a note that you appreciate their visit. This bilingual approach shows both Thai and international customers that you are paying attention.

<Callout type="warning">
Never offer compensation publicly in your review response ("come back for a free meal"). This invites other customers to leave negative reviews hoping for free food. Handle compensation privately via direct message, phone, or LINE.
</Callout>

### Handling Fake or Unfair Reviews

Fake reviews happen in Thailand, sometimes from competitors, sometimes from customers who never visited, sometimes from people confusing your restaurant with another. Google allows you to flag reviews that violate their policies (spam, fake, off-topic, conflict of interest). The process:

1. Open the review in your Google Business Profile dashboard
2. Click the three-dot menu and select "Flag as inappropriate"
3. Choose the applicable violation type
4. Submit and wait -- Google typically reviews flags within 7-14 days

Success rates vary. If the review is clearly fake (reviewer has no other reviews, mentions services you do not offer, or you can prove they never visited), Google usually removes it. If it is simply an unfair review from a real customer, Google almost never removes it. In that case, your best response is a professional, empathetic reply that shows future readers you handle complaints gracefully.

## Google Business Profile Posts: The Free Marketing Tool Nobody Uses

Google Business Profile allows you to publish posts that appear directly on your listing in Google Search and Maps. These posts are free, take 5 minutes to create, and roughly 90% of restaurants in Bangkok do not use them. That is an opportunity.

### Types of Posts

**Update posts** are general announcements -- new menu items, chef spotlights, behind-the-scenes stories, or news about your restaurant. These appear in the "Updates" tab of your profile.

**Offer posts** have a specific promotion with start and end dates. "20% off all cocktails, Monday-Wednesday, March 1-31." These appear with a highlighted "View offer" button that stands out.

**Event posts** have a title, date/time, and description. "Wine Dinner with Villa Maria -- March 15, 7:00 PM. 5-course pairing menu, 2,800 THB per person." These appear in the "Events" section.

### Posting Strategy

Post once or twice a week. Each post stays visible for 7 days (except Event and Offer posts, which stay until their end date), so weekly posting keeps your profile active and fresh.

**What to post:**
- New dishes or menu changes, with a high-quality photo
- Weekly or monthly promotions
- Upcoming events (holiday specials, live music, themed dinners)
- Awards, press coverage, or Michelin mentions
- Seasonal updates ("Our new terrace is open for the cool season")

**Photo quality matters.** Posts with high-quality food photos get 35% more clicks than text-only posts, according to Google's own data. Use the same professional photos you use for Instagram -- repurpose content across channels.

**Include a call to action.** Every post should have a CTA button: "Call now," "Learn more," "Order online," "Book," or "Get offer." Link these to your phone number, website, LINE, or delivery platform.

## Photo Optimization

Photos are the second most-viewed element of a Google Business Profile after reviews. Businesses with 100+ photos get 520% more calls and 2,717% more direction requests than businesses with fewer than 10 photos, according to Google's published data. Those numbers are dramatic, but the principle holds: more quality photos mean more customer engagement.

### What to Upload

Aim for a minimum of 30 photos across these categories:

**Exterior (3-5 photos):** Your storefront during the day and at night, your signage, the entrance, and the street view showing nearby landmarks or BTS/MRT stations. These help customers recognize your restaurant when they arrive -- a bigger deal than you might think in Bangkok's maze of sois and unmarked shophouses.

**Interior (5-8 photos):** The dining room from multiple angles, the bar area, private dining rooms, the patio or terrace, and any distinctive design elements (feature walls, lighting, art). Shoot during golden hour or with your restaurant's evening lighting at its best.

**Food (15-20 photos):** Your hero dishes, signature cocktails, desserts, and any visually striking presentations. Use the same professional photos from your [Instagram content strategy](/blog/instagram-marketing-bangkok-restaurants). Overhead shots of communal Thai dining spreads perform particularly well.

**Team (3-5 photos):** Your chef in the kitchen, your service team, and any candid moments of staff interacting with guests. These humanize your business.

**Menu (2-3 photos):** Clear, readable photos of your physical menu. Customers check these to see prices and options before visiting.

### Photo Technical Requirements

- Minimum resolution: 720 x 720 pixels (higher is better)
- Format: JPG or PNG
- File size: 10 KB to 5 MB
- No heavy filters or text overlays -- Google may reject edited photos
- Geotagging helps: if your phone tags photos with location data, leave it on

### Managing Customer Photos

Customers upload their own photos to your listing, and you cannot remove them (unless they violate Google's policies). But you can influence the quality of customer photos through your restaurant's design and lighting.

Restaurants that invest in good lighting above tables and Instagram-worthy plating generate better customer photos on Google, too. The customer who photographs your dish for Instagram will often upload that same photo as a Google review image. Your design investment pays dividends across every platform.

<Callout type="tip">
Upload new photos at least monthly. Google's algorithm favors businesses with recent photo activity. Set a recurring calendar reminder to upload 3-5 new photos on the first Monday of each month. Seasonal refreshes (holiday decorations, new menu items, staff photos) keep your profile looking current and active.
</Callout>

## Q&A Section Management

Google Business Profile has a Q&A section where anyone can ask questions and anyone can answer them -- including other customers. If you are not monitoring this section, random strangers might be answering questions about your restaurant incorrectly.

### Proactive Q&A Strategy

Seed your Q&A section with the 10-15 most common questions customers ask, and answer them yourself. This prevents misinformation and gives you another place to include helpful information.

Common questions to seed:

- "Do you take reservations?" -- answer with your booking method (LINE, phone, walk-in)
- "Is there parking?" -- specific parking instructions, including nearby garages
- "Do you have vegetarian/halal/gluten-free options?" -- direct answer with examples
- "Is there outdoor seating?" -- describe your outdoor area
- "What is the price range?" -- give a typical per-person range
- "Do you deliver?" -- link to your Grab Food or LINE MAN page
- "Is there a dress code?" -- clarify expectations
- "Do you cater for large groups or private events?" -- describe your group dining options and capacity
- "How do I get there from BTS [nearest station]?" -- walking directions from the nearest transit stop
- "Do you accept credit cards?" -- list accepted payment methods including PromptPay and mobile wallets

### Monitoring

Check your Q&A section weekly. When a new question appears, answer it within 24 hours. If someone else has answered incorrectly, you cannot delete their answer, but you can add your own official answer and upvote it to push it above the incorrect one.

## Tracking Insights and Analytics

Google Business Profile provides free analytics (called "Insights") that show you how customers find and interact with your listing. Check these monthly to understand what is working and where to focus.

### Key Metrics

**Search queries.** What terms are people using to find your restaurant? This tells you what your customers actually search for, which is often different from what you assume. If you see unexpected queries performing well ("quiet restaurant Thonglor" or "birthday dinner Bangkok"), you can create content and posts targeting those terms.

**How customers find you.** Google breaks this into "Direct" (searched your business name) and "Discovery" (searched a category, product, or service and found you). A high Discovery percentage means your profile is effectively reaching new customers, while a high Direct percentage means your brand awareness is strong.

**Customer actions.** How many people clicked for directions, called your phone number, visited your website, or clicked your menu link? These are bottom-of-funnel actions that represent high-intent potential customers. Track these monthly and correlate with any changes you make to your profile.

**Photo views.** How many times were your photos viewed compared to businesses similar to yours? If your photo views are below the benchmark, you need more and better photos.

**Post performance.** Each Google post shows views and clicks. Track which post types (updates, offers, events) drive the most engagement, and adjust your posting strategy accordingly.

## Common Google Business Profile Mistakes

After auditing over 100 restaurant profiles in Bangkok, these are the errors I see most frequently.

**Wrong primary category.** I have seen Thai restaurants categorized as "Catering," "Event venue," "Thai massage" (yes, more than once), and simply "Restaurant." Each of these costs you visibility in the searches that matter. Check your primary category today.

**Incomplete information.** A profile missing hours, phone number, menu link, or website looks abandoned to both Google's algorithm and potential customers. Fill in every available field. If you do not have a website, link to your Instagram page or Facebook.

**Stale hours.** Your hours change for a holiday, you forget to update Google, and customers show up to a locked door. Set a calendar reminder to update hours before every Thai public holiday. Google also allows you to set "Special hours" for specific dates without changing your regular schedule.

**Duplicate listings.** This happens when a restaurant moves locations, changes names, or when Google auto-generates a second listing from different data sources. Duplicate listings split your reviews, confuse customers, and hurt your ranking. Search for your restaurant name on Google Maps regularly to check for duplicates. If you find one, report it for removal through the Google Business Profile dashboard.

**Ignoring the Q&A section.** If you are not answering questions, other people are -- and they are often wrong. "Do they have parking?" answered by a customer who visited once and guessed "No" when you actually have 10 spots behind the building. Monitor and manage this section.

**Never posting.** A Google Business Profile with no posts looks dormant. Weekly posts signal to Google and customers that your restaurant is active and engaged, and each one takes 5 minutes.

**Not responding to reviews.** I have seen restaurants with 200+ Google reviews and zero owner responses. That is not a good look. Respond to every review, positive and negative.

## GMB for Multiple Locations

If you operate more than one restaurant location, each needs its own Google Business Profile. Do not try to list multiple addresses under one profile -- this violates Google's guidelines and can result in all your listings being suspended.

### Multi-Location Management

**Use the same Google account** to manage all locations. This gives you a single dashboard view of all your restaurants' performance.

**Differentiate each listing.** Even if the concept is the same, each location should have unique photos (showing that specific location's interior, exterior, and team), a unique description mentioning the neighborhood and specific features, and independently managed reviews.

**Consistent branding, localized content.** Your business name should be consistent across locations (e.g., "Siam Kitchen - Thonglor" and "Siam Kitchen - Sathorn"), while photos, descriptions, and posts should be specific to each location.

**Location groups.** Google Business Profile offers a "Business Group" feature that lets you manage multiple locations more efficiently, with shared user access controls and bulk editing capabilities. Set this up once you have three or more locations.

## Google Ads Local Campaigns

Google Ads offers local campaign types that integrate directly with your Google Business Profile to promote your restaurant across Google Search, Maps, YouTube, and the Google Display Network. This is paid advertising, not organic optimization -- but it is worth discussing because it can accelerate your local visibility significantly.

### How Local Campaigns Work

You set a budget, define your target geographic area (typically 3-10 km around your restaurant), and Google's machine learning automatically distributes your ads across its network. Ads appear as promoted pins on Google Maps, sponsored search results, and display banners on websites your potential customers browse.

### Budget and Performance

For a single restaurant location, 5,000-30,000 THB per month is a reasonable local campaign budget. Google's minimum daily budget for local campaigns is approximately 500 THB. Performance benchmarks in Bangkok:

- Cost per store visit: 15-50 THB (Google tracks foot traffic from ad exposure to in-store visits using location data)
- Cost per call: 20-80 THB
- Cost per direction request: 10-40 THB

These numbers vary significantly by neighborhood, competition, and restaurant category. Tourist-heavy areas like Sukhumvit and Silom tend to have higher costs per action due to more competition, while emerging neighborhoods like Ari, Charoen Krung, and Bang Rak are less competitive and cheaper.

<Callout type="info">
Google Ads local campaigns work best as a supplement to a well-optimized organic profile, not as a substitute. A restaurant with 5 reviews and no photos running ads will generate clicks but poor conversion -- customers click through, see a bare profile, and leave. Invest in organic profile quality first, then consider paid campaigns to amplify your reach.
</Callout>

### When to Use Local Campaigns

- **Grand opening:** Drive awareness in your first 30 days with a 15,000-30,000 THB campaign targeting 5 km around your location
- **Seasonal pushes:** Valentine's Day, Christmas, Chinese New Year -- run event-specific campaigns 2 weeks before the occasion
- **Slow periods:** During rainy season (June-October) when foot traffic drops, targeted ads can maintain a baseline of discovery-driven visits
- **New menu launch:** Combine with Google posts about the new menu for maximum impact

## Wongnai vs. Google: Understanding the Thai Market

The relationship between Wongnai and Google in Thailand's restaurant market is not either/or -- they serve different roles in the customer journey, and you need both.

**Wongnai dominates Thai restaurant discovery.** With over 10 million monthly active users, Wongnai is where Thai diners go to discover new restaurants, browse menus, read detailed reviews in Thai, and compare options. A restaurant's Wongnai rating carries more weight with Thai consumers than its Google rating. If your Wongnai rating is below 4.0, Thai customers will hesitate regardless of your Google score.

**Google dominates navigation, logistics, and tourist discovery.** When a Thai customer decides to visit a restaurant they found on Wongnai or Instagram, they open Google Maps for directions, hours, and contact information. And for tourists, Google is the entire pipeline -- discovery, evaluation, navigation, and review.

**The practical approach:** Invest more time and energy in Wongnai for Thai customer acquisition, while maintaining a fully optimized Google Business Profile for navigation, tourist traffic, and the growing segment of Thai users who search Google for dining options. Do not neglect either platform.

## Tourist vs. Local Search Behavior Differences

Understanding how tourists and locals search differently helps you optimize your profile for both audiences.

### Tourist Search Patterns

Tourists search in English, use generic category terms ("Thai restaurant," "rooftop bar," "best pad thai"), and heavily filter by proximity ("near me," near their hotel, near tourist landmarks). They are more likely to:

- Read Google Reviews (not Wongnai)
- Look at photos before visiting
- Click for directions (unfamiliar with the area)
- Search during mealtime hours (high-intent, deciding where to eat right now)
- Leave detailed Google reviews after visiting

**Optimization for tourists:** Write your business description in English, upload photos with English captions in the filename (Google reads file metadata), respond to English reviews in English, and include neighborhood landmarks in your description ("5 minutes walk from BTS Saphan Taksin" is more useful to a tourist than a street address).

### Local Search Patterns

Thai locals search in Thai, use more specific terms (cuisine names, neighborhood names, specific dishes), and often search to confirm logistics rather than discover. They are more likely to:

- Check hours and contact info on Google after discovering the restaurant elsewhere
- Call directly from the Google listing
- Use Google Maps for navigation
- Leave Wongnai reviews instead of Google reviews

**Optimization for locals:** Include a Thai-language business description (Google allows multiple language descriptions in some markets; otherwise, include key Thai terms in your English description), ensure your Thai phone number is correct and someone answers it, and make sure your Google Maps pin is perfectly accurate -- Thai drivers navigating via Google Maps to a wrong pin in Bangkok traffic will not circle back.

<Callout type="tip">
Check your Google Insights to see the split between Direct and Discovery searches. If you are getting mostly Direct searches (people searching your name), your brand awareness is working but your profile is not reaching new customers. Increase Discovery traffic by adding more secondary categories, posting regularly, and building your review count.
</Callout>

## Integration with Your Broader Marketing Strategy

Your Google Business Profile should not exist in isolation. It is one node in a connected marketing ecosystem, and it works best when integrated with your other channels.

**Instagram to Google pipeline.** Customers discover you on Instagram, then search your name on Google to check hours, location, and reviews before visiting. Make sure the information matches across both platforms -- name, hours, menu, and contact details. For the complete Instagram playbook, see: [Instagram Marketing for Bangkok Restaurants](/blog/instagram-marketing-bangkok-restaurants).

**Wongnai cross-pollination.** A customer who leaves a Wongnai review might also leave a Google review if prompted. Include both review platform links on your table tents and bill inserts.

**LINE integration.** Include your LINE Official Account link as the "Booking" or "Message" link on your Google profile. This drives Google profile visitors into your LINE ecosystem where you can nurture them into repeat customers.

**Website SEO.** If you have a website, embedding a Google Map on your contact page, using consistent NAP (Name, Address, Phone) information, and adding LocalBusiness schema markup all reinforce your Google Business Profile's authority. If you are planning your location strategy, our guide on [how to find a restaurant location in Bangkok](/blog/how-to-find-restaurant-location-bangkok) covers how foot traffic and search visibility interact.

**Delivery platforms.** Link your Grab Food and LINE MAN pages from your Google profile's "Order" button. Customers who find you on Google but prefer delivery can convert immediately.

## A Monthly GMB Maintenance Checklist

Once your profile is set up and optimized, ongoing maintenance takes about 30-45 minutes per week. Here is a monthly routine:

**Weekly (15 minutes each):**
- Respond to all new reviews
- Check Q&A for new questions
- Publish 1-2 Google posts (promotion, update, or event)
- Upload 2-3 new photos

**Monthly (30 minutes once):**
- Review Insights data and track trends
- Check for duplicate listings
- Verify hours are accurate (especially around upcoming holidays)
- Audit customer photos for any problematic images to flag
- Refresh your business description if anything has changed

**Quarterly (1 hour once):**
- Professional food photography refresh
- Update menu link with any changes
- Review category strategy against competitors
- Analyze which Google posts performed best and adjust strategy

This is not a heavy lift. The problem is not time -- it is that most restaurant owners never build the habit. Set a recurring calendar reminder, assign it to a specific team member, and make it part of your weekly operations meeting.

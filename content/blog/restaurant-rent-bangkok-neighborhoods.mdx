A prospective client walked into our office last month with a lease offer for a 120 sqm shophouse on Thonglor Soi 13. The asking rent was 2,200 THB per square meter per month -- 264,000 THB total -- with 3 months deposit and 4 months key money. She wanted to open a casual Thai bistro with an average check of 400 THB per person.

I ran the numbers with her. To keep rent at 10% of revenue, she would need to generate 2,640,000 THB per month. At a 400 THB average check, that requires 6,600 covers per month, or 220 covers per day in a 120 sqm space with maybe 40-45 seats. Even with excellent table turnover (4 turns per day), she would need to fill every seat four times, every single day, with zero slow periods.

It was not going to work. Not because her concept was wrong, but because the rent was wrong for the concept. We found her a space on Ari Soi 1 at 900 THB per square meter -- roughly the same size, a strong residential walk-in customer base, and a rent-to-revenue ratio that left room for profit. She opened five months later and broke even in month three.

This is why rent data matters. Not as an abstract data point, but as the number that determines whether your restaurant's financial model is viable before you even open the doors. This article gives you current rent benchmarks for every major restaurant neighborhood in Bangkok, along with the foot traffic profiles, customer demographics, and key money expectations you need to evaluate any space intelligently.

<Callout type="info">
All rent figures in this article are monthly rates per square meter in Thai Baht, based on actual lease data from restaurant clients and real estate contacts as of Q1 2026. Rates fluctuate based on floor level (ground floor commands a 20-40% premium over upper floors), street frontage, building condition, and negotiation. Use these as benchmarks, not absolutes.
</Callout>

## The Rent-to-Revenue Rule

Before diving into neighborhood data, anchor yourself to this number: **your monthly rent should be 8-12% of your projected monthly revenue.** This is the range where rent is affordable enough to leave room for food costs (28-35%), labor (20-25%), operating expenses (10-15%), and profit (10-20%).

- **Below 8%:** You are in an unusually cheap location, possibly with trade-offs in foot traffic or visibility. Fine if the concept works without walk-in traffic (cloud kitchens, destination dining, strong delivery), but make sure the low rent is not masking a location problem.
- **8-10%:** The sweet spot. Leaves healthy room in your P&L for other expenses and profit.
- **10-12%:** Acceptable for high-turnover concepts with strong revenue per square meter (quick-service, high-volume casual dining, bars with late-night hours).
- **Above 12%:** Dangerous territory. At this level, rent is eating into your margin and any revenue shortfall will immediately turn into losses. Only viable for mall food courts or flagship locations with guaranteed foot traffic.

**How to calculate it:** Estimate your monthly revenue conservatively (assume 70% occupancy, not 100%), then multiply by 0.10. That is your target maximum rent.

**Example:** A 60-seat restaurant with a 350 THB average check, 2 turns per lunch and 1.5 turns per dinner, 6 days a week.
- Daily covers: (60 x 2) + (60 x 1.5) = 210 covers
- Daily revenue: 210 x 350 = 73,500 THB
- Monthly revenue (26 operating days): 1,911,000 THB
- Target rent at 10%: 191,100 THB/month
- For a 150 sqm space, that is 1,274 THB/sqm

This narrows your search immediately. You are looking at neighborhoods where quality restaurant spaces rent for 1,000-1,300 THB per square meter, which eliminates the top end of Thonglor and all of Siam/Chit Lom, but opens up plenty of viable options in Ekkamai, Ari, lower Sukhumvit, and Sathorn.

## Neighborhood Rent Guide

### Siam / Chit Lom / Phloen Chit

**Rent range:** 1,500-3,500 THB/sqm

This is Bangkok's retail core -- the intersection of multiple BTS lines, flanked by CentralWorld, Siam Paragon, Siam Center, and Gaysorn Village. Foot traffic is the highest in the city, but almost all of it flows through malls rather than street-level spaces.

**Key money:** 4-8 months rent for prime street-level spaces (which are extremely rare). Mall spaces typically do not charge key money but require significant fit-out investment and revenue-sharing arrangements.

**Foot traffic:** Extremely high (500+ pedestrians per 30-minute window near BTS stations), but overwhelmingly mall-directed. Street-level restaurant spaces in this area are scarce and command premium rents.

**Target customer:** Tourists, office workers (particularly around Phloen Chit and Wireless Road), affluent Thai shoppers, and the luxury hotel crowd.

**Best concepts:** Quick-service formats inside malls, branded restaurant concepts with proven models, cafes leveraging tourist foot traffic. Independent restaurants should think carefully here -- mall rents of 2,500-3,500 THB/sqm (or 25-30% revenue share) require very high turnover to justify.

**Gentrification trend:** Stable. This area has been premium for decades and is unlikely to shift significantly.

<Callout type="warning">
Mall food-and-beverage leases in Siam/Chit Lom typically require revenue-sharing of 25-30% (whichever is higher -- minimum guaranteed rent or the percentage of revenue). This means your "rent" is effectively variable and can exceed 3,500 THB/sqm during strong months. Factor this into your financial model. Many restaurants calculate based on the minimum guaranteed rent and get shocked when the revenue-share kicks in.
</Callout>

### Thonglor (Sukhumvit Soi 55)

**Rent range:** 1,200-2,500 THB/sqm

Thonglor is Bangkok's most competitive dining and nightlife district. The clientele skews affluent Thai and expat, design-conscious, and willing to spend. Average restaurant checks range from 500 to 2,000+ THB per person. The concentration of restaurants per kilometer is among the highest in Southeast Asia.

**Key money:** 3-6 months rent. Almost universal for ground-floor spaces on the main soi and popular sub-sois (Soi 13, Soi 17, Soi 25). Some landlords also charge a non-refundable "goodwill fee" on top of key money if the previous tenant was successful.

**Foot traffic:** Moderate-to-high on the main soi (80-150 per 30-minute window near J Avenue and 72 Courtyard), lower on sub-sois. Evening and night traffic significantly outweighs daytime. Weekend traffic is strong.

**Target customer:** Upper-middle-class Thai (25-45 years old), Japanese and Korean expats, Western expats, tourists staying at Sukhumvit hotels.

**Best concepts:** Upscale casual dining, Japanese and Korean cuisine, cocktail bars, date-night restaurants, contemporary Thai, brunch cafes. Be warned: Thonglor is saturated with ramen shops, Korean BBQ, and coffee shops. Differentiation is not optional -- it is survival.

**The math problem:** At 1,800 THB/sqm for a 100 sqm space (180,000 THB/month rent), you need 1,800,000 THB/month in revenue to keep rent at 10%. That is 6,000 covers per month at a 300 THB average check, or 200 per day. Achievable for a high-turnover concept, but many Thonglor restaurants fail to hit these numbers because competition splits the customer base across hundreds of options.

**Gentrification trend:** Mature and potentially plateauing. Some landlords are pricing aggressively, while newer openings are gravitating toward Ekkamai and lower Thonglor sois where rents are 20-30% lower.

### Lower Sukhumvit (Soi 1-33, Nana to Phrom Phong)

**Rent range:** 800-1,500 THB/sqm

Lower Sukhumvit stretches from Nana to Phrom Phong and encompasses a wide range of micro-neighborhoods. The Nana area (Soi 3-11) is heavily tourist-oriented with a concentration of hotels and the Arab/Indian quarter. Soi 22-24 are more residential with condominium towers. Phrom Phong (Soi 24-39) is the heart of Bangkok's Japanese community with EmQuartier and Emporium malls anchoring the area.

**Key money:** 2-4 months rent. More negotiable than Thonglor, especially above ground floor.

**Foot traffic:** Varies enormously by sub-area. Near BTS Nana and Phrom Phong: high (100-200 per 30 minutes). Deep in the sois: low (20-40). The foot traffic quality also varies -- Nana skews heavily tourist, while Phrom Phong is more upscale residential.

**Target customer:** Nana area -- tourists, Middle Eastern visitors, budget-conscious expats. Phrom Phong -- Japanese expat families, affluent Thai, hotel guests. The demographic shifts block by block.

**Best concepts:** Nana -- casual international, late-night, budget dining. Phrom Phong -- Japanese cuisine (huge built-in market), family-friendly, upscale casual. Soi 22-24 -- neighborhood bistros, wine bars, casual dining targeting condo residents.

**Gentrification trend:** Phrom Phong is stable-to-growing. Nana is undergoing gradual gentrification as older buildings are replaced by newer mixed-use developments, but the transition is slow.

### Silom / Sathorn

**Rent range:** 900-1,800 THB/sqm

Bangkok's financial district. Dense with office towers, banks, embassies, and corporate headquarters. The lunchtime crowd is enormous (Silom Road sees 200+ pedestrians per 30-minute window between 11:30 AM and 1:30 PM), but evenings and weekends are quieter, especially on Silom's main drag which empties out after 7 PM.

**Key money:** 2-5 months rent. Higher for ground-floor spaces on Silom Road proper, lower in the sois.

**Foot traffic:** Very high weekday lunch (200+), moderate weekday dinner near Sala Daeng and Convent Road, low on weekends. Patpong area has strong evening tourist traffic.

**Target customer:** Office workers (weekday lunch), afterwork professionals (5-8 PM), embassy and corporate event crowd, tourists near Patpong and Silom Night Market area.

**Best concepts:** Quick-service lunch (high turnover, 150-250 THB average check), afterwork bars, business-lunch restaurants, Japanese and international casual dining. Weekend-only concepts struggle here -- you need a concept that generates 70%+ of revenue Monday through Friday.

**The hidden gem:** The sois off Silom and Sathorn -- particularly Convent Road, Soi Sala Daeng, Sathorn Soi 10-12, and Yen Akat -- have a more neighborhood feel with residential condominiums, embassies, and schools. Rents drop to 900-1,200 THB/sqm, foot traffic is moderate but loyal, and the customer base is consistent. Some of Bangkok's most enduringly successful independent restaurants are tucked into these sois.

**Gentrification trend:** Stable. Silom has been Bangkok's business district for 40+ years, and while individual buildings change, the overall character does not shift dramatically.

### Ari / Pradipat

**Rent range:** 600-1,200 THB/sqm

Ari has become Bangkok's most beloved residential dining neighborhood. The vibe is relaxed, village-like, and community-driven. The customer base is primarily Thai (25-40 years old, creative professionals, young families) with a growing expat contingent. The cafe culture is arguably Bangkok's strongest, and the restaurant scene has matured significantly since 2020.

**Key money:** 1-3 months rent. More negotiable than Thonglor or Silom, and some landlords waive it entirely for strong concepts.

**Foot traffic:** Moderate (50-100 per 30 minutes near Ari BTS and La Villa mall). The area does not have Thonglor's foot traffic volume, but the customers are loyal and repeat frequently, which matters more than raw pedestrian count for most restaurant models.

**Target customer:** Thai creative professionals, young families, digital nomads, health-conscious diners, the Instagram-brunch crowd.

**Best concepts:** Cafes (Ari is arguably the cafe capital of Bangkok), bistros, wine bars, casual Thai with a twist, bakeries, health-focused concepts, neighborhood restaurants where the owner knows regulars by name. Avoid formal fine dining -- the Ari customer wants quality without formality.

**Why Ari works for first-time operators:** The rent-to-quality ratio is the best in central Bangkok. At 800 THB/sqm for a 100 sqm space (80,000 THB/month), you need 800,000 THB/month in revenue to hit 10% rent-to-revenue. At a 300 THB average check, that is 2,667 covers per month, or about 89 per day. With 40 seats and 2 turns per meal service, that is very achievable.

**Gentrification trend:** Still growing. New condominium projects are bringing more residents. The area between Ari BTS and Saphan Khwai BTS (particularly Pradipat Road) is the emerging frontier -- rents are 15-25% below core Ari, and the demographic is similar. Keep an eye on this zone.

### Ekkamai (Sukhumvit Soi 63)

**Rent range:** 700-1,400 THB/sqm

Ekkamai sits in Thonglor's shadow, literally one soi over, and that proximity is both its advantage and its limitation. The advantage: similar customer demographics at 20-40% lower rent. The limitation: the "second choice" perception lingers, though it is fading rapidly as Ekkamai develops its own identity.

**Key money:** 2-4 months rent. Less aggressive than Thonglor.

**Foot traffic:** Moderate near Gateway Ekkamai and the main soi entrance (60-100 per 30 minutes). Deeper sois (Ekkamai Soi 10, Soi 12) have lower foot traffic but a growing concentration of destination restaurants and bars.

**Target customer:** Similar to Thonglor but slightly younger and slightly more price-sensitive. Thai creative crowd, young expats, the after-work Sukhumvit set.

**Best concepts:** Casual dining, craft beer bars, creative cuisine, date-night restaurants, Korean and Japanese casual. The area around Gateway Ekkamai is developing a strong dining cluster. Soi 10 has emerged as a specific destination for bars and late-night dining.

**Gentrification trend:** Actively growing. Multiple new mixed-use developments are under construction, and the restaurant scene is expanding rapidly. Rents will likely increase 10-15% over the next 2-3 years.

### Ratchada (Ratchadaphisek)

**Rent range:** 500-1,000 THB/sqm

Ratchada is Bangkok's mid-market growth corridor. The opening of multiple MRT stations along Ratchadaphisek Road has transformed the area from a primarily auto-dependent district into one with genuine transit-oriented foot traffic. Condominium construction has been relentless -- dozens of new projects have launched since 2022, bringing tens of thousands of new residents.

**Key money:** 0-2 months rent. Many landlords in Ratchada do not charge key money, especially for upper-floor or non-street-facing units.

**Foot traffic:** Moderate near MRT stations (Lat Phrao, Ratchadaphisek, Sutthisan, Huai Khwang), low in between. The famous Ratchada night market area near Esplanade mall generates strong evening foot traffic.

**Target customer:** Middle-class Thai residents (25-40), condo dwellers, office workers from nearby commercial buildings. Less expat presence than Sukhumvit neighborhoods.

**Best concepts:** Affordable Thai restaurants (150-300 THB average check), hotpot and BBQ (extremely popular with the Ratchada demographic), bubble tea and dessert shops, cloud kitchens (the area is a cloud kitchen hub due to low rent and central location). Avoid high-end concepts -- the price sensitivity in this area is real.

**Why cloud kitchen operators love Ratchada:** At 500-700 THB/sqm, you can rent a 60 sqm kitchen space for 30,000-42,000 THB per month. With MRT stations providing rider accessibility and a dense surrounding condo population providing delivery demand, Ratchada offers some of the best unit economics for delivery-only operations in Bangkok.

**Gentrification trend:** Steady upward trajectory. More MRT connectivity, more condos, more foot traffic. Rents have increased 15-20% since 2023 and will continue climbing, but the base is low enough that the area remains affordable.

### Bang Na / On Nut / Udom Suk

**Rent range:** 400-800 THB/sqm

Eastern Bangkok's suburban growth corridor. The BTS extension to Bearing and Samut Prakan has opened up these neighborhoods, and residential development is booming. The demographic skews younger (25-35), Thai, and budget-conscious, with a growing contingent of expats priced out of central Sukhumvit.

**Key money:** 0-2 months rent. Rare for landlords to demand significant key money in these areas.

**Foot traffic:** Moderate near BTS stations (On Nut BTS sees 50-80 per 30 minutes), low elsewhere. The area is car-dependent between BTS stations. Mega Bangna and Central Bangna malls anchor retail traffic.

**Target customer:** Young Thai families, students (several universities nearby), budget-conscious condo residents, factory and office workers.

**Best concepts:** Affordable casual dining (100-250 THB average check), food courts, street-food-style shopfront restaurants, delivery-focused concepts (high condo density drives delivery demand), cafes near residential clusters. The area is underserved in quality dining -- there is room for concepts that deliver good food at accessible prices.

**Gentrification trend:** Growing, but still early-stage. On Nut has seen the most development, while Bang Na is further behind. Rents will climb gradually as more condos fill, but the area will remain significantly cheaper than central Bangkok for the foreseeable future.

### Old Town / Charoen Krung / Talad Noi

**Rent range:** 500-1,500 THB/sqm

This is Bangkok's most fascinating restaurant neighborhood -- and its most unpredictable. Charoen Krung is the city's oldest road, running from Chinatown through a series of historic neighborhoods that have been progressively discovered by the creative and hospitality industries. Talad Noi (the small market), Soi Nana (the speakeasy-bar district), and the streets around Warehouse 30 and the Bangkok Art and Culture Centre's satellite galleries have become magnets for design-driven restaurant and bar concepts.

**Key money:** 0-4 months rent. Wildly inconsistent. Some old-guard landlords charge nothing. Others, recognizing the gentrification premium, have started charging key money comparable to Thonglor. The variable is the landlord's awareness of what their property is now worth.

**Foot traffic:** Highly variable by micro-location. Near tourist attractions (Wat Arun area, Chinatown, Asiatique): high. In the creative-district sois: moderate on weekends, low on weekdays. Talad Noi sees spikes when exhibitions or events are running.

**Target customer:** Creatives, design-conscious millennials, tourists exploring beyond the BTS corridor, food bloggers and media, expats seeking character-driven spaces.

**Best concepts:** Wine bars, cocktail bars, creative dining, bakery-cafes, gallery-restaurant hybrids, omakase, and intimate tasting-menu concepts. The neighborhood rewards concepts with strong visual identity and Instagram appeal. Straightforward Thai restaurants do less well here -- the audience is paying for the experience and the setting as much as the food.

**The renovation factor:** Most Charoen Krung spaces are historic shophouses requiring significant renovation. Budget 15,000-25,000 THB per square meter for a quality fit-out that respects the building's character. The space itself is the attraction -- exposed brick, original tile, high ceilings, natural patina. Some of Bangkok's most photographed restaurant interiors are in these buildings, which provides built-in marketing.

**Gentrification trend:** Actively gentrifying, and the pace is accelerating. The opening of a major new hotel (Capella Bangkok) and the expansion of the creative district have pushed rents up 25-30% in desirable pockets since 2023. Early movers locked in 500-700 THB/sqm leases; new entrants are seeing 900-1,500 THB/sqm for comparable spaces.

<Callout type="tip">
If you are considering Charoen Krung, lock in a long lease (5+ years) with a reasonable escalation cap. The area is gentrifying fast, and landlords are catching on to what their properties are worth. A 3-year lease at 800 THB/sqm that renews at "market rate" could jump to 1,400 THB/sqm at renewal. Get the escalation in writing before you invest 2 million THB in renovating someone else's building.
</Callout>

## Key Money by Neighborhood

Key money -- the upfront, non-refundable payment to secure a lease -- varies more by neighborhood culture than by any fixed rule. Here is what to expect.

| Neighborhood | Key Money Range | Negotiability |
|---|---|---|
| Siam / Chit Lom | 4-8 months rent | Low -- premium locations, high demand |
| Thonglor | 3-6 months rent | Moderate -- depends on vacancy duration |
| Lower Sukhumvit | 2-4 months rent | Moderate to high |
| Silom / Sathorn | 2-5 months rent | Moderate |
| Ari / Pradipat | 1-3 months rent | High -- many landlords waive entirely |
| Ekkamai | 2-4 months rent | Moderate to high |
| Ratchada | 0-2 months rent | High -- often zero |
| Bang Na / On Nut | 0-2 months rent | Very high -- rarely charged |
| Charoen Krung | 0-4 months rent | Wildly variable |

**Negotiation tactic:** Key money is the most negotiable part of any Bangkok restaurant lease. Offer to sign a longer lease (4-5 years instead of 3) in exchange for reduced or eliminated key money. Landlords who are confident you will stay and pay rent for 5 years are more willing to forgo the upfront payment. ## Mall vs Street vs Shophouse Economics

The three main space types in Bangkok have fundamentally different economics. Understanding these differences is as important as understanding neighborhood rents.

### Mall Spaces

**Rent model:** Either fixed rent (1,500-3,500+ THB/sqm) or revenue-sharing (25-30% of monthly revenue, whichever is higher than minimum guaranteed rent).

**Total occupancy cost:** 30-40% of revenue when you factor in rent, common area maintenance (CAM) fees, marketing fund contributions, and mandatory operational requirements (uniforms, mall-approved signage, required operating hours).

**Build-out cost:** High. Mall management controls design approval, and fit-out requirements are strict. Budget 20,000-40,000 THB/sqm for a mall restaurant build-out.

**Lease terms:** 3-5 years, limited negotiation. Malls dictate terms.

**Break-even challenge:** With 30-40% of revenue going to occupancy, your food cost must stay below 30% and labor below 18% to have any profit margin. This is very tight and leaves almost no room for error.

**When malls make sense:** Established chains with proven unit economics, food court stalls (lower rent, higher turnover), and brands using the mall as a marketing/awareness play rather than a profit center.

### Street-Level Shopfront

**Rent model:** Fixed rent per square meter, plus security deposit (typically 3 months) and key money (0-6 months).

**Total occupancy cost:** 8-15% of revenue (rent, deposit amortization, utilities, maintenance).

**Build-out cost:** Moderate. 8,000-20,000 THB/sqm depending on the existing condition of the space.

**Lease terms:** 3+3 years is standard (3-year initial term with a 3-year renewal option). More negotiable than malls.

**Advantages:** Flexible hours, full creative control, reasonable occupancy costs, direct street visibility.

**Disadvantages:** No built-in foot traffic (you generate your own), parking can be difficult, infrastructure varies (some shophouses have dated electrical and plumbing).

### Heritage Shophouse

**Rent model:** Fixed rent, but the range is wide (500-2,500 THB/sqm) because the market for heritage spaces is less standardized. Some landlords have owned the building for generations and charge below market. Others have caught the gentrification wave and price aggressively.

**Total occupancy cost:** 8-18% of revenue. The renovation cost is the wildcard -- a heritage shophouse needing structural work can cost 25,000-35,000 THB/sqm to renovate, which means your effective occupancy cost (including renovation amortization over the lease term) can be significantly higher than the rent alone suggests.

**Build-out cost:** High if restoration is needed. 15,000-35,000 THB/sqm. Structural reinforcement, updated electrical (most heritage shophouses have single-phase power only), modern plumbing in a building designed before indoor plumbing existed, and cosmetic restoration that preserves character while meeting health codes.

**Lease terms:** Variable. Some heritage shophouse landlords offer only 1-2 year leases, which is dangerous given the renovation investment. Insist on 5+ years with a renewal option, or walk away regardless of how charming the space is.

**Advantages:** Unique character that is impossible to replicate in a new building, built-in Instagram appeal, strong media coverage potential, lower base rents in some cases.

**Disadvantages:** Infrastructure challenges, uncertain structural condition, potentially difficult landlord relationships (family-owned properties with informal lease practices), and the renovation investment may not be recoverable if the lease is short.

<Callout type="warning">
Heritage shophouse leases in Bangkok are often informal. I have seen operators invest 3 million THB in renovation based on a verbal agreement, only to have the landlord raise rent by 40% at the end of year one. Get everything in writing. Have a lawyer review the lease. Confirm the landlord actually owns the property (request a chanote -- land title deed). The romance of the old shophouse wears off quickly when your lease is not enforceable.
</Callout>

## Co-Working Kitchen Spaces

A newer option that sits between renting your own space and running a cloud kitchen from a shared facility. Co-working kitchens rent fully equipped kitchen stations by the hour, day, or month, typically without long-term lease commitments.

**Monthly cost:** 10,000-45,000 THB depending on location, station size, and hours of access.

**Key players:** CloudKitchens, FoodStory Kitchen, Kitchen Hub, and various independent operators across Bangkok.

**Best for:** Concept testing before committing to a full lease, pop-up restaurants, catering businesses needing kitchen access, small-batch production (sauces, baked goods, meal prep).

**Limitations:** You do not control the space, shared equipment can create bottlenecks, limited branding opportunities, and you are dependent on the facility operator's schedule and rules.

**Rent equivalent:** If you use a co-working kitchen for 8 hours per day at 500 THB per hour, your monthly cost is roughly 120,000 THB -- comparable to renting your own 100 sqm space at 1,200 THB/sqm. The advantage is flexibility (no long-term commitment, no renovation investment), but the disadvantage is that you are paying a premium for that flexibility and never building equity in a space.

For a full exploration of the cloud kitchen and co-working kitchen model, see our [cloud kitchen guide](/blog/cloud-kitchen-bangkok).

## Neighborhood Foot Traffic Profiles

Foot traffic count is the most under-used data point in restaurant site selection, which is remarkable given that it is free to collect and directly predicts revenue potential. Here are the profiles I have observed across Bangkok's main dining neighborhoods.

### Weekday Lunch (12:00-12:30 PM)

| Neighborhood | Foot Traffic (per 30 min) | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Silom / Sathorn (main road) | 200-350 | Office workers, fast-paced, price-sensitive |
| Siam / Chit Lom (near BTS) | 300-500+ | Mall shoppers, tourists, mixed |
| Thonglor (main soi) | 80-130 | Young professionals, lunch groups |
| Ari (near BTS) | 60-100 | Residents, freelancers, relaxed |
| Lower Sukhumvit | 70-150 | Tourists, office workers, hotel guests |
| Ekkamai | 50-80 | Similar to Thonglor but lighter |
| Ratchada | 40-80 | Condo residents, office workers |
| Bang Na / On Nut | 30-60 | Students, residents, budget-conscious |

### Weekend Dinner (7:00-7:30 PM)

| Neighborhood | Foot Traffic (per 30 min) | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Thonglor | 120-200 | Date nights, groups, bar-hoppers |
| Ekkamai | 80-130 | Similar to Thonglor, growing |
| Ari | 70-110 | Families, couples, neighborhood regulars |
| Charoen Krung | 50-100 | Creatives, tourists, bar-goers (varies with events) |
| Silom / Sathorn | 40-70 | Drops sharply from weekday |
| Siam / Chit Lom | 150-300 | Mall dinner crowd |
| Ratchada | 60-100 | Night market, condo residents |
| Bang Na / On Nut | 30-60 | Mall-dependent |

The pattern is clear: Silom dominates weekday lunch and drops on weekends, while Thonglor and Ekkamai dominate evenings and weekends. Ari is consistent across all periods but never hits extreme peaks. Ratchada and Bang Na are steadier but at lower volumes. Your concept needs to match the foot traffic timing of your chosen neighborhood.

## Gentrification Watch: Where Rents Are Moving

Two neighborhoods are worth watching closely because they are in active gentrification -- meaning today's rents are lower than what they will be in 2-3 years. Getting in early means locking in favorable lease terms before the market catches up.

### Charoen Krung / Talad Noi

The creative-district transformation is well underway, but still has room to run. Key indicators:

- Capella Bangkok hotel opened, bringing high-spending guests to the area
- Warehouse 30 and surrounding gallery/cafe cluster expanding
- Multiple new cocktail bars and restaurants have opened since 2024, each raising the area's profile
- International media coverage (Condé Nast Traveller, Monocle, Wallpaper*) driving tourist awareness
- Several prominent Bangkok restaurateurs have signed leases in the area for 2026-2027 openings

**Prediction:** Core Charoen Krung rents will increase 15-25% by 2028. The best spaces (ground-floor shophouses with original architectural details on quiet sois) are being snapped up. If you are serious about this area, act within the next 12 months.

### Ari / Saphan Khwai / Pradipat

Ari proper is well-established, but the northern extension -- Pradipat Road, Saphan Khwai, and the area between Ari BTS and Saphan Khwai BTS -- is the current frontier. Rents here are 15-30% below core Ari while the customer base is similar.

**Why it is growing:**
- New condominium projects bringing residents (4-5 projects completed in 2024-2025)
- BTS Saphan Khwai station provides transit connectivity
- Core Ari rent increases are pushing price-sensitive operators north
- The cafe and casual dining scene is actively expanding into the zone

**Prediction:** Pradipat/Saphan Khwai rents will converge toward core Ari levels (600-1,200 THB/sqm) within 3-4 years, up from today's 400-800 THB/sqm.

## How to Use This Data

Rent benchmarks are a starting point, not a decision. Here is how to translate these numbers into action.

**Step 1: Calculate your target rent from your financial model.** Use the rent-to-revenue ratio (8-12%) against your conservative revenue projection. This gives you a maximum rent per month.

**Step 2: Divide by your required square meters.** This gives you a maximum THB/sqm, which immediately narrows your neighborhood options.

**Step 3: Cross-reference with customer demographics.** Your concept needs to match the neighborhood's customer profile. A 1,200 THB/person omakase in Ratchada will struggle regardless of how cheap the rent is. A 100 THB/person lunch spot in Thonglor will generate volume but may not cover the rent.

**Step 4: Visit the shortlisted neighborhoods.** Walk the streets, count foot traffic, eat at competitors, talk to neighboring business owners. Data tells you where to look, but feet-on-the-ground observation tells you whether it actually works.

**Step 5: Negotiate the lease.** Use the benchmark data in this article to anchor your rent negotiation. If a Ratchada landlord asks 1,200 THB/sqm, you know the market range is 500-1,000 THB/sqm and can push back with confidence. For detailed negotiation tactics, see our [lease negotiation guide](/blog/negotiate-restaurant-lease-bangkok).

For a full financial model including rent, build-out, equipment, staffing, and working capital, see our [restaurant cost breakdown](/blog/cost-restaurant-bangkok).

## The Bottom Line

Rent is the single largest fixed cost in your restaurant, and it is the one cost you lock in before you serve a single customer. Getting it wrong does not just reduce your profit -- it can make profitability impossible regardless of how good your food or how strong your concept.

The neighborhoods in this article are not ranked from best to worst. They are different markets serving different concepts at different price points. And if you are considering locations outside Bangkok entirely, our [guide to the best cities for restaurants in Thailand](/blog/best-cities-restaurant-thailand) covers Chiang Mai, Phuket, Pattaya, and more -- often at 20-40% lower rent than equivalent Bangkok spaces. Thonglor at 2,000 THB/sqm is fair value for a concept that can generate 600+ THB average checks with strong weekend dinner traffic. Ratchada at 600 THB/sqm is fair value for a concept targeting the neighborhood's middle-class Thai diners at 200 THB average checks. Both can be profitable. Both can also fail if the concept does not match the economics.

Match your concept to the neighborhood. Match the rent to your revenue model. Lock in a lease with fair escalation terms. That is the formula.

Licensing is the part of opening a restaurant that nobody gets excited about. There is no Instagram post for your food handler certificate. Nobody throws a party when your grease trap inspection passes. But getting your licenses wrong, or simply not having them, can result in fines, forced closure, or criminal prosecution under Thai law.

I keep a running spreadsheet of every license, permit, and registration my clients need. Over the past 15 years, that list has grown from about eight items to over twenty. Regulations have tightened, enforcement has become more consistent, and Bangkok district offices have gotten noticeably more thorough since 2023.

This guide is the full checklist. Every license. Every permit. Every registration. With actual costs, realistic timelines, and renewal schedules as of early 2026. Print it. Save it. Share it with your lawyer.

<Callout type="info">
All costs in this article are in Thai Baht (THB). At the time of writing, 1 USD is approximately 35.5 THB. Fees are set by government agencies and are generally consistent nationwide, though processing times vary significantly between Bangkok district offices and provincial offices. Bangkok is typically faster.
</Callout>

## Before You Start: Prerequisites

Before you can apply for any restaurant-specific license, you need three things in place:

**1. A registered company.** If you are a foreigner, this means a Thai limited company with at least 51% Thai shareholding, a BOI-promoted company, or a Treaty of Amity company (US citizens). Company registration takes 2-4 weeks and costs 30,000-80,000 THB through a law firm. For a full breakdown of company structures, see: [Can a Foreigner Own a Restaurant in Thailand?](/blog/foreigner-opening-restaurant-thailand)

**2. A signed lease.** Most license applications require proof that you have a legal right to occupy the premises. You need either a signed lease agreement or property ownership documentation. The lease must match the company name on the application.

**3. A completed or near-completed build-out.** Several permits, particularly the restaurant license and fire safety certificate, require a physical inspection of the premises. The kitchen must be installed, ventilation must be functional, and fire safety equipment must be in place before inspectors will sign off.

<Callout type="warning">
The licensing process in Thailand is largely sequential, not parallel. You cannot apply for a restaurant license without a lease and a build-out. You cannot get a liquor license without a restaurant license. Plan for 3-5 months of licensing work after your build-out is complete. I have seen operators budget zero time for this and end up paying rent on a finished restaurant for three months while waiting for permits.
</Callout>

## The Complete License Checklist

### 1. Restaurant License (Sor Bor 5 or Sor Bor 6)

This is the foundational permit. You cannot legally operate a food establishment without it.

**What it is:** Authorization from the local district office (Khet in Bangkok, Amphoe in other provinces) to operate a food service establishment. The license type depends on your establishment size:

- **Sor Bor 5** — For establishments with a food preparation area exceeding 200 square meters. This covers most full-service restaurants.
- **Sor Bor 6** — For smaller establishments under 200 square meters. This covers most cafes, small restaurants, and food stalls with permanent structures.

**Issuing authority:** Local district office (Khet/Amphoe), specifically the health and sanitation division.

**Requirements:**
- Completed application form (available at the district office or online through the Bangkok Metropolitan Administration website for Bangkok locations)
- Copy of company registration certificate and list of directors
- Copy of the signed lease or land title deed
- Building plan showing kitchen layout, storage areas, and dining area
- Identification documents of the managing director or authorized signatory
- Photos of the premises (exterior and interior, including kitchen)
- Physical inspection of the premises by district health officers

**The inspection process:** A health officer will visit your premises to check food preparation areas, storage, refrigeration, waste disposal systems, hand-washing stations, pest control measures, and general cleanliness. They are looking for compliance with the Public Health Act B.E. 2535 (as amended) and local sanitation ordinances. Common failure points include inadequate hand-washing stations (you need at least one dedicated hand-washing sink in the kitchen area with soap dispenser), insufficient ventilation above cooking stations, and missing or non-functional grease traps.

**Cost:** 2,000-5,000 THB (varies by district and establishment size)

**Timeline:** 15-30 working days from submission of complete application to issuance

**Renewal:** Annual. Submit renewal application 30 days before expiration.

<Callout type="tip">
Build a relationship with your local district office early. Before you even start your build-out, visit the Khet office with your floor plans and ask for informal guidance. Health officers will often tell you exactly what they want to see during the inspection. This pre-consultation is free and can save you from failing the formal inspection — which delays your opening by 2-4 weeks while you fix issues and reschedule.
</Callout>

### 2. Food Handler Certificate (Sor Sor 6)

**What it is:** Individual certification that food handlers in your establishment have received basic food safety training. Under the Public Health Act, anyone who directly handles food that will be served to customers must hold this certificate.

**Issuing authority:** Local district office or the Department of Health, Ministry of Public Health.

**Requirements:**
- Attendance at an approved food safety training course (typically 3-6 hours)
- Basic health screening (no communicable diseases)
- Identification documents

**Who needs it:** All kitchen staff who handle food, including the head chef, sous chefs, prep cooks, and anyone involved in plating. Front-of-house staff who only serve plated food generally do not need it, though some district offices interpret the requirement more broadly.

**Cost:** 100-500 THB per person for the training course. Some district offices offer free training sessions.

**Timeline:** Training courses run regularly, weekly in most Bangkok districts and monthly in smaller provinces. Certificate is issued upon completion.

**Renewal:** Every 3 years. New staff must obtain their certificate within 30 days of starting work.

**Practical reality:** Enforcement varies. In central Bangkok (Watthana, Pathum Wan, Khlong Toei districts), inspectors regularly check for food handler certificates. In outer districts and smaller provinces, enforcement is less consistent. Regardless, get your staff certified. If a customer gets food poisoning and you cannot produce food handler certificates, your legal liability increases significantly.

### 3. Alcohol License (Excise Department)

If you serve alcohol, you need a license from the Excise Department. This is one of the most commonly misunderstood licenses in Thailand. I have written a comprehensive guide on this topic: [Thailand Alcohol Law in 2026](/blog/thailand-alcohol-law-2026). Here is the operational summary for restaurants.

**License types for restaurants:**

**Type 1 — On-premises consumption only:** Allows you to serve alcohol for consumption on your premises. This is sufficient for the vast majority of restaurants. You buy alcohol from licensed distributors and serve it by the glass or bottle. Customers consume it at your establishment.

**Type 2 — On-premises consumption plus retail sales:** Allows you to both serve alcohol on-premises and sell sealed bottles for customers to take away. If you want to sell bottles of wine or craft beer for takeaway, you need a Type 2 license. This is relevant for wine bars, craft beer restaurants, and any establishment with a retail component.

**Issuing authority:** Excise Department (Krom Sanphaasamit). Apply at the provincial Excise office for your area. In Bangkok, this is the Bangkok Metropolitan Excise Office.

**Requirements:**
- Valid restaurant license (Sor Bor 5 or 6)
- Company registration documents
- Lease agreement or property documents
- Floor plan showing the service area and alcohol storage
- Identification of the license applicant (must be at least 20 years old)
- No criminal record certificate for the license holder
- The premises must not be within 300 meters of a school, temple, or hospital (measured by walking distance, not straight-line distance)

<Callout type="warning">
The 300-meter rule is the number one reason alcohol license applications get rejected. Before you sign a lease, check the proximity to schools, temples (including small street-side shrines that are officially registered), and hospitals. Use Google Maps to get approximate distances, then walk the actual route. The Excise Department measures by walking distance along public roads, not straight-line distance. I have seen applications rejected because a temple entrance was 280 meters away by foot, even though it was 350 meters in a straight line.
</Callout>

**Timeline:** 30-60 working days from complete application. Apply well before your planned opening date. The Excise Department processes applications in batches, not individually, so timing can be unpredictable.

**Renewal:** Annual. The renewal window opens 90 days before your license expiration. Renew early. Operating with an expired license carries the same penalties as operating without one.

### 4. Signboard Tax (Phasi Paay)

**What it is:** A local tax on any commercial signage displayed on or around your premises. This includes your restaurant name sign, menu boards visible from the street, hanging banners, and neon or LED signs. If it is visible from public space and promotes your business, it is technically taxable.

**Issuing authority:** Local Administration Organization. In Bangkok this is the BMA (Bangkok Metropolitan Administration); in other provinces, the local Tessabaan (municipality).

**How it is calculated:** The tax is based on sign dimensions. For signs in Thai language only, the rate is 5 THB per 500 square centimeters per year. For signs that include foreign languages (which most restaurant signs do), the rate increases to 52 THB per 500 square centimeters per year. Yes, adding English to your sign increases the tax by roughly 10x.

**Practical example:** A standard restaurant signboard that is 3 meters wide by 0.8 meters tall = 24,000 square centimeters = 48 units of 500 sq cm. In Thai only: 48 x 5 = 240 THB/year. In Thai and English: 48 x 52 = 2,496 THB/year. A large illuminated sign could run 5,000-15,000 THB annually.

**Timeline:** Register within 30 days of installing your sign. You can do this at your local district office.

**Renewal:** Annual. Tax is due in March each year.

**Penalty for non-registration:** A surcharge of 10% per month on the unpaid amount.

### 5. VAT Registration

**What it is:** Registration for Value Added Tax with the Revenue Department. Thailand's standard VAT rate is 7%.

**Threshold:** Mandatory registration if your annual revenue exceeds 1.8 million THB (approximately 150,000 THB per month). Given that most restaurants in Bangkok will exceed this threshold within their first few months, you should plan on registering.

**Issuing authority:** Area Revenue Office (Samnak Ngaan Sannphaakon Phuun Thii).

**Requirements:**
- Company registration documents
- Lease agreement
- Identification of company directors
- Bank account statements
- Evidence of business activity (invoices, receipts)

**Cost:** Free to register.

**Timeline:** 7-15 working days.

**Obligations once registered:**
- Issue tax invoices for all transactions
- File monthly VAT returns (Por Por 30) by the 15th of the following month
- Maintain VAT purchase and sales records
- Submit annual VAT audit if requested

<Callout type="info">
Many small restaurant operators try to avoid VAT registration by keeping reported revenue below 1.8 million THB. This is increasingly risky. The Revenue Department cross-references data from delivery platforms (Grab, LINE MAN), POS system providers, and bank account activity. If your actual revenue exceeds the threshold and you are not registered, you face back-tax assessments, penalties, and potential criminal charges. Register properly and build VAT into your pricing from day one.
</Callout>

### 6. Social Security Registration

**What it is:** Registration with the Social Security Office for employer and employee contributions. This is mandatory for any business with one or more employees.

**Issuing authority:** Social Security Office (Samnak Ngaan Prakan Sangkhom).

**Timeline:** You must register within 30 days of hiring your first employee.

**Cost:** No registration fee. The ongoing contribution is:
- Employer: 5% of employee's salary (capped at 750 THB/month per employee)
- Employee: 5% of salary (also capped at 750 THB/month, deducted from wages)
- Government: 2.75% contribution

The cap is based on a maximum salary of 15,000 THB for contribution calculation purposes. So even if you pay a chef 80,000 THB/month, your social security contribution is still capped at 750 THB/month for that employee.

**Practical impact:** For a restaurant with 10 staff, your monthly social security cost as employer is 4,500-7,500 THB. This is a recurring payroll expense. Build it into your staffing budget from day one.

For more on employment law and staffing costs: [Restaurant Staffing in Thailand: Salaries, Labor Law, and Retention](/blog/restaurant-staffing-thailand)

### 7. Fire Safety Certificate

**What it is:** Certification that your premises meet fire safety requirements under the Building Control Act and the Fire Prevention and Suppression Act.

**Issuing authority:** Local district fire department (in Bangkok, coordinated through the BMA Fire and Rescue Department).

**Requirements:**
- Fire extinguishers appropriate for kitchen fires (Class K / wet chemical extinguisher for cooking oil fires, plus ABC dry chemical for general use)
- Clearly marked emergency exits with illuminated signage
- Evacuation plan posted in visible locations
- Fire suppression system in the kitchen hood/ventilation (required for larger establishments, generally those above 200 sqm)
- Annual inspection of fire equipment by a certified provider
- For buildings with multiple floors: smoke detectors, sprinkler systems, and fire-rated doors may be required

**Cost:** Inspection is free. Fire safety equipment costs 15,000-80,000 THB depending on your premises size and requirements:
- Fire extinguishers: 1,500-3,000 THB each (you need 2-4 minimum)
- Emergency exit signs: 800-2,000 THB each
- Kitchen hood fire suppression system: 30,000-60,000 THB (for commercial kitchen hoods)
- Smoke detectors: 500-1,500 THB each

**Timeline:** Schedule an inspection through your district office. Typical wait time is 2-4 weeks. If you fail, fix the issues and reschedule, which adds another 2-3 weeks.

**Renewal:** Inspection is typically required annually as part of your restaurant license renewal. Keep your fire equipment maintenance records. Inspectors ask for them.

### 8. Building Use Permit (Ror Ror 4)

**What it is:** Authorization to use a building for commercial purposes. If you are converting a residential property to a restaurant, changing the use of an existing commercial space, or doing significant structural modifications, you need this permit.

**Issuing authority:** Local Building Control Office (through the BMA in Bangkok or the Tessabaan in provinces).

**The two forms:**
- **Ror Ror 4** — Building use permit for new construction or major modifications. Issued after final building inspection confirms compliance with approved plans.
- **Ror Ror 14** — Notification of building modification. Required if you are making structural changes to an existing building (moving walls, adding mezzanines, modifying load-bearing elements).

**When you need it:** If you are leasing an existing restaurant space and not making structural changes, the landlord should already have the Ror Ror 4. Confirm this before signing your lease. If the building does not have a valid use permit for commercial/restaurant use, you are taking on serious risk. The district office can order the building vacated.

If you are doing a build-out that involves structural changes (which many restaurant renovations do), you need to file Ror Ror 14 before starting construction.

**Cost:** Application fee is 200-500 THB. The real cost is the architect or engineer's certification that the building meets code, typically 10,000-30,000 THB.

**Timeline:** 30-45 working days for Ror Ror 4. Ror Ror 14 notification processing is faster, typically 15-20 working days.

<Callout type="tip">
When scouting restaurant locations, always ask the landlord to show you the existing Ror Ror 4 (building use permit). If the building was originally permitted for residential use, converting it to commercial/restaurant use requires a new permit application — and it is not guaranteed to be approved. Zoning restrictions can block the conversion entirely. Verify zoning before you negotiate lease terms.
</Callout>

### 9. Music License

**What it is:** Authorization to publicly perform or broadcast copyrighted music in your establishment. This includes playing background music through speakers, live performances, DJ sets, and even having a television that broadcasts music channels.

**Issuing authority:** Music Copyright (Thailand) Ltd. (MCT) is the primary performing rights organization. For international repertoire, you may also need a license from the International Federation of the Phonographic Industry (IFPI) Thailand chapter.

**Cost:** MCT license fees are based on your venue type and capacity:
- Small restaurant/cafe (under 50 seats): 2,000-4,000 THB/year
- Medium restaurant (50-150 seats): 4,000-8,000 THB/year
- Large restaurant/bar (150+ seats): 8,000-15,000 THB/year
- Venues with live music or DJ: 10,000-25,000 THB/year (additional performance license)

**Timeline:** Application processing takes 5-10 working days.

**Renewal:** Annual.

**Enforcement reality:** MCT has become more aggressive about enforcement since 2023. They send representatives to restaurants, record what music is playing, and issue demand letters for unlicensed venues. The penalty for copyright infringement under Thai law can reach 200,000 THB per offense, plus imprisonment up to four years. For the sake of a 4,000 THB annual license, this is not worth the risk.

**Alternative:** Use royalty-free music services (Epidemic Sound, Artlist, or similar) specifically licensed for commercial use. This eliminates the need for an MCT license, though you should keep documentation of your royalty-free license in case of an inspection.

### 10. Tobacco Sales License

**What it is:** Authorization to sell tobacco products at your establishment. Most restaurants do not sell cigarettes, but some bars and restaurants with late-night operations choose to.

**Issuing authority:** Excise Department (same office as your alcohol license).

**Cost:** 100 THB/year for a retail tobacco license.

**Requirements:** The applicant must be at least 18 years old. You cannot sell tobacco to anyone under 20. Tobacco product displays must comply with the Tobacco Products Control Act, which means no visible branding and no display of individual packs (they must be stored behind a counter or in a closed cabinet).

**Timeline:** 7-15 working days.

<Callout type="info">
Thailand has some of the strictest tobacco control laws in Southeast Asia. Smoking is banned inside all air-conditioned restaurants. If you have an outdoor smoking area, it must be clearly designated and separated from the dining area. Ashtrays must carry health warnings. Failing to enforce smoking regulations in your restaurant can result in fines against the business, not just the smoker.
</Callout>

### 11. Environmental Permits

Thailand's environmental regulations for restaurants focus on three areas: wastewater, grease, and ventilation emissions.

**Grease Trap Requirement**

Every restaurant that does any form of cooking must have an operational grease trap. This is a physical device installed in your kitchen drainage system that separates fats, oils, and grease from wastewater before it enters the sewer system.

- **Requirement:** Mandatory under the Enhancement and Conservation of National Environmental Quality Act and local BMA ordinances
- **Inspection:** Part of the restaurant license inspection. The grease trap must be installed and functional before your Sor Bor 5/6 is issued.
- **Cost:** 5,000-25,000 THB for the grease trap itself, depending on size. Installation: 3,000-10,000 THB. Regular cleaning (at least monthly): 2,000-5,000 THB per service.
- **Common failure:** Grease traps that are installed but not maintained. District health officers will check for a maintenance log during inspections. If your grease trap is full and non-functional, you can be fined and your restaurant license can be suspended.

**Kitchen Ventilation and Exhaust**

Your kitchen exhaust system must adequately remove smoke, steam, and cooking odors. The exhaust vent must not discharge directly into public walkways, neighboring residences, or enclosed areas.

- **Requirements vary by district** but generally include: commercial kitchen hood over all cooking stations, exhaust fan with minimum airflow capacity (based on kitchen size), exhaust duct routed to roof level or an approved discharge point, and grease filters in the hood system.
- **Cost:** A commercial kitchen hood and exhaust system runs 50,000-200,000 THB depending on kitchen size and complexity.
- **Common problem:** Restaurants in shophouses that vent kitchen exhaust into narrow sois, causing complaints from neighbors. The district office can order you to modify your exhaust system, which is an expensive retrofit if your kitchen is already built.

**Wastewater Discharge**

If your restaurant generates significant wastewater (which it will), you must comply with wastewater discharge standards. In Bangkok, the BMA monitors wastewater quality from commercial establishments.

- **Requirement:** Wastewater must meet standard effluent parameters (BOD, TSS, oil and grease content) before discharge into the sewer system.
- **Practical compliance:** An operational grease trap and proper kitchen drainage are usually sufficient for most restaurants. Larger establishments may need an additional wastewater treatment system.

### 12. FDA Compliance

**When you need it:** If your restaurant imports food ingredients, manufactures food products (sauces, condiments, baked goods) for retail sale, or uses food supplements or additives, you may need FDA registration.

**What most restaurants need:** Standard restaurants that purchase ingredients from local suppliers and prepare food for immediate on-premises consumption are generally exempt from FDA product registration. However, you must still comply with FDA food safety standards, and your ingredients must be sourced from FDA-approved suppliers.

**When FDA registration becomes relevant:**
- You produce a signature sauce or condiment that you sell in bottles (retail product — requires FDA product registration)
- You import specialty ingredients directly (importer license from FDA required)
- You use food coloring, preservatives, or additives that fall under FDA regulation
- You sell food products through retail channels (online, grocery stores, markets)

**Cost:** FDA product registration costs 2,000-5,000 THB per product. Import license costs 5,000-10,000 THB. A food safety consultant can guide you through the process for 20,000-50,000 THB.

**Timeline:** FDA product registration takes 30-90 working days. Import license processing takes 15-30 working days.

### 13. Commercial Insurance

**What it is:** While not technically a "license," commercial insurance is a practical requirement for any restaurant in Thailand. Several landlords in Bangkok now require proof of insurance before signing a lease.

**Essential coverage:**
- **Public liability insurance:** Covers claims from customers or third parties injured on your premises. Minimum recommended coverage: 5 million THB. Premium: 8,000-25,000 THB/year.
- **Property insurance:** Covers fire, flood, and other damage to your equipment and fit-out. Coverage should match your renovation and equipment investment. Premium: 15,000-50,000 THB/year depending on coverage amount and location (flood risk affects premium significantly).
- **Business interruption insurance:** Covers lost income if you are forced to close due to insured events. Worth considering if your monthly fixed costs (rent, salaries) are high. Premium: 10,000-30,000 THB/year.
- **Workers' compensation:** Supplements the Social Security system for work-related injuries. Kitchen environments have inherent hazards (burns, cuts, slips). Premium: 5,000-15,000 THB/year depending on staff count.

**Recommendation:** Get quotes from at least three insurers. AIA, Muang Thai Insurance, and Bangkok Insurance all offer restaurant-specific packages. A good insurance broker can bundle these policies and save you 15-20% compared to buying separately.

</Callout>

## Special Situations

### Opening Inside a Shopping Mall

If your restaurant is in a shopping mall (CentralWorld, EmQuartier, ICONSIAM, etc.), the mall management typically handles several permits on your behalf or as part of the common lease structure:

- Building use permit (Ror Ror 4) — held by the mall
- Fire safety compliance — managed by mall building management
- Some environmental permits — covered by the mall's central systems

You still need your own: restaurant license, alcohol license (if applicable), food handler certificates, VAT registration, social security registration, and music license.

The mall will require you to show proof of all your individual licenses before allowing you to open. They are usually more strict about compliance than standalone landlords because their own operating permits depend on tenant compliance.

### Operating a Food Truck

Food trucks and mobile food operations have a different licensing path. You need a mobile vendor license from the local district office rather than a standard Sor Bor 5/6. You also need vehicle registration documents showing the vehicle is approved for commercial food service use. Health inspections apply to the vehicle itself.

For more on the food truck model: [How to Start a Food Truck Business in Thailand](/blog/food-truck-thailand)

### Foreign-Owned Restaurants

If you are a foreigner, your licensing process includes additional layers: work permit for yourself and any foreign staff, potential Foreign Business License (if required by your company structure), and additional scrutiny during inspections in some districts. Your Thai lawyer should coordinate the licensing sequence with your work permit application to avoid gaps where you have a restaurant license but cannot legally work in your own business.

Full guide: [Can a Foreigner Own a Restaurant in Thailand?](/blog/foreigner-opening-restaurant-thailand)

## Common Licensing Mistakes

After helping 50+ restaurants through this process, here are the mistakes I see repeatedly:

**Signing a lease before checking the 300-meter alcohol rule.** This is the most expensive mistake because you are locked into a lease for a location where you cannot legally serve alcohol. If your concept depends on beverage revenue, and most restaurants do, this can destroy your financial model.

**Not budgeting time for sequential processing.** You cannot get your alcohol license without your restaurant license, and you cannot get your restaurant license without passing inspection — each step has a queue. If the health inspector fails your kitchen for a missing hand-washing station, you fix it, then wait another 2-3 weeks for a re-inspection. These delays cascade.

**Assuming your landlord has all building permits.** Many older buildings in Bangkok, especially shophouses in Chinatown, Charoen Krung, and Rattanakosin, do not have valid building use permits for commercial restaurant operation. Verify before you commit.

**Ignoring music licensing.** MCT sends undercover inspectors to restaurants. They use the Shazam app to identify what is playing, document it, and then send a demand letter. The settlement they request is typically 50,000-200,000 THB for retroactive licensing. Paying 4,000 THB per year for a license is considerably cheaper.

**Not training staff on compliance.** Your staff need to know what to do when an inspector arrives: who to contact, where licenses are posted, how to show the grease trap maintenance log. A surprised or uncooperative staff member can turn a routine inspection into a problem.

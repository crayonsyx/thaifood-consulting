In May 2025, Foodpanda officially pulled out of Thailand. After burning through an estimated 13 billion THB over a decade trying to compete in one of Southeast Asia's most competitive food delivery markets, Delivery Hero sold its Thai operations to Grab and shut the doors. The orange riders disappeared from Bangkok's streets within weeks. Restaurants that had built part of their revenue on the platform scrambled to redirect orders elsewhere.

I remember the week it happened. Three of my consulting clients called me on the same day. One was a cloud kitchen operator in Lat Phrao who had been doing 35% of his volume through Foodpanda. Another was a casual Thai restaurant in Thonglor where Foodpanda happened to be the top-performing platform because of a promotional deal they had been running. The third was a new operator who had just finished onboarding with Foodpanda and had not even completed setup on Grab or LINE MAN yet.

The Foodpanda exit was not a surprise to anyone paying attention. The company had been cutting back on promotions since late 2024, closing smaller city operations, and losing market share steadily. But the speed of the final withdrawal caught many restaurant owners off guard. One day you have three platform options. The next day you have two.

Thailand's food delivery market is now a duopoly: GrabFood and LINE MAN Wongnai. Together they control roughly 86% of all food delivery orders in the country, with the remaining 14% split among direct ordering channels, smaller aggregators, and restaurant-owned delivery.

This article breaks down what the duopoly means for your restaurant, how commission structures have shifted, when you should build your own delivery channel, and how to protect your margins in a market where two platforms hold all the leverage.

## The State of Thailand's Delivery Market in 2026

Thailand's food delivery market hit approximately 86 billion THB in 2025, up from 79 billion THB in 2024. Growth has slowed from the pandemic years (2020-2022 saw 40-60% annual growth), but the market is still expanding at 8-10% per year. Projections for 2026 put the market at 93-95 billion THB.

**Platform market share (estimated, Q1 2026):**

- **GrabFood:** ~46% (up from ~40% pre-Foodpanda exit, absorbed most of Foodpanda's customer base)
- **LINE MAN Wongnai:** ~40% (gained share through aggressive promotions during the transition period)
- **Direct channels and others:** ~14% (LINE OA ordering, restaurant websites, Robinhood, ShopeeFood remnants)

GrabFood lists over 300,000 merchants in Thailand, and LINE MAN lists over 250,000. There is significant overlap. Most restaurants are on both platforms.

Average order values run 180-220 THB on GrabFood and 160-200 THB on LINE MAN, which skews more toward affordable Thai food. The average Bangkok delivery customer orders 3-4 times per week, with over 70% of orders concentrated in two windows: lunch (11:00 AM-1:30 PM) and dinner (5:30 PM-8:30 PM). Weekend orders are more spread across the day.

<Callout type="info">
After Foodpanda's exit, Grab absorbed approximately 60-65% of Foodpanda's former customer base, while LINE MAN picked up 20-25%. The remaining 10-15% either reduced delivery ordering frequency or moved to direct channels. If you were a Foodpanda-heavy restaurant, your orders likely migrated to Grab by default — but you may have lost 10-15% permanently.
</Callout>

## How Foodpanda's Exit Changed the Power Dynamic

When three platforms compete for restaurant partnerships, you have leverage. Each platform needs your listing to attract customers, and they are willing to offer lower commissions, promotional subsidies, and marketing support to win your business. With three options, you can play them against each other.

With two platforms, the dynamic shifts. Grab and LINE MAN know you need both of them. They know you cannot afford to leave either platform because doing so would cut your delivery revenue by 40-50%. This is a textbook duopoly, and duopolies are bad for suppliers. Your restaurant is the supplier in this equation.

Here is what has changed since the Foodpanda exit.

Both platforms have quietly increased standard commission rates by 1-2 percentage points since mid-2025. The "new merchant" discounted rates that used to last 3-6 months now last 1-3 months before reverting to standard rates.

Promotional subsidies have also dropped. During the three-platform era, Foodpanda was the most aggressive subsidiser, burning cash on free delivery and deep discounts to win market share. That competitive pressure forced Grab and LINE MAN to match. Without Foodpanda, both platforms have pulled back on restaurant-facing promotions. Customer-facing promotions still exist, but the platform contribution has shrunk.

Small operators have lost the most leverage. When Foodpanda existed, a restaurant doing 30 orders per day could credibly threaten to move volume to Foodpanda if Grab did not offer better terms. That threat is gone. Only high-volume operators (100+ orders per day) have meaningful negotiating power now.

And advertising costs have climbed. With fewer organic discovery channels (two platforms instead of three), the competition for visibility within each platform has intensified. Sponsored listing costs on GrabFood have increased 15-25% since mid-2025.

## Commission Structures: Grab vs LINE MAN

Understanding the commission structure is not optional. It is the single biggest factor determining whether delivery is profitable for your restaurant or a money-losing customer acquisition channel.

### GrabFood Commission

**Standard commission:** 30-35% of the order subtotal (before delivery fee).

- 30% is the standard rate for new merchants signing up today.
- 35% applies if you opt into GrabFood's "preferred partner" program, which gives you higher visibility in search results and priority placement during promotions.
- Large chains (50+ outlets) can negotiate down to 25-28%.

**Self-delivery option:** If you provide your own delivery riders, GrabFood's commission drops to 15-18%. You handle the logistics, they handle the platform and payment processing. This only makes sense if you have reliable riders and a tight delivery radius (under 3 km).

**Payment terms:** Weekly settlement, every Monday for the previous week's orders. GrabFood deducts commission, promotions, and any advertising charges before paying you.

**Advertising:** Sponsored search placement costs 3-8 THB per click (varies by location and competition). Banner ads and homepage placement are available for larger budgets — typically 15,000-50,000 THB per campaign.

### LINE MAN Wongnai Commission

**Standard commission:** 25-30% of the order subtotal.

- 25% is available for restaurants that maintain high ratings (4.5+) and consistent order volumes.
- 30% is the standard rate for most merchants.
- LINE MAN has been more flexible than Grab on commission for SMEs, especially outside Bangkok.

**Self-delivery option:** Commission drops to 12-15% with self-delivery. LINE MAN has a wider self-delivery adoption rate than Grab because of its integration with LINE messaging — customers can order through LINE OA and the restaurant can manage delivery directly.

**Payment terms:** Weekly settlement, similar to Grab.

**Advertising:** Wongnai-integrated advertising starts at 2-6 THB per click. LINE MAN also offers promotions through the LINE app ecosystem, which can be effective because LINE has 54 million active users in Thailand (roughly 75% of the population).

<Callout type="tip">
LINE MAN's lower standard commission (25-30% vs Grab's 30-35%) does not automatically make it the better platform. Grab has a larger customer base and higher average order values. The net revenue per order may be similar or even favour Grab despite the higher commission. Calculate your actual net revenue on each platform before deciding where to focus your marketing spend.
</Callout>

## How to Negotiate With Platforms

You are not powerless in this duopoly, but your leverage depends on your volume. Here is how to negotiate with Grab and LINE MAN based on your order volume:

### Under 30 Orders Per Day

You have minimal negotiating leverage. Both platforms offer standard terms and are unlikely to customize for a low-volume merchant.

Your best move is to maximize the new merchant promotion window. Both platforms offer reduced commission (typically 20-25%) for the first 1-3 months, so push your marketing hard during that period. Focus on maintaining high ratings, too. LINE MAN in particular offers better commission tiers for restaurants rated 4.5+, and a consistent 4.7+ can save you 2-3 percentage points on commission. Finally, participate in every co-funded promotion that makes financial sense. Both Grab and LINE MAN periodically offer these, with the platform covering 30-50% of the discount.

### 30-100 Orders Per Day

You have moderate leverage. At this volume, you are a meaningful merchant and losing your listing would be noticeable.

At 50+ orders per day, both platforms assign dedicated account managers. Build this relationship. They can offer ad credits, promotional support, and occasionally commission adjustments. When your initial contract period ends (typically 12 months), use the renewal as an opportunity to renegotiate. Present your volume data and ask for 2-3 percentage points off the standard rate.

Some platforms offer reduced commission (2-5 percentage points lower) in exchange for exclusivity. I generally advise against full exclusivity because it cuts your customer reach. But a "preferred platform" arrangement where you agree to run 70% of your promotions on one platform can yield useful concessions.

### 100+ Orders Per Day

You have real leverage. At this volume, you are in the top 5-10% of merchants, and losing your listing would visibly impact the platform's coverage in your area.

Push commission to 25-28%. High-volume operators can get below the standard rate on both platforms. Come with data: your order volume, average order value, customer retention rate, and rating. Demand marketing support alongside the rate cut, including free sponsored listing credits, homepage placement during your promotional periods, and co-funded delivery fee subsidies.

You should also negotiate payment terms. Instead of weekly settlement, push for faster payouts (bi-weekly or even daily for very high volume). Cash flow matters. And if you have multiple locations, negotiate a group rate across all outlets. The combined volume gives you significantly more leverage.

<Callout type="warning">
Never make threats you cannot follow through on. Telling Grab "I will leave your platform" when you do 80% of your delivery volume through them is not credible. They know you will not leave. Instead, focus on what you can offer them — higher order volume, better ratings, promotional participation — in exchange for better terms. Frame negotiations as partnerships, not ultimatums.
</Callout>

## Menu Strategy for Delivery

Your delivery menu should not be a copy of your dine-in menu with the same prices. Delivery has a different cost structure, different customer expectations, and different food quality constraints.

### Price Padding (Yes, You Should Do It)

Most successful delivery restaurants price their delivery menu 15-25% higher than their dine-in menu. Both Grab and LINE MAN allow you to set different prices on their platforms than what you charge in-house. This is not deceptive. Delivery has real additional costs (packaging, platform commission, longer prep-to-consumption time) that justify the price difference.

A pad thai that is 160 THB dine-in becomes 190-200 THB on delivery. The customer is already paying a delivery fee and is accustomed to delivery prices being slightly higher. The psychological threshold for most Thai delivery customers is around 200-250 THB per person. Price your items to land comfortably within this range.

### Delivery-Only Items

Create 2-3 items that exist only on your delivery menu. Price them specifically for the platform commission structure since they have no dine-in reference price. Design them to travel well from the start: rice bowls, sealed curries, wrapped items. And make at least one a bundle. "Delivery Set A: Green curry + rice + Thai iced tea, 249 THB" increases average order value and gives you a blended margin higher than individual items.

I have seen delivery-only bundles increase average order value by 25-35% compared to a la carte ordering. The key is pricing the bundle at a perceived discount (10-15% below the sum of individual items) while maintaining a 60%+ contribution margin after commission.

### Items to Remove From Your Delivery Menu

Not everything belongs on a delivery platform. Cut any item with food cost above 35%, because after commission you are losing money on every order. Remove anything that does not travel well: crispy items that go soggy, dishes that need immediate plating, anything temperature-sensitive that degrades in 20-30 minutes. Drop complex items that slow down the kitchen during peak delivery hours, and very low-priced items (under 100 THB) that do not justify the kitchen time and packaging cost.

For a detailed framework on which items to keep, reprice, or remove, see our guide on [menu engineering for Thai restaurants](/blog/menu-engineering-thailand).

### Packaging That Protects Your Margins

Packaging is your storefront on delivery. Bad packaging generates negative reviews, which tank your platform ranking, which reduces your orders. Good packaging costs 12-18 THB per order. Great packaging costs 18-25 THB per order.

Start with leak-proof containers for curries and soups. Not the flimsy snap-lid kind; invest in proper sealed containers with rubber gaskets. Use separate sauce containers for dishes that should not be sauced until eating (pad thai sauce, salad dressing, dipping sauces). For hot items, use insulated bags or at minimum foil-lined containers that retain heat.

On the branding side, custom-printed containers are ideal if your volume justifies the minimum order (typically 1,000-2,000 units). At minimum, use branded stickers. And put a review card inside every order: "Enjoying your meal? Rate us 5 stars on [platform]!" It costs 0.50 THB per card and pays for itself many times over in maintaining your rating.

<Callout type="tip">
Rice should always be packaged separately from curry or stir-fry dishes. This seems obvious, but I audit cloud kitchens where staff dump the rice and curry into one container "to save packaging cost." The rice becomes mushy in transit, the customer leaves a bad review, and your rating drops. The 3 THB for a separate rice container is the best 3 THB you will ever spend.
</Callout>

## Cloud Kitchen Integration: Delivery-Only Brands

The Foodpanda exit made cloud kitchens slightly more complicated and slightly more important at the same time.

More complicated because you now have fewer platforms to distribute across. A cloud kitchen that was running three brands across three platforms had nine total listings. With two platforms, it drops to six. That is a 33% reduction in platform visibility.

More important because the delivery market is still growing, margins on platforms are tightening, and the multi-brand cloud kitchen model remains the best delivery strategy for generating profit at scale.

If you are running or considering a cloud kitchen, here is what changes in the duopoly era.

You need to be strong on both platforms. A cloud kitchen that dominates on Grab but is invisible on LINE MAN is leaving 40% of the market untouched. Invest in advertising and promotions on both.

Differentiate your brands more aggressively, too. With fewer platform listings, each brand needs to work harder. Distinct cuisine types, distinct price points, distinct visual branding. Two brands that look similar will cannibalize each other. If Brand A is a Thai rice box concept, Brand B should be a burger concept, not a Thai noodle concept.

And consider adding a direct ordering channel as your third "platform." More on this below.

## When to Build Your Own Delivery Channel

The big question in the duopoly era is whether you should build a direct ordering channel to reduce platform dependency.

The answer depends on your volume, your brand strength, and your willingness to invest in marketing.

### Option 1: LINE Official Account (LINE OA) Ordering

LINE OA is Thailand's most accessible direct ordering channel. Over 54 million Thais use LINE daily, and LINE OA integrates ordering, payment, and customer communication in one place.

**How it works:** You set up a LINE OA for your restaurant, build a menu using LINE's rich menu feature or integrate a third-party ordering system (FoodStory, Ocha, or a custom Liff app), and customers order by messaging your LINE OA.

**Costs:**
- LINE OA account: Free (basic) or 1,200-4,000 THB/month (upgraded for more broadcasts and features)
- Ordering system integration: 2,000-10,000 THB/month depending on the provider
- Delivery logistics: You need your own riders (15,000-20,000 THB per rider per month) or use a third-party logistics service like Lalamove (40-80 THB per delivery)

**Commission equivalent:** Depending on your setup, the effective "commission" on a LINE OA order is 5-15% (delivery logistics cost + ordering system fees). Compare that to 25-35% on platforms.

**The catch:** You have to drive your own traffic. Grab and LINE MAN have millions of active users who browse and discover restaurants. Your LINE OA only reaches people who already know about you and have added your account. This means marketing spend — social media ads, in-store LINE OA promotions, QR codes on every piece of packaging, and ongoing engagement to keep customers ordering directly.

### Option 2: Own Website Ordering

A branded ordering website gives you full control over the customer experience, branding, and data. You own the customer relationship entirely.

**Costs:**
- Website development: 30,000-150,000 THB (one-time, depending on complexity)
- Hosting and payment processing: 1,000-3,000 THB/month
- Delivery logistics: Same as LINE OA — own riders or third-party
- Marketing: 10,000-30,000 THB/month in ads to drive traffic

**Commission equivalent:** 3-10% (payment processing fees + delivery logistics).

**The catch:** Even harder to drive traffic than LINE OA. Consumers in Thailand default to Grab or LINE MAN for food delivery. Convincing them to visit your website, create an account, and order directly requires a real incentive: a 10-15% discount on direct orders, a loyalty program, or exclusive menu items.

### Break-Even Analysis: When Does Direct Delivery Beat Platforms?

Here is the math on when building your own channel makes financial sense.

**Assumptions:**
- Average order value: 200 THB
- Platform commission: 30% (60 THB per order)
- Direct channel cost: 12% all-in (24 THB per order — delivery logistics, payment processing, ordering system)
- Direct channel marketing cost: 25,000 THB/month to maintain customer acquisition
- Margin improvement per direct order: 36 THB (60 THB platform commission minus 24 THB direct cost)

**Break-even:** 25,000 THB / 36 THB per order = 694 direct orders per month, or roughly 23 orders per day.

If you can shift 23 or more orders per day from platforms to your direct channel, the investment in direct ordering pays for itself. Every order above 23 per day is pure margin improvement.

For a restaurant currently doing 80+ delivery orders per day, shifting 25-30% of that volume to direct channels is realistic with consistent marketing. That is 20-24 direct orders per day — right at the break-even point — with upside as direct channel awareness grows.

For a restaurant doing under 50 delivery orders per day, the math is harder. You would need to shift nearly half your volume to direct channels, which requires significant brand strength and marketing investment. In this case, it may be better to focus on optimizing your platform presence rather than building a separate channel.

<Callout type="info">
The best direct ordering strategy for most Thai restaurants is not to replace platforms but to layer on top of them. Keep Grab and LINE MAN for discovery and new customer acquisition. Use your LINE OA and repeat-customer marketing to shift returning customers to direct ordering. Over time, a healthy split is 60-70% platform, 30-40% direct. This gives you platform reach while building a direct relationship with your most valuable customers.
</Callout>

## Operational Optimization for Delivery

Delivery speed is one of the top three factors in your platform ranking, alongside rating and order volume. Slow delivery times push you down in search results, reduce order volume, and create a negative spiral. Here is how to optimize.

### Dedicated Prep Station

If you are doing more than 30 delivery orders per day, set up a dedicated delivery prep and packaging station in your kitchen. This station should have:

- Its own counter space for packing
- Packaging supplies organized by order type (single, double, family)
- A tablet or printer for delivery order tickets, separate from dine-in tickets
- Heat lamps or a holding station for completed orders waiting for riders

Mixing delivery prep with dine-in service creates chaos during peak hours. Delivery orders come in continuously. Dine-in orders come in waves. When they collide at 12:30 PM, something suffers. A dedicated station keeps delivery flowing without disrupting dine-in service.

### Speed Benchmarks

**Target times:**
- Order acceptance to kitchen start: under 2 minutes
- Kitchen start to food ready: under 10 minutes (most items)
- Food ready to rider pickup: under 5 minutes
- Total time from order to dispatch: under 15 minutes

Both Grab and LINE MAN track your average preparation time. Restaurants that consistently prepare orders in under 12 minutes get a "fast delivery" badge that increases organic visibility by an estimated 15-20%.

### Peak Hour Management

70% of your delivery orders will come during two 2.5-hour windows: 11:00 AM-1:30 PM and 5:30 PM-8:00 PM. Your kitchen must be staffed and prepped for this volume.

**Pre-shift prep for delivery:**
- Pre-portion proteins and toppings for delivery items
- Pre-fill sauce containers
- Pre-stage packaging for the top 5 most-ordered items
- Check tablet/printer connectivity and paper supply
- Brief the delivery station cook on the day's promotions (which items will see higher volume)

**During peak:**
- Assign one person exclusively to delivery packaging and rider handoff
- Use a "first in, first out" queue for delivery orders — do not let dine-in orders queue-jump
- If delivery order volume exceeds kitchen capacity, temporarily increase estimated preparation time on the platform rather than accepting orders you cannot fulfil on time. A 20-minute prep time is better than a 10-minute estimate that you consistently miss.

### Rider Waiting Area

This is a small detail that makes a big difference. Designate a rider pickup area near your kitchen: a covered spot with a bench, phone charging, and clear signage. Riders who can find your restaurant quickly and pick up orders efficiently will favour your restaurant over competitors. Both Grab and LINE MAN riders have some discretion in which orders they accept, and they prefer restaurants with fast, easy pickups.

<Callout type="tip">
Put your restaurant's name and order number display in a visible spot near the entrance. Riders who have to call you, wait at the wrong door, or navigate a confusing building add 3-5 minutes to every delivery. At scale, this costs you thousands of orders per year in slower delivery times and lower platform rankings.
</Callout>

## Advertising Strategy on Platforms

With only two platforms, advertising on both matters more than it used to. Here is how to allocate your budget.

### GrabFood Advertising

**Sponsored search:** Your restaurant appears at the top of search results for relevant keywords. Cost: 3-8 THB per click. Conversion rate (click to order): 8-15%. Effective cost per order from ads: 25-80 THB.

**Super Brand Day / Mega Promotions:** Grab periodically runs large-scale promotional events (similar to Shopee's sales events). Participating costs 5,000-20,000 THB but gives you massive visibility. The ROI depends on your promotion depth — a 20% discount during a Mega Promotion can generate 3-5x your normal daily volume.

**Recommended budget:** 15,000-30,000 THB/month for restaurants doing 50+ delivery orders per day. Scale back to 8,000-15,000 THB/month once you have a stable order volume and 4.5+ rating.

### LINE MAN Wongnai Advertising

**Wongnai integration:** LINE MAN's advertising leverages Wongnai, Thailand's largest restaurant review platform. Your restaurant's Wongnai profile drives discovery on LINE MAN. Investing in your Wongnai profile (complete information, responding to reviews, updated photos) is free advertising.

**Sponsored placement:** Similar to GrabFood, cost: 2-6 THB per click. Generally cheaper than Grab due to lower competition for ad inventory.

**LINE ads:** Because LINE MAN is integrated with the LINE ecosystem, you can run ads through LINE Ads Platform that target delivery-intent users. This is more advanced and requires a larger budget (minimum 10,000 THB/campaign), but can be effective for driving direct LINE OA orders alongside LINE MAN orders.

**Recommended budget:** 10,000-25,000 THB/month.

## What This Means for Different Restaurant Types

### Casual Thai Restaurants (80-200 THB Average Order)

You are the core of the delivery market. Most delivery orders in Thailand are in this price range. The duopoly affects you most because platform commission eats a larger percentage of your already-thin margins.

Focus on delivery-specific menu engineering: higher-margin items, bundles, delivery-only sets. Price your delivery menu 15-20% above dine-in. Invest in LINE OA for repeat customers, and target 30% of delivery volume through direct channels within 12 months.

### Fine Dining and Upscale (500+ THB Average Order)

Delivery is not your primary channel and should not be. Your customers want the experience, not just the food. But a carefully curated delivery menu (5-8 premium items, 400-600 THB per order) can generate incremental revenue from customers who already know your brand.

Be on platforms primarily for brand visibility, not volume. Offer a limited premium delivery menu. Use your own delivery riders for quality control, and price aggressively. Your customers are less price-sensitive on delivery. For more on fine dining strategy, see our [fine dining restaurant guide](/blog/fine-dining-bangkok).

### Cloud Kitchens (Delivery-Only)

The duopoly hits cloud kitchens hardest. You have no dine-in revenue to fall back on, and platform commission is your single largest cost.

Run multiple brands across both platforms (minimum two brands, ideally three). Negotiate commission hard. Your volume justifies it. Build a direct channel aggressively, and consider joining a shared kitchen that has negotiated group commission rates with platforms. See our [cloud kitchen guide](/blog/cloud-kitchen-bangkok) for the full strategy.

### New Restaurants (Pre-Opening or Just Opened)

You have the least leverage but the most opportunity to set up your delivery strategy correctly from day one.

Launch on both platforms simultaneously and take advantage of new merchant commission discounts. Invest in food photography (10,000-20,000 THB) and platform advertising during months 1-3, when the algorithm gives new listings a visibility boost. Set up your LINE OA from day one and include a LINE QR code in every delivery package. For the complete opening guide, see [how to open a restaurant in Thailand](/blog/how-to-open-restaurant-thailand).

## The Future of Delivery in Thailand

Here is where I think the market is headed over the next 2-3 years.

Commission rates will continue climbing. Without a third competitor, Grab and LINE MAN have limited incentive to compete on commission. Expect standard rates to reach 33-38% within 2 years unless a new entrant disrupts the market.

A third player may emerge. ShopeeFood briefly entered Thailand and withdrew. TikTok has launched food delivery in Indonesia and Vietnam, and a TikTok-integrated food delivery service in Thailand is not a matter of if, but when. This would break the duopoly and restore some competitive pressure on commission rates.

Direct channels will grow as restaurants become more sophisticated about LINE OA ordering and as delivery logistics services become cheaper and more reliable. I expect the direct channel share to grow from ~14% to 20-25% within 3 years.

Subscription models are expanding, too. Both Grab and LINE MAN are pushing subscription memberships (GrabUnlimited, LINE MAN VIP) that give consumers free delivery for a monthly fee. This shifts delivery costs from the consumer to a shared model between the platform and the restaurant. Expect more pressure on restaurants to subsidize "free delivery" promotions as part of these programs.

Both platforms are also investing in AI-driven personalized restaurant recommendations. Your platform ranking will increasingly depend on whether the algorithm thinks a specific customer will enjoy your food, not just your overall rating and volume. This makes data (order history, customer ratings, reorder rates) more important than ever.

<Callout type="info">
The restaurant owners who will thrive in the duopoly era are those who treat delivery as a distinct business channel with its own P&L, its own menu, its own marketing budget, and its own operational workflow. Restaurants that bolt delivery onto their dine-in operation as an afterthought will see margin erosion accelerate as platform costs rise. The restaurants that build a parallel delivery operation — optimized for commission structures, packaging quality, and platform algorithms — will generate meaningful profit from the channel.
</Callout>

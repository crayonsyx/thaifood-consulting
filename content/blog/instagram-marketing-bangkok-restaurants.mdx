A client of mine opened a modern Thai restaurant in Ekkamai last year. Good food, solid location, reasonable prices. After two months, weekday dinner covers were averaging 18 people in a 50-seat restaurant. Revenue was 40% below projections. She asked me to look at her marketing.

Her Instagram had 287 followers and 14 posts -- all photographed under the restaurant's warm-toned pendant lights, which turned every plate of food orange-brown. The bio said "Thai food with a twist." No location, no booking link, no phone number.

We rebuilt her Instagram from scratch. Hired a food photographer for a single session (12,000 THB), established a content calendar, started posting five times a week with a proper hashtag strategy, and invited 15 micro-influencers over the first month. Within 90 days, she had 4,200 followers, weekday covers were averaging 38, and weekend tables were fully booked by Thursday. Total marketing spend for those 90 days: 67,000 THB.

Instagram is not a nice-to-have for Bangkok restaurants. It is the primary discovery platform for the city's dining scene, and the gap between restaurants that use it well and restaurants that ignore it is measurable in revenue.

This is the complete playbook -- the same framework I give to my clients, with real numbers and real examples from the Bangkok market.

## Why Instagram Dominates Restaurant Discovery in Bangkok

Bangkok is one of the most Instagram-active cities in the world. Thailand ranks 8th globally in Instagram users, with over 22 million active accounts. Bangkok alone accounts for roughly 40% of those users. And food is the single most popular content category among Thai Instagram users -- more than travel, fashion, or fitness.

For restaurants, this creates a specific dynamic: your potential customers are already on Instagram, already looking at food content, and already making dining decisions based on what they see. A 2025 survey by the Digital Economy Promotion Agency found that 72% of Bangkok residents aged 18-35 chose a restaurant in the past month based on an Instagram post or Story they saw.

That is not a marketing trend. That is a distribution channel.

Here is what this means in practice:

**Your Instagram feed is your storefront.** A Bangkok diner's first impression of your restaurant is almost never your physical exterior. It is your Instagram grid. They scroll through your recent posts, check the vibe, look at the food, and decide whether they want to visit -- all in 5-10 seconds. If your feed looks amateur, inconsistent, or inactive, they move on to the next restaurant.

**Instagram drives both discovery and validation.** Thai diners use Instagram in two ways: they discover new restaurants by scrolling Explore and hashtag feeds, and they validate restaurants they have heard about by checking the restaurant's profile. You need to win at both.

**The algorithm rewards restaurants.** Instagram's algorithm favors content that generates saves and shares, and food content does both at high rates. A well-photographed dish gets saved by people planning future meals. A beautiful restaurant interior gets shared in group chats as a suggestion for Friday dinner. This means restaurant content has a natural algorithmic advantage -- if the quality is there.

## Setting Up Your Profile for Conversion

Before posting a single photo, your profile needs to convert visitors into customers. This is where most Bangkok restaurants lose people immediately.

### Profile Optimization Checklist

**Username:** Use your restaurant name. Keep it simple, no underscores or numbers if possible. If your restaurant name is taken, add "bkk" (e.g., @restaurantname.bkk).

**Bio:** You have 150 characters to communicate three things:
1. What you are (cuisine type and concept)
2. Where you are (neighborhood, not full address)
3. How to act (book, visit, order)

Bad bio: "Thai food with a twist. We love food. Come eat with us!"
Good bio: "Modern Thai sharing plates. Ekkamai Soi 10. Book via LINE @restaurantname or walk in."

**Link in bio:** Use a link aggregator (Linktree or Milkshake -- both free) that points to:
- Your LINE Official Account QR code
- Google Maps directions
- Your delivery platform pages (Grab, LINE MAN)
- Your menu (PDF or website)
- Reservation system (if applicable)

**Contact buttons:** Enable the "Call" and "Directions" action buttons. These reduce friction for customers who want to act immediately.

**Category:** Set your business category to "Restaurant" or the most specific subcategory available.

**Highlights:** Create Story Highlights for:
- **Menu** -- your current menu, photographed clearly
- **How to find us** -- directions from nearest BTS/MRT, parking info
- **Reviews** -- screenshots of positive Wongnai and Google reviews, customer testimonials
- **Events** -- upcoming events, special menus, collaborations
- **Team** -- introduce your chef and key staff

<Callout type="tip">
A common mistake: putting your full address in the bio. Nobody memorizes an address from Instagram. Put your neighborhood name and use the Google Maps link for the actual address. "Thonglor Soi 13" or "Near BTS Ekkamai" tells people everything they need at this stage.
</Callout>

## Content Pillars: What to Post

Random posting kills your Instagram growth. You need a content framework -- a set of recurring content types that balance discovery, engagement, and conversion. Here are the four content pillars I recommend for Bangkok restaurants.

### Pillar 1: Food Photography (40-50% of Posts)

This is your core content. Beautiful, appetizing images and videos of your food. Every post in this pillar should make someone hungry.

**What to shoot:**
- **Hero dishes** -- your 3-5 signature items, photographed from the most flattering angle with professional lighting. These are the posts that get saved and shared.
- **New menu items** -- whenever you add or change a dish, it deserves a dedicated post with a compelling description.
- **Seasonal specials** -- limited-time items create urgency and give followers a reason to visit now.
- **Close-up details** -- the crispy skin on a pork belly, the bubbling cheese on a pizza, the steam rising from a soup. Texture and detail trigger hunger.

**Photography guidelines:**
- **Natural light is king.** Shoot during daylight hours near a window. The single biggest improvement most restaurant Instagram accounts can make is switching from artificial to natural light.
- **Overhead (flat lay) works for shared plates.** Thai food, which is typically served communally, photographs well from above. Arrange 3-5 dishes together on the table for a "spread" shot.
- **45-degree angle for height.** Burgers, cakes, tall cocktails, anything with vertical interest -- shoot from a 45-degree angle.
- **Minimal props.** The food is the star. A clean table surface, a simple napkin, maybe a hand reaching for the plate. Do not clutter the frame.
- **Consistent editing.** Pick a filter or editing preset and use it on every post. This creates a cohesive grid that looks professional. VSCO and Lightroom offer food-specific presets.

**Budget for food photography:**
- Professional session (20-30 edited images): 8,000-25,000 THB
- Frequency: one session per quarter, plus phone shots for daily content
- Monthly cost if outsourced to a content creator: 15,000-35,000 THB

### Pillar 2: Behind-the-Scenes (20-25% of Posts)

Behind-the-scenes content builds emotional connection and trust. It shows the craft, the people, and the process behind the food -- which is exactly what differentiates your restaurant from the thousands of others in Bangkok.

**What to capture:**
- **Kitchen prep** -- a chef hand-pulling noodles, butchering a fish, assembling a tasting plate. Movement and skill are inherently interesting on video.
- **Ingredient sourcing** -- trips to Klong Toei market, deliveries from local farms, a crate of fresh herbs. This tells the quality story without you having to say "we use fresh ingredients."
- **Recipe development** -- the chef tasting a new dish, testing different plating, scribbling notes. The creative process is compelling content.
- **Morning prep rituals** -- the first coffee, the stove igniting, the team arriving. These create a rhythm that followers look forward to.

**Format:** Behind-the-scenes works best as short-form video (Reels or Stories) rather than static photos. A 15-second clip of a wok toss performs better than a photo of a wok.

### Pillar 3: Atmosphere and Design (15-20% of Posts)

People eat with their eyes first, and they choose restaurants partly based on ambiance. Your atmosphere content sells the experience, not just the food.

**What to capture:**
- **Interior wide shots** -- show the full dining room, the bar, the patio. Capture the lighting mood at dinner service.
- **Design details** -- interesting wall art, custom ceramics, a unique light fixture, handcrafted menus. These details signal thoughtfulness.
- **Table settings** -- a beautifully set table with glassware, napkins, and candles ready for service. This creates aspiration.
- **Outdoor areas** -- if you have a terrace, rooftop, or garden, this is premium content in Bangkok. Outdoor dining spaces are scarce and highly sought after.
- **Seasonal decorations** -- Chinese New Year, Loy Krathong, Christmas. Seasonal refreshes give you a reason to reshoot your space.

<Callout type="info">
I will cover Instagram-worthy design elements later in this article, but the principle is simple: design decisions you make during your build-out directly affect your Instagram marketing performance for years. A restaurant that is easy to photograph generates free content from every customer who visits.
</Callout>

### Pillar 4: Community and Customer Content (10-15% of Posts)

This pillar builds social proof and creates a sense of belonging. It shows that real people eat at your restaurant and enjoy themselves.

**What to post:**
- **User-generated content (UGC)** -- repost customer photos and Stories that tag your restaurant or use your hashtag. Always credit the original poster with a tag and a thank-you in the caption.
- **Group celebrations** -- birthdays, anniversaries, corporate dinners. With permission, photograph groups enjoying themselves. This signals that your restaurant is a place for occasions.
- **Staff spotlights** -- introduce your team members by name, share their food recommendations, or capture them laughing during prep. This humanizes your restaurant.
- **Customer testimonials** -- screenshot positive reviews (with the reviewer's name visible) and share them as Stories or feed posts.

**UGC reposting etiquette in Thailand:** Always ask permission via DM before reposting someone's content to your feed (Stories reposts of tagged content are generally considered acceptable without asking). A quick "Hi! We love your photo -- would you mind if we shared it on our feed?" is sufficient. Thai users are almost always flattered and say yes.

## Posting Frequency and Optimal Timing

Consistency matters more than volume, but volume matters more than most Bangkok restaurants think.

### Recommended Posting Frequency

**Feed posts:** 4-5 per week minimum. Daily is ideal for the first 6 months while building your following. After you have established a base (2,000+ followers), 4-5 per week is sustainable.

**Stories:** Daily. Post 3-8 Stories per day. Behind-the-scenes clips, daily specials, customer reposts, polls, and casual content. Stories expire in 24 hours, which lowers the quality bar -- you can post casual, unpolished content here.

**Reels:** 2-3 per week. This is the highest-priority format for growth. Reels reach non-followers through the Explore page and Reels tab, making them your primary discovery tool.

### Optimal Posting Times for Bangkok

Based on aggregate data from restaurant accounts I manage and general Thai Instagram usage patterns:

**Weekday feed posts:**
- **11:00-12:30** -- lunch hour browsing, catches the "where should I eat?" decision
- **18:00-19:30** -- dinner decision window, the best single time slot for restaurant content
- **21:00-22:00** -- evening scrolling, plants the seed for a future visit

**Weekend feed posts:**
- **10:00-11:00** -- brunch planning
- **16:00-17:30** -- dinner planning for the evening

**Stories:** Post throughout the day. Morning prep at 8:00-9:00, lunch rush at 12:00, afternoon prep at 15:00-16:00, dinner service at 19:00-20:00. Stories create a narrative arc of your restaurant's day that followers engage with passively.

**Reels:** Post between 11:00-13:00 or 18:00-20:00 for maximum initial engagement. The first 30-60 minutes of engagement on a Reel determine how widely the algorithm distributes it.

<Callout type="warning">
Do not post and disappear. The Instagram algorithm rewards accounts that stay active after posting. After publishing a feed post or Reel, spend 15-20 minutes engaging -- reply to comments, respond to Stories, visit and like posts from accounts in your community. This signals to the algorithm that you are an active participant, not just a broadcaster.
</Callout>

## Reels vs. Stories vs. Feed: Strategy by Format

Each Instagram format serves a different purpose. Using them interchangeably is a common mistake.

### Feed Posts: Your Portfolio

Feed posts are permanent. They form your grid, which is the first thing potential customers see when they visit your profile. Treat feed posts like a curated portfolio.

**Best for:** Hero dish photography, interior shots, team portraits, milestone announcements, polished content that represents your restaurant at its best.

**Production level:** High. Professional photography or carefully composed phone shots with consistent editing.

**Caption strategy:** Feed captions should be informative and include a call to action. Describe the dish, share the story behind it, tell people how to book or visit. Captions of 100-200 words perform well -- long enough to provide value, short enough that people finish reading.

### Reels: Your Growth Engine

Reels reach non-followers. This is the format that grows your audience. A feed post might reach 10-20% of your existing followers. A Reel can reach 10 times your follower count if it performs well.

**Best for:** Process videos (cooking, plating, cocktail-making), restaurant tours, "day in the life" content, trending audio clips overlaid on food footage, before-and-after transformations (raw ingredients to finished plate).

**Production level:** Medium. Reels do not need to be polished -- they need to be engaging in the first 1-2 seconds. Phone-shot vertical video with natural sound or trending audio works well. Over-produced Reels actually perform worse because they feel like ads.

**What makes a Reel perform in Bangkok:**
- **Hook in the first second.** Start with the most visually interesting moment, a flame, a pour, a sizzle, a knife cutting through crispy skin. Do not start with a title card or your logo.
- **Short and punchy.** 7-15 seconds is the sweet spot for food Reels. Longer Reels (30-60 seconds) work for tutorials or restaurant tours but need strong pacing.
- **Trending audio.** Use trending sounds from the Thai Instagram ecosystem. Check the Reels tab weekly for popular audio clips and adapt them to your content.
- **Text overlay in Thai and English.** Bangkok's Instagram audience is bilingual. Include short text overlays in both languages to maximize reach.

### Stories: Your Daily Conversation

Stories are ephemeral, casual, and interactive. They maintain your connection with existing followers between feed posts.

**Best for:** Daily specials, real-time kitchen activity, customer reposts, polls ("Which new dessert should we add?"), Q&A sessions, countdowns to events, reservation availability updates, behind-the-scenes moments.

**Production level:** Low. Phone shots and quick videos. Stories should feel spontaneous and unfiltered -- that is what makes them engaging.

**Interactive features to use:**
- **Polls** -- "Pad Thai or Pad See Ew tonight?" Easy engagement that also gives you menu data.
- **Question stickers** -- "Ask our chef anything" creates engagement and content ideas.
- **Countdown stickers** -- for upcoming events, new menu launches, or holiday specials.
- **Location stickers** -- always tag your location. This puts your Story in the location feed where nearby users can discover it.
- **Mention stickers** -- tag customers, collaborators, and influencers to encourage resharing.

## Hashtag Strategy for Bangkok Restaurants

Hashtags still drive discovery on Instagram, but the strategy has evolved. Stuffing 30 random hashtags in your caption is outdated and can actually hurt your reach. Here is what works now.

### Hashtag Framework

Use 8-15 hashtags per post, organized into three tiers:

**Tier 1: High-volume Bangkok food hashtags (3-5 per post)**
These have millions of posts and cast the widest net:
- #bangkokfood (5.2M+ posts)
- #bkkfoodie (1.8M+)
- #bangkokrestaurant (1.1M+)
- #thaifood (16M+)
- #bangkokeats (800K+)

**Tier 2: Medium-volume niche hashtags (3-5 per post)**
These have 50,000-500,000 posts and reach more targeted audiences:
- #thonglorfood / #ekkamaifoodies / #aricafe (neighborhood-specific)
- #bangkokfoodie (400K+)
- #eatbangkok (200K+)
- #bangkokfinedining / #bangkokbrunch / #bangkokcafe (concept-specific)
- #whattoeatinbangkok (150K+)
- #bangkokfoodguide (300K+)

**Tier 3: Low-volume specific hashtags (2-5 per post)**
These have under 50,000 posts but attract highly targeted audiences:
- Your branded hashtag (e.g., #restaurantnamebkk) -- create one and use it on every post
- Cuisine-specific (#isanfood, #omakasebangkok, #italianfoodbangkok)
- Occasion-specific (#bangkokdatenight, #bangkokbirthdaydinner)
- Diet-specific (#veganbbk, #glutenfreebangkok)

### Hashtag Mistakes to Avoid

**Using only high-volume hashtags.** Your post will be buried in seconds under millions of other posts. Mix in medium and low-volume tags where you can actually rank.

**Using irrelevant trending hashtags.** Tagging #fitness or #travel on a food post because they are popular does not help. Instagram's algorithm can detect relevance mismatch and may suppress your post.

**Hiding hashtags in comments.** This worked in 2020. It does not work in 2026. Instagram's own guidance says to put hashtags in the caption for best discovery.

**Never changing your hashtags.** Rotate your hashtag sets. Using the exact same 15 hashtags on every post signals low-effort automation to the algorithm. Create 3-4 hashtag sets and rotate them.

## Instagram-Worthy Restaurant Design

This section is for restaurant owners who are still in the build-out phase or planning a renovation. The design decisions you make now will directly impact your Instagram marketing for years. I am not talking about making your restaurant "Instagrammable" in a gimmicky way -- I am talking about design choices that photograph well and generate organic content from every customer who visits.

### Design Elements That Drive Customer Content

**Feature walls.** A single distinctive wall -- neon sign, mural, textured stone, living plant wall, interesting tile pattern -- gives customers a backdrop for photos. Position it near the entrance or in the main dining area. Every customer photo with your feature wall is free advertising with your restaurant's location tagged. Cost: 15,000-80,000 THB depending on complexity.

**Signature plating.** Design 2-3 dishes specifically to photograph well. This does not mean sacrificing taste for aesthetics -- it means choosing serving vessels, garnishes, and arrangements that create visual impact. A smoking cocktail, a dessert with tableside pour, a colorful sharing platter. These become your most-shared items. For fine dining concepts where presentation is everything, see: [How to Open a Fine Dining Restaurant in Bangkok](/blog/fine-dining-bangkok).

**Natural light.** Restaurants with abundant natural light generate better customer photos, which generates more tagged content, which generates more discovery. If your space has windows, do not block them with heavy curtains. If you are choosing between two locations, the one with better natural light will produce better social media content.

**Overhead-friendly table arrangement.** Thai dining is communal -- people order multiple dishes and spread them across the table. If your table surfaces are attractive (wood grain, marble, interesting ceramic tiles), customers will photograph the full spread from above. This flat-lay aesthetic is the single most common restaurant photo format on Bangkok Instagram.

**Contrast.** Dark plates on light tables, colorful food on neutral ceramics, green plants against concrete walls. Contrast creates visual interest in photos. A white plate of white pasta on a white table does not photograph well, no matter how good the food is.

<Callout type="tip">
During your build-out, take test photos of mockup plates on your actual tables under your actual lighting at different times of day. If the photos do not look good, adjust the lighting or table surfaces before you open. This 30-minute exercise saves you from having a beautiful restaurant that photographs poorly.
</Callout>

### Lighting Design for Photography

Restaurant lighting needs to serve ambiance and phone cameras simultaneously. Moody, low-light atmosphere makes dinner feel special but turns phone photos grainy, yellow, and unflattering.

**Solutions:**
- **Daylight-balanced LED spots** above tables improve phone camera results without destroying the ambient mood. Put these on a separate circuit.
- **Lighter wall colors** in the main dining area reflect more light. Deep black or dark brown walls absorb light and make phone photos worse.
- **LED candles or low-profile table lamps** instead of real candles, which create flickering light that looks beautiful in person and terrible in photos.

## Working with Micro-Influencers: The Bangkok Playbook

Micro-influencers (10,000-50,000 followers) are the most cost-effective marketing channel for Bangkok restaurants. The math is straightforward: a micro-influencer with 20,000 engaged local followers generates more qualified foot traffic per baht than a macro-influencer with 500,000 followers spread across the country.

### Finding the Right Influencers

**Search Bangkok food hashtags.** Browse #bangkokfood, #bkkfoodie, and neighborhood-specific hashtags to find active food accounts. Look for accounts where the followers are engaged (comments, questions, saves) rather than just numerous.

**Check follower quality.** An account with 30,000 followers and 50 likes per post has bought followers or has a disengaged audience. Look for 3-5% engagement rate (likes + comments divided by followers). A 15,000-follower account with genuine 5% engagement is worth more than a 50,000-follower account with 0.5%.

**Location relevance.** The influencer should be Bangkok-based and primarily post about Bangkok dining. An influencer based in Chiang Mai with 40,000 followers will drive zero foot traffic to your Thonglor restaurant.

**Content quality.** Review their last 20 posts. Is the photography quality consistent? Do their captions provide useful information (not just "Yummy! Recommend!")? Would you be proud to have their content associated with your restaurant?

**Audience demographics.** Ask potential influencers for their Instagram Insights -- specifically, audience location (should be 60%+ Bangkok) and age range (should align with your target customer). Any professional influencer will share this without hesitation.

### The Invitation Process

**Step 1: Follow and engage.** Follow the influencer, like and comment on their posts genuinely for 1-2 weeks before reaching out. This establishes familiarity.

**Step 2: Send a DM or email.** Keep it concise:

"Hi [name], I am [your name] from [restaurant]. We are a [brief concept description] in [neighborhood]. I have been following your content and think our food would resonate with your audience. We would love to invite you for a complimentary dinner for two. Would you be interested?"

**Step 3: Agree on deliverables.** Before they visit, confirm expectations via message: 1 feed post + 3-5 Stories minimum, tag your restaurant, include location tag, post within 7 days of visit. This does not need to be a formal contract -- a LINE message confirmation is sufficient in the Bangkok influencer community.

**Step 4: Host them well.** When they visit, send your best dishes. Assign your best server. The chef should come out to explain the concept. Make the experience special enough that their genuine reaction translates into compelling content.

**Step 5: Track and follow up.** When they post, engage immediately -- like, comment, share to your Stories. Screenshot the performance data they share. Log everything in a tracking spreadsheet.

## Case Studies: Bangkok Restaurants That Grew Via Instagram

These are drawn from restaurants I have worked with or studied closely. Names and some details are changed where clients prefer privacy, but the strategies and numbers are real.

### Case Study 1: A Modern Thai Restaurant in Ari

**Starting point:** 400 followers, 5-8 covers per weekday dinner, 3 months after opening.

**Strategy:** Hired a part-time content creator (18,000 THB/month) to shoot daily Reels and Stories. Established a content calendar with the four-pillar framework. Invited 20 micro-influencers over 6 weeks. Created a branded hashtag campaign. Redesigned the restaurant's outdoor area with a plant wall specifically as a photo backdrop (cost: 25,000 THB).

**Results after 6 months:**
- Followers: 400 to 8,700
- Average weekday dinner covers: 8 to 34
- Weekend booking rate: 40% to fully booked by Wednesday
- Monthly revenue increase: 480,000 THB
- Total marketing spend over 6 months: 210,000 THB
- ROI: roughly 13:1 on the marketing investment

The plant wall alone generated over 500 tagged customer photos in 6 months. At zero cost per photo, that is thousands of baht in free advertising.

### Case Study 2: A Japanese Omakase in Thonglor

**Starting point:** New opening, zero followers, 8-seat counter.

**Strategy:** Pre-opening Instagram campaign starting 6 weeks before launch -- daily Stories documenting the build-out, teaser Reels of plating technique with no dish fully revealed. Invited 5 mid-tier food influencers (50K-150K followers) for soft launch week, staggering their visits so coverage appeared across 10 consecutive days.

**Results after 3 months:**
- Followers: 0 to 5,400
- Fully booked 2 weeks in advance from month 2
- Monthly revenue: 1,080,000 THB (8 seats x 2 turns x 4,500 THB x 30 days)
- Total marketing spend: 185,000 THB

The staggered influencer strategy was the key decision. Instead of all 5 posting on the same day (one-day spike), spreading coverage across 10 days created the perception of sustained buzz -- each influencer's followers saw their post, then saw another influencer post about the same restaurant two days later, building social proof through repeated exposure.

### Case Study 3: A Cafe Pivot from Low-Quality to Professional Content

**Starting point:** 1,200 followers, flat engagement, 25 average daily customers despite a high-foot-traffic Ekkamai location. All content shot by a staff member under fluorescent lighting with no editing and inconsistent posting.

**Strategy:** Single professional photography session (15,000 THB for 30 images), 3 Lightroom presets (500 THB), a 2-hour staff training on composition and editing, a fixed posting schedule (1 post/day, 5 Stories/day), and a "Latte of the Week" Reel series featuring a new latte art design every Monday.

**Results after 4 months:**
- Followers: 1,200 to 3,800
- Engagement rate: 1.2% to 4.7%
- Average daily customers: 25 to 42
- Investment: 15,500 THB one-time + staff time

The Latte of the Week series averaged 8,000 views per episode (versus 200-400 on previous content), with three episodes exceeding 50,000 views. That is the power of a consistent content series with a clear hook. For more on the cafe business model, see: [How to Open a Cafe in Thailand](/blog/open-cafe-thailand).

## Common Instagram Mistakes Bangkok Restaurants Make

I audit restaurant Instagram accounts regularly as part of my consulting work. These are the patterns I see repeated constantly.

**Inconsistent posting.** Seven posts in one week, then nothing for three weeks. The algorithm punishes gaps. A restaurant that posts 4 times per week every week will outperform one that posts 10 times one week and twice the next.

**Only posting food.** Food photography should be 40-50% of your content, not 100%. A feed that is nothing but plate after plate becomes monotonous. Mix in behind-the-scenes, atmosphere, and community content to create variety and emotional connection.

**Ignoring Reels.** In 2026, Reels generate 2-3 times the reach of static feed posts. A restaurant posting only static images is leaving discovery on the table. Even simple Reels -- a 10-second clip of a dish being plated, shot on a phone -- outperform polished static photos in reach.

**No call to action.** Every post should tell the viewer what to do next. "Book via LINE @restaurantname," "Walk-ins welcome, we are open until 22:00," "Tag someone who needs this right now." Without a CTA, beautiful content generates likes but not visits.

**Bad lighting in photos.** I mentioned this at the start, and I will say it again because it is the single most common technical problem. Yellow, dim, overhead lighting makes food look unappetizing. Shoot during the day near a window, or invest in a small portable LED panel (1,500-3,000 THB) for evening shots.

**Not engaging with the community.** Posting and disappearing. Respond to every comment within 2 hours, engage with nearby restaurants and food bloggers, and share customer Stories. The algorithm rewards engagement, and humans reward attention.

**Buying followers.** I can spot a purchased following in 10 seconds: 15,000 followers, 30 likes per post, zero comments. Fake followers destroy your engagement rate, making the algorithm show your content to fewer real people. It is worse than having no followers at all.

**No Thai-language captions.** If your target audience includes Thai customers, include a Thai translation or summary in your captions. Many Bangkok food Instagram users browse in Thai and skip English-only content.

## Instagram Advertising for Restaurants

Organic reach on Instagram has declined steadily, and paid promotion is now necessary to reach new audiences at scale. The good news: Instagram ads for restaurants can be highly cost-effective because of precise geographic targeting.

### Ad Types That Work for Restaurants

**Boosted Reels.** Take your best-performing organic Reel and boost it with a budget of 500-2,000 THB. Target: ages 18-45, within 5-10 km of your restaurant, interests in food and dining. A well-performing boosted Reel can reach 50,000-200,000 people for 2,000 THB.

**Story ads.** Full-screen vertical ads that appear between users' Stories. These are immersive and swipeable, and they work well for event promotions and limited-time offers. Budget: 3,000-10,000 THB per campaign.

**Local awareness ads.** Target people physically near your restaurant. These are ideal for lunch rush (run ads 10:00-11:30 targeting people within 2 km) or weekend dinner (run ads Thursday-Saturday 15:00-18:00 targeting within 5 km).

### Ad Budget

For most Bangkok restaurants, 5,000-20,000 THB/month in Instagram advertising is sufficient to supplement organic content. During the first 3 months and around major campaigns (grand opening, seasonal events), increase to 15,000-30,000 THB/month.

**Cost benchmarks:**
- Cost per 1,000 impressions (CPM): 30-80 THB in Bangkok
- Cost per click to profile: 3-8 THB
- Cost per click to website/LINE: 5-15 THB

<Callout type="tip">
Never boost underperforming content. Only promote posts and Reels that already showed strong organic performance (higher than your average engagement). The algorithm has already validated that this content resonates -- paid promotion amplifies what is already working.
</Callout>

## Building a Content Calendar

A content calendar removes the daily stress of "what should I post today?" and ensures consistent, balanced output across all four content pillars.

| Tuesday | Atmosphere: Interior detail | -- | 3-5: Customer repost, poll |

This template gives you 5 feed posts, 3 Reels, and 25-40 Stories per week. Adjust based on your capacity, but do not drop below 4 feed posts and 2 Reels per week.

### Content Batching

Dedicate 2-3 hours once a week to photography and video creation. Shoot 10-15 pieces of content in one session, then schedule them throughout the week using Later, Planoly, or Meta Business Suite (free). Batching is more efficient than shooting daily, ensures consistent quality, and means you are not scrambling for content on a busy Friday night when you should be focused on service.

## Measuring Instagram Performance

Track these metrics weekly and review trends monthly:

**Follower growth rate.** Aim for 5-15% monthly growth in the first year. If growth stalls, increase Reels output and experiment with new content formats.

**Engagement rate.** (Likes + comments + saves + shares) divided by followers. Target: 3-5% for a restaurant account. Below 2% means your content is not connecting.

**Reach per post.** Reels should reach 2-5 times your follower count, while feed posts typically reach 15-30% of followers.

**Saves.** The most valuable engagement metric for restaurants -- a save means someone is planning to visit. Track which posts generate the highest saves and create more content like them.

**Profile visits and link clicks.** How many people visit your profile, and how many click through to your LINE, menu, or booking link? These are your closest metrics to conversion.

**Story completion rate.** Below 70% means your Stories are too long or uninteresting. Above 85% is excellent.

Instagram Insights (available on all business and creator accounts) provides all of these metrics for free. Check them every Monday morning as part of your weekly marketing review.

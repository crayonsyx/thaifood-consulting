The worst restaurant opening I have ever witnessed was also the most expensive. A foreign investor spent 18 million THB building a stunning 80-seat Mediterranean restaurant in Sathorn, flew in a chef from Barcelona, and threw a 400,000 THB launch party with imported wine, a DJ, and 200 guests. The food was excellent. The vibe was electric. Every influencer in Bangkok posted about it that weekend.

And then Monday came.

No follow-up marketing plan. No soft opening to stress-test the kitchen. No social media calendar. No delivery platform presence. No LINE Official Account. No review strategy. No press outreach beyond the party itself. The opening weekend buzz evaporated in 10 days, weekday covers dropped to single digits by week three, and the restaurant closed in five months.

He had thrown a party. He had not launched a restaurant.

A restaurant opening marketing plan is not a single event -- it is a 90-day operation that begins months before your first customer walks in and continues well after the opening night confetti is swept up. The restaurants that survive their first year in Bangkok are the ones that treat the launch as a campaign, not a celebration.

This is the complete launch marketing playbook I use with my clients in Thailand, covering everything from the 90-day pre-launch countdown through the first month of operation. If you are planning to [open a restaurant in Thailand](/blog/how-to-open-restaurant-thailand), this is the marketing blueprint that sits alongside your operational and financial planning.

<Callout type="info">
All budget figures are in Thai Baht (THB). At time of writing, 1 USD is approximately 35.5 THB. Budgets assume a mid-range Bangkok restaurant (40-80 seats, casual to upscale casual). Fine dining concepts should increase these figures by 50-100%, while smaller concepts or locations outside Bangkok can typically reduce by 30-50%.
</Callout>

## The 90-Day Pre-Launch Timeline

Your marketing does not start when you open. It starts the day your lease is signed and your build-out begins. The 90 days before opening are when you build the audience that becomes your first wave of customers.

### Days 90-60: Foundation Building

This phase is about establishing your digital presence and laying the groundwork. Nothing flashy, but everything that follows depends on getting this right.

**Set up all digital accounts.** Create your Instagram business account, Facebook page, LINE Official Account, and Google Business Profile. Do not wait until the restaurant is "ready" -- the construction phase is some of the best content you will ever produce. Register on Wongnai as a "Coming Soon" listing if the option is available.

**Define your brand identity.** Before posting anything, lock down your visual identity: logo, color palette, font choices, and photography style. This does not require a branding agency -- a freelance designer can create a restaurant brand kit for 15,000-40,000 THB. What matters is consistency. Every post, every Story, every piece of print material should feel like it comes from the same place.

**Start your Instagram account with construction content.** The build-out of a restaurant is inherently interesting to people who eat at restaurants. Post progress photos, behind-the-scenes Stories of design decisions, time-lapse videos of walls going up, fixtures being installed, and the kitchen taking shape. This "building in public" approach creates early followers who feel invested in your success before they have tasted a single dish.

**Build your media contact list.** Identify 30-50 food writers, bloggers, and media outlets that are relevant to your concept and target audience. For Bangkok, this means BK Magazine, Timeout Bangkok, Wongnai editorial, Thai food bloggers, and English-language food writers. Collect their contact information and social media handles. For a complete list of key media outlets, see my [restaurant marketing guide](/blog/restaurant-marketing-thailand).

**Identify your influencer shortlist.** Research and list 40-60 potential influencer collaborators across tiers: 20-30 nano-influencers (1K-10K followers), 15-20 micro-influencers (10K-50K), and 5-10 mid-tier accounts (50K-200K). Follow all of them, start engaging with their content genuinely, and begin building familiarity before you pitch anything.

### Days 60-30: Building Buzz

By this point, your space is taking shape, your kitchen equipment is being installed, and your menu is being finalized. This is when you shift from background presence to active buzz-building.

**Increase posting frequency.** Move from 2-3 posts per week to daily posts. Mix construction updates with content teasers -- a close-up of a dish being tested in your temporary kitchen, a sneak peek of your menu design, your chef selecting ingredients at the market. Each post should create curiosity without revealing everything.

**Launch a "countdown" campaign.** Start a visible countdown to your opening date. Use Instagram Story countdown stickers, create a series of countdown posts ("30 days until our doors open"), and pin a countdown post to your Instagram grid. This creates urgency and gives followers a specific date to look forward to.

**Send your press kit to media contacts.** Your press kit should include: 5-10 high-resolution food and interior photos (even if they are from menu testing, not the final space), a one-page concept summary, a sample or preview menu, chef biography, owner biography, and contact information. Send this via email 4-6 weeks before opening, with a personal note explaining why this restaurant is worth covering. Timing matters -- food writers plan their calendars weeks in advance.

**Confirm your influencer invitations.** Reach out to your shortlisted influencers with specific dates for media preview dinners and soft opening visits. Stagger their visits across 10-14 days so coverage appears continuously rather than all at once. For your [Instagram marketing strategy](/blog/instagram-marketing-bangkok-restaurants), staggered influencer content creates the perception of sustained buzz.

**Set up delivery platforms.** Register with Grab Food and LINE MAN at least 30 days before opening. The onboarding process -- menu upload, photography requirements, contract signing, tablet setup -- takes 2-3 weeks. You want to be live on delivery platforms within the first week of opening, and the setup process cannot be rushed.

**Plan your soft opening schedule.** Block out 7-10 days before your public grand opening for friends-and-family nights and media preview events. Create guest lists, finalize the menu for these events, and brief your team on the purpose of each night.

<Callout type="tip">
Create an Instagram-specific teaser: post 9 photos that form a single image when viewed as a grid (a "grid reveal"). Use the build-up to post these one by one over 9 days, then reveal the full image as your opening announcement. This is a visual trick that gets people visiting your profile repeatedly and generates buzz in food-focused Instagram communities.
</Callout>

### Days 30-1: Activation

The final month before opening is about converting your built-up audience into booked tables and confirmed media coverage.

**Book your professional photography session.** Schedule this for the first or second day of your soft opening period, before the public opening. You need 30-50 professional images -- hero dishes, cocktails, interior, exterior, team -- that will serve as your visual assets for the next 3-6 months across all platforms. Budget: 15,000-35,000 THB for a food photographer, or 25,000-50,000 THB for a combined food and interior session.

**Finalize your opening promotions.** Decide on the specific offers you will run during your first two weeks. These should be compelling enough to drive action but sustainable enough that you do not lose money on every customer. Good examples:

- **Free welcome drink** with any main course (low cost, high perceived value)
- **15-20% off** for LINE Official Account followers (drives LINE signups)
- **Free dessert for tables of 4+** (encourages group dining, increases average check)
- **Complimentary appetizer** for Google or Wongnai reviewers (drives reviews)

Avoid deep discounts (30%+ off everything) that attract deal-seekers who will never return at full price and set an expectation of discounted pricing.

**Confirm media preview dinner logistics.** Your media dinner should be 5-7 days before the public opening. Finalize the guest list (10-20 food writers, bloggers, and influencers), plan the menu (5-7 courses showcasing your range), arrange beverage pairings, assign a photographer to capture the event, and brief your chef and service team. This is a performance, not just a dinner -- every dish needs to be flawless because these are the people who will shape your public narrative.

**Prepare all print materials.** Table tents with LINE QR code and review links, business cards for the owner and chef, opening promotion flyers for the neighborhood, bill inserts with follow-up prompts ("Thank you for visiting! Follow us on Instagram for updates and offers.").

**Brief your entire team.** Every staff member should know the opening marketing plan, the current promotions, how to ask for reviews, how to point customers to your LINE account, and how to handle the likely operational chaos of the first week. A 30-minute team meeting on this topic prevents hundreds of missed opportunities.

## The Soft Opening: Your Most Underrated Marketing Tool

A soft opening is not a dress rehearsal. It is a controlled marketing activation that serves two simultaneous purposes: stress-testing your operations at reduced capacity, and generating your first wave of content, reviews, and word-of-mouth before the general public arrives.

### Friends and Family Nights (7-5 Days Before Grand Opening)

**Night 1: Inner circle.** 20-30 people -- your personal friends, family, investors, and close contacts. The purpose is to test every system: kitchen timing, service flow, POS transactions, and the overall dining experience. Mistakes will happen, and that is the point. Better to have a 40-minute ticket time with your uncle than with a food critic.

**Night 2-3: Extended network.** 40-60 people each night -- industry friends, neighboring business owners, your contractors and suppliers, and friends of your inner circle. Increase the cover count each night to stress the kitchen progressively. These guests are your first social media ambassadors -- many will post without being asked, and you should encourage it.

**What to offer:** A limited menu (60-70% of your full menu) at no charge or a significant discount. The goal is not revenue -- it is feedback, content, and early word-of-mouth. Budget 3,000-5,000 THB per person in food and beverage cost for these nights.

<Callout type="warning">
Do not skip the soft opening. I have seen owners go straight from construction to grand opening, and it almost always ends badly. Kitchens that have never served 50 covers in a service will have timing breakdowns, servers who have never worked your floor plan will collide, and systems that worked in theory will fail in practice. A soft opening catches these problems when the stakes are low. The cost of 2-3 friends-and-family dinners (50,000-150,000 THB) is a fraction of the cost of a disastrous public opening that generates negative reviews you can never delete.
</Callout>

### Media Preview Dinner (5-3 Days Before Grand Opening)

This is the highest-stakes event in your entire launch sequence. The 10-20 food writers and influencers at this dinner will produce the first public content about your restaurant, and that content shapes how Bangkok perceives you.

**Guest list curation.** Invite a mix of:
- 3-5 food writers from established media (BK, Timeout, Wongnai editorial)
- 5-8 micro-influencers with engaged local food audiences
- 2-3 mid-tier or macro-influencers for broader reach
- 2-3 industry contacts (other chefs, bar owners) whose opinions carry weight in Bangkok's F&B community

**Event structure:**
1. Welcome drinks at the bar (15-20 minutes) -- lets guests settle in and photograph the space
2. Introduction by the owner or chef (5 minutes) -- the story of the restaurant, the concept, what inspired the menu
3. Multi-course tasting menu (5-7 courses over 90 minutes) -- show your range, from appetizers to dessert
4. Chef meet-and-greet (15 minutes) -- the chef comes out, explains key dishes, answers questions
5. Farewell gift (optional but effective) -- a small branded item (custom chopstick set, miniature sauce jar, branded tote bag) that extends the experience beyond the dinner

**Key logistics:**
- Assign a staff member to handle social media during the event (repost Stories, respond to tags)
- Hire a photographer to capture the evening (use these photos for your own marketing)
- Provide a one-page fact sheet with your restaurant name, correct Instagram handle, hashtag, and location tag
- Ensure your Wi-Fi works and is fast -- influencers will post in real-time

**Budget: 50,000-120,000 THB** including food, beverage, photographer, and any farewell gifts.

### Influencer Stagger Strategy (3 Days Before Through 10 Days After Opening)

Do not invite all your influencers on the same night. Stagger their visits across a 14-day window so that content about your restaurant appears in Bangkok's social media feeds continuously, creating the impression of sustained momentum rather than a one-day spike.

**Suggested stagger:**
- **Days -3 to -1 (soft opening week):** 3-5 influencers
- **Days 1-3 (opening weekend):** 5-8 influencers
- **Days 4-7 (first full week):** 4-6 influencers
- **Days 8-14 (second week):** 3-5 influencers

Each influencer sees your restaurant at a slightly different stage -- early visitors experience the excitement of being first, while later visitors benefit from the operational improvements you make during the first week. Both perspectives generate authentic content.

## Grand Opening Event Planning

Your grand opening is not your most important marketing moment -- it is one moment in a sustained campaign. But it is the most visible one, and getting it right sets the tone for everything that follows.

### Choosing Your Opening Date

In Thailand, the choice of opening date carries weight beyond logistics. Several factors unique to this market should influence your timing:

**Auspicious dates.** Many Thai restaurant owners consult a monk or astrologer to select an auspicious opening date based on the Thai lunar calendar. Whether or not you personally believe in this practice, be aware that your Thai staff, partners, and some customers will notice and care. Auspicious dates for business openings are typically Tuesdays and Thursdays, and specific dates are published annually based on the Chinese and Thai calendars.

**Monk blessing ceremony.** It is customary in Thailand to invite monks to bless a new business before it opens. This typically happens on the morning of your opening day or a day before. The ceremony (tam bun) involves 1, 3, 5, or 9 monks who chant, sprinkle holy water, and receive food offerings. Budget: 3,000-10,000 THB for monk offerings, food, and supplies. Even if you are a foreign owner, hosting this ceremony shows respect for Thai culture and is meaningful to your Thai staff.

**Avoid major holidays.** Do not open during Songkran (mid-April), when Bangkok empties. Avoid opening the week of major religious holidays (Makha Bucha, Visakha Bucha) when alcohol sales are banned. Steer clear of long weekends when your target audience may be traveling.

**Day of week.** Open on a Wednesday, Thursday, or Friday. This gives you 2-3 days to settle into operations before the weekend rush. Opening on a Saturday means your highest-traffic night is also your most chaotic and least experienced service.

**Season.** The cool season (November-February) is the best time to open a restaurant in Bangkok -- weather is pleasant, tourist numbers are at their peak, and outdoor dining areas can be used comfortably. Avoid opening during peak rainy season (August-October) when foot traffic drops 20-30%.

<Callout type="tip">
If you are choosing a location and concept that will rely on both Thai and tourist customers, plan your opening for late November or early December. This catches the start of the cool season, the tourist high season, and the corporate end-of-year dining rush all at once, giving you the best possible conditions for a strong first 90 days.
</Callout>

### Opening Night Event

Your opening night should be an invitation-only event, not a public free-for-all. A controlled guest list lets you manage capacity, curate the experience, and ensure the people in the room are the ones most likely to generate word-of-mouth.

**Guest list (80-150 people depending on your capacity):**
- Influencers and media who could not attend the preview dinner
- Local business owners from neighboring establishments
- Potential corporate clients (EAs, HR managers, event planners)
- Friends, family, and early supporters
- VIP customers who signed up pre-opening through LINE or Instagram

**Event format:**
- Cocktail reception style works best for large groups (no fixed seating, more mingling)
- Offer a curated selection of 8-10 menu items as passed canapes, plus a signature cocktail
- A brief speech by the owner (3-5 minutes, not 15 -- no one came to hear a monologue)
- Background music (live acoustic or DJ) appropriate to your concept
- A photographer capturing the evening

**Do not try to do table service on opening night.** A cocktail format with passed food lets you serve 100+ people with less kitchen stress than trying to plate 50 full three-course dinners on the most chaotic night of your restaurant's life. Save the full dining experience for the following nights when your team is warmed up.

## Launch Day Operations Prep

Marketing is only half the equation. The other half is making sure your restaurant can actually deliver on the promise your marketing creates. A beautifully marketed opening that results in 90-minute wait times, cold food, and confused staff will generate the kind of attention you do not want.

### Operational Readiness Checklist

**Kitchen capacity testing.** During your soft opening, push your kitchen incrementally. Night 1: 30 covers. Night 2: 50 covers. Night 3: 70 covers. Identify the breaking point -- where ticket times exceed 25 minutes or quality drops -- and set your maximum cover count 20% below that breaking point for the first two weeks. You can increase capacity as the team finds its rhythm.

**Service flow rehearsal.** Walk your entire service team through a mock service: greeting, seating, menu presentation, ordering, food running, check delivery, and farewell. Do this at least twice before opening night. Every server should know the floor plan, the table numbers, the menu inside and out, and the current promotions.

**POS and payment testing.** Run test transactions through your POS system for every payment method you accept: cash, credit card, PromptPay, LINE Pay, Rabbit Card. Test split checks, discounts, and refunds. A POS crash during your first Friday dinner rush is preventable.

**Delivery platform dry run.** If you are launching on Grab Food and LINE MAN simultaneously with your dine-in opening, do a dry run the day before: process test orders, verify that your tablet notifications work, confirm packaging holds up during transport, and time the preparation of delivery orders alongside dine-in orders. If you find that delivery orders compromise dine-in quality, consider delaying delivery launch by 1-2 weeks.

**Staff briefing on marketing.** Every team member should know:
- How to mention the LINE Official Account and encourage sign-ups
- How to ask for Google and Wongnai reviews at the right moment
- The current promotions and how to explain them
- Your Instagram handle (so they can tell customers who ask for it)
- How to handle influencers (seat them at well-lit tables, send chef's selections, introduce the chef)

## The First Week Marketing Calendar

Your opening night generates a burst of attention. The first week after opening determines whether that attention converts to sustained traffic or fades in a week. Here is a day-by-day marketing calendar.

**Day 1 (Opening Night):**
- Opening event (invitation only)
- Photographer captures content
- Post real-time Stories throughout the evening
- Team reposts all customer and influencer content
- Publish a "We're Open" post on Instagram, Facebook, and Google Business Profile

**Day 2:**
- First day of public walk-in service
- Post opening night recap (best photos from the event)
- Staggered influencer visit #1-2
- Respond to all social media comments and messages from opening night
- Launch opening promotions (free drink, LINE signup discount, etc.)

**Day 3:**
- Post a behind-the-scenes Reel (kitchen during first service, chef plating)
- Staggered influencer visit #3-4
- First Google Business Profile post ("Now Open -- visit us at [address]")
- Reply to any early Wongnai or Google reviews

**Day 4:**
- Customer spotlight post (repost UGC from opening night or first customers)
- Begin LINE broadcast to early followers (thank you message + current offer)
- Staggered influencer visit #5
- Update any operational details based on first-week learnings (adjust hours if needed)

**Day 5:**
- Food photography post (hero dish close-up)
- Post weekend reservation availability on Stories
- Staggered influencer visit #6-7

**Day 6-7 (First Weekend):**
- Full social media coverage of weekend service
- Instagram Stories: busy dining room, cocktails being made, happy customers
- Post weekend special or promotion
- Thank early supporters via Stories

<Callout type="info">
Your first week is your highest-leverage marketing period. The content you create and the impressions you generate this week will define your restaurant's digital presence for the next 2-3 months. Assign a specific team member (or hire a temporary content creator) to focus exclusively on social media during launch week. This is not the time to try to run social media between expediting tickets and seating guests.
</Callout>

## The First Month Marketing Calendar

After the opening week buzz fades, the risk of a traffic dip is real. The first month is about converting opening excitement into sustainable traffic patterns.

### Week 2: Momentum Maintenance

- Continue staggered influencer visits (3-5 more across the week)
- Publish 5 feed posts and 3 Reels, maintaining the rhythm you established in week 1
- Run a "Second Visit" promotion for opening week customers ("Show your opening week receipt for 15% off your next visit")
- Submit your restaurant to BK Magazine's "New Openings" section and Timeout Bangkok's listings
- Go live on delivery platforms if you did not launch simultaneously

### Week 3: Retention Activation

- Launch your LINE loyalty program (stamp card, birthday rewards)
- Send a LINE broadcast promoting the loyalty program to your current followers
- Shift some social media focus to behind-the-scenes content (give followers a reason to keep watching beyond the novelty of "new restaurant")
- Analyze your first 2 weeks of data: which days are slow, which dishes are popular, what are customers saying in reviews? Adjust your marketing messaging accordingly.
- If covers are below target, increase Instagram ad spend by 50% for 2 weeks, targeting "restaurant near me" searchers within 5 km

### Week 4: Evaluation and Adjustment

- Conduct a full marketing performance review (metrics below)
- Identify your top 3 performing marketing channels and double down
- Identify underperforming channels and either adjust strategy or reduce spend
- Plan your month-2 content calendar based on first-month learnings
- Begin planning your first seasonal or themed event (wine dinner, brunch launch, live music night)

## Press and Media Outreach Timeline

Getting media coverage requires proactive outreach on a specific timeline. Waiting for journalists to discover you organically is not a strategy.

### Timeline

**8 weeks before opening:** Send initial "announcement" emails to your media list. Brief, personal, with your concept one-pager attached. "We are opening [Restaurant Name], a [concept] restaurant in [neighborhood], in [month]. I would love to invite you to our media preview dinner. More details coming soon."

**4 weeks before:** Send your full press kit with confirmed media dinner date and a personal invitation. Follow up 3-5 days later with anyone who has not responded.

**1 week before media dinner:** Send a reminder with logistics (date, time, address, parking, dress code, any dietary needs).

**Day after media dinner:** Send a thank-you email with a link to high-resolution photos from the evening (make this easy for them -- a shared Google Drive or Dropbox folder).

**2 weeks after opening:** Follow up with media who attended but have not published yet. A gentle "Just checking in -- let me know if you need any additional information or photos."

**Ongoing (monthly):** Send brief updates when you have something newsworthy -- a new menu launch, an award, a collaboration, a seasonal event. Do not email monthly just for the sake of it. Only reach out when you have a genuine story.

### Thai Media Specifics

**Thai-language press matters.** If your target audience includes Thai customers, do not only pursue English-language media. Thai food bloggers, Wongnai editorial features, and Thai-language publications like Sanook and Pantip forums reach a larger Thai audience than BK Magazine and Timeout combined. Hire a Thai-speaking PR freelancer (10,000-25,000 THB/month) if you are not fluent, or ask a Thai business partner or manager to handle Thai media outreach.

**Pantip threads.** Pantip is Thailand's largest web forum, and the food subforum (Pantip Cafe Bakery) has enormous influence with Thai diners. A genuine, detailed post about your restaurant's opening -- written in Thai, with photos, menu information, and a personal story -- can generate significant discussion and traffic. This is not advertising; it is community participation, and the Pantip audience will reject anything that feels like a sales pitch.

**Wongnai editorial.** Wongnai publishes "Best Of" lists, new opening roundups, and feature articles. Contact their editorial team directly (not the business team) to pitch your restaurant for inclusion. Wongnai editorial coverage drives more Thai customer traffic than almost any other single media placement.

## Delivery Platform Launch Timing

Delivery platforms deserve their own strategic consideration during launch. Launching dine-in and delivery simultaneously is common, but not always wise.

### Option 1: Simultaneous Launch

**Pros:** Maximizes revenue from day one, captures the platform's "new restaurant" algorithm boost immediately, and establishes delivery as a channel before competitors in your area capture delivery customers.

**Cons:** Adds operational complexity during your most chaotic period, delivery orders can overwhelm a kitchen that is still finding its rhythm with dine-in, and packaging failures damage your rating before you have established a reputation.

**Best for:** Restaurants with experienced teams, delivery-optimized menus, and sufficient kitchen capacity to handle both channels.

### Option 2: Delayed Launch (1-2 Weeks After Dine-In)

**Pros:** Lets your team stabilize dine-in operations first, gives you time to refine packaging and presentation for delivery, and you can use the delivery launch as a second wave of marketing buzz ("Now on Grab Food!").

**Cons:** You miss the first 1-2 weeks of revenue from delivery, and you delay the start of your "new restaurant" algorithm boost on the platforms.

**Best for:** First-time restaurant operators, small kitchen teams, and concepts where food quality is highly sensitive to transport (ramen, items with crispy elements, delicate plating).

My recommendation for most Bangkok restaurants: launch delivery 5-7 days after your dine-in opening. This gives you a working week to stabilize kitchen operations while still capturing the delivery platform boost within your first month. For a deeper dive into delivery platform strategy, see: [What Thailand's Delivery Duopoly Means for Restaurants](/blog/thailand-food-delivery-2026).

## Loyalty Program from Day One

Most restaurants wait 3-6 months to launch a loyalty program. This is a mistake. Your opening period generates your highest volume of first-time visitors, and without a retention mechanism, most of them will never return.

### Why Launch Early

A customer who visits during your first week is excited, curious, and forming their first impression. If they have a good experience and you capture them in a loyalty program before they leave, the probability of a second visit within 30 days is roughly 40%. Without any retention mechanism, that probability drops to 12-15%. The math is straightforward.

### What to Launch

Keep it simple. A LINE stamp card is the easiest loyalty program to deploy in Thailand -- no app download, no separate login, no complicated points system. Everyone already has LINE.

**Structure:** 1 stamp per visit (or per 500 THB spent). 8 stamps earns a reward (free dessert, 200 THB credit, or a signature cocktail). Birthday bonus: 2 extra stamps plus a free birthday dessert for groups of 4+.

**Setup cost:** Zero to 5,000 THB, depending on whether you use LINE's built-in stamp card feature or a third-party LINE CRM integration with more customization.

**How to drive signups during launch:** Include the LINE QR code on every table tent, on the bill, and in your opening promotion materials. Train staff to mention it naturally: "If you join our LINE, you will get a free welcome drink today and start collecting stamps toward a free dessert."

A good loyalty program pays for itself within the first month. A restaurant with 600 new customers in its first month that captures 200 in a LINE loyalty program and converts 80 of them into second visits has generated 80 additional covers -- at roughly 650 THB average check, that is 52,000 THB in revenue from a free tool.

## Common Launch Marketing Mistakes

I have consulted on over 60 restaurant openings in Thailand. These are the patterns that consistently lead to failed launches.

**Opening too early.** The construction is not quite done, the menu is not finalized, the staff have not been trained, but the owner is hemorrhaging rent and decides to open anyway. First impressions are permanent on the internet. If a food blogger visits your unfinished restaurant and has a mediocre experience, that review exists forever. Wait until you are ready. An extra two weeks of rent (40,000-100,000 THB) is cheaper than a year of recovering from bad early reviews.

**Skipping the soft opening.** I covered this above, but it bears repeating. The soft opening is not optional. It is where you discover that your kitchen cannot handle more than 40 covers, that table 7 gets food 20 minutes late because the runner forgets it exists, and that your POS system crashes when processing split checks. Discover these problems with forgiving friends, not paying strangers.

**All budget on the party, none on month 2-12.** The 400,000 THB opening party that I described at the start of this article is an extreme example, but the pattern is common in milder forms. Owners blow 60-70% of their marketing budget on the opening event and have nothing left for sustained monthly marketing. The party generates a spike; the sustained marketing generates a business. Allocate no more than 25-30% of your first-year marketing budget to the opening period.

**Over-promising on social media.** Teaser campaigns that build enormous hype set expectations so high that reality disappoints. If your pre-opening Instagram shows professionally styled food shots, celebrity chef endorsements, and promises of "Bangkok's most revolutionary dining experience," customers arrive expecting perfection. When they get a very good (but not revolutionary) meal from a restaurant still finding its feet, they feel let down. Build anticipation, but keep your promises deliverable.

**No review strategy.** Restaurants that do not actively encourage reviews during the first month miss the window when customers are most enthusiastic. A restaurant with 5 Google reviews and 3 Wongnai reviews after a month looks dead. A restaurant with 50 and 30 looks thriving. The difference is not traffic -- it is whether you asked.

**Ignoring Thai cultural elements.** A foreign owner who opens without a monk blessing, chooses an inauspicious date, or dismisses Thai customs as superstition will alienate Thai staff and miss an opportunity to build goodwill with the local community. These gestures cost almost nothing and demonstrate respect. Your Thai team will appreciate it, and so will Thai customers who notice.

## Measuring Launch Success

How do you know if your launch worked? Not by how the opening party felt, but by the data you collect during the first 30 days.

### Key Metrics to Track

**Covers per day (week 1 vs. week 4).** A successful launch shows either stable or increasing daily covers. A sharp decline from week 1 to week 4 means your opening generated attention but not repeat business -- a retention problem.

**Review volume and rating.** Target: 30+ Wongnai reviews and 20+ Google reviews within 30 days, with average ratings above 4.0 on both platforms. If you are below these numbers, your review-generation tactics need attention.

**LINE follower count.** Target: 300-800 LINE followers in the first month, depending on your cover volume. This is your owned audience for future retention marketing.

**Instagram follower growth.** Target: 1,500-5,000 followers within 30 days of opening, depending on your pre-opening following and influencer investment.

**Delivery platform metrics.** Target: 100+ delivery orders in the first month, with a rating above 4.5 on Grab and LINE MAN.

**Revenue vs. projections.** Track daily revenue against your business plan projections. Most restaurants hit 50-70% of projected revenue in month 1 and reach 80-100% by month 3. If you are below 40% of projections by end of month 1, there is a structural problem -- either with the concept, the marketing, or the operations -- that needs immediate diagnosis.

**Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC).** Total first-month marketing spend divided by total first-month new customers. For casual dining in Bangkok, a CAC under 200 THB per customer is strong. Above 400 THB is concerning and suggests your marketing is inefficient or reaching the wrong audience.

## Thailand-Specific Launch Considerations

Every market has its quirks, and Thailand has more than most when it comes to restaurant launches. These are the things that surprised foreign clients and tripped up even experienced local operators.

**Auspicious dates are real business considerations.** I mentioned this in the opening date section, but it goes deeper. Some Thai landlords will not hand over keys on inauspicious dates. Some Thai partners will refuse to sign contracts on certain days. Your Thai staff may be visibly uneasy if you open on a date considered unlucky. Consult the Thai calendar (or ask a Thai partner to) and at minimum avoid dates that are widely considered inauspicious.

**The monk blessing is not optional.** In Thai business culture, opening without a monk blessing (tam bun) is roughly equivalent to opening without a sign on the door -- technically possible, but something everyone notices and many silently judge. For a foreign owner, hosting this ceremony is one of the most effective cultural goodwill gestures you can make. It typically takes 30-60 minutes in the morning, involves offerings of food and flowers, and your Thai staff will help organize it if you ask.

**Word-of-mouth operates through LINE groups.** In Western markets, word-of-mouth happens on social media and in conversation. In Thailand, it happens in LINE groups -- neighborhood groups, office groups, friend groups, family groups. When a Thai customer discovers a restaurant they like, they share the Instagram post or Google Maps link directly into 3-5 LINE groups. This is why capturing customers on LINE and making your content easily shareable matters so much.

**Thai media works on personal relationships.** Cold pitching Thai food writers rarely works. The food media community in Bangkok is small and relationship-driven. If you are a foreign owner without existing connections, invest time in attending food events, dining at food writers' recommended restaurants, and engaging with their content genuinely before asking for coverage. Or hire a Thai PR freelancer who already has these relationships.

**Alcohol advertising restrictions.** Thailand has strict laws on alcohol advertising. You cannot show alcohol bottles or brand names in social media posts (technically), and you cannot promote alcohol discounts publicly. This is inconsistently enforced but worth knowing -- your "Happy Hour: Half-Price Cocktails" Instagram post could technically result in a fine. Many restaurants navigate this by promoting "drink specials" without naming specific alcoholic products or showing bottles. For the full regulatory picture, see: [Thailand's Alcohol Law Guide](/blog/thailand-new-alcohol-law).

**Seasonal timing matters more than you think.** Bangkok has distinct seasons -- hot (March-May), rainy (June-October), and cool (November-February) -- and each has a direct impact on restaurant traffic. The cool season brings peak tourist traffic, pleasant outdoor dining weather, and the corporate entertainment season. Opening in the cool season gives you the best possible conditions. Opening in August means your first three months are rainy season, when foot traffic drops 20-30% and outdoor seating is unusable.

Thailand's cafe scene has exploded. Bangkok alone has an estimated 8,000+ coffee shops, and the number keeps growing. Chiang Mai, Chiang Rai, and the islands are equally saturated. Yet new cafes continue to open, and some of them do very well.

The difference between the cafes that thrive and the ones that close in 8 months comes down to location, financial discipline, and understanding what business you are actually in. A cafe is a beverage business that happens to serve some food. The economics are different from a restaurant in ways that catch people off guard.

I have helped open over 30 cafes across Thailand. Here is what works.

## The Cafe Business Model: Why It Is Different

A cafe's financial structure is built around beverages, not food. Your beverage gross margin should be 75-85%. A latte that costs you 20-25 THB in ingredients (coffee, milk, cup, lid) sells for 120-160 THB, which works out to an 80%+ margin.

Compare that to food, where margins run 60-70% at best. Your avocado toast costs 60 THB in ingredients and sells for 180 THB — decent margin, but nothing like the latte sitting next to it.

This means your success depends on:

- **Beverage volume:** how many drinks you sell per day
- **Average beverage price:** whether you can push customers toward higher-margin specialty drinks
- **Dwell time management:** how long each customer occupies a seat
- **Food attachment rate:** what percentage of drink buyers also order food

A cafe that sells 150 drinks per day at an average price of 120 THB generates 18,000 THB/day in beverage revenue, or roughly 540,000 THB/month. With 80% beverage margins, that is 432,000 THB in gross profit from beverages alone — before food revenue.

<Callout type="info">
The breakeven point for most Bangkok cafes is 100-150 drinks per day, depending on your rent and staffing level. If you are consistently selling fewer than 80 drinks per day after 3 months, you have a location or concept problem that needs to be addressed immediately.
</Callout>

## Cafe Types: Pick Your Model

Before anything else, decide what type of cafe you are building. Each model has different cost structures, customer profiles, and operational requirements.

### Specialty Coffee Shop

Focus on high-quality single-origin coffee, pour-over methods, and coffee education — attracts coffee enthusiasts and the Instagram crowd. Higher equipment costs (expect to spend 300,000-500,000 THB on an espresso machine alone), but also higher average check (140-200 THB per drink).

**Best locations:** Ari, Ekkamai, Charoen Krung, Chiang Mai Old City

### Cafe-Bakery

Coffee plus fresh-baked pastries, cakes, and bread. The food component requires baking equipment and a pastry chef, which increases both setup costs and daily operations. But the food margin on baked goods is strong (65-75%), and the visual appeal drives social media traffic.

**Best locations:** Thonglor, Ari, Sathorn residential areas, near universities

### Workspace Cafe

Designed for remote workers and digital nomads, with larger tables, reliable wifi, power outlets everywhere, and a tolerance for customers who sit for 4 hours on a single coffee. Revenue per seat is lower, but you compensate with high capacity and add-on sales (lunch, snacks, second and third drinks).

**Best locations:** Ari, Phrom Phong, near co-working spaces, Chiang Mai Nimman

### Brunch Cafe

Coffee is the supporting act; the main attraction is a substantial food menu with brunch classics, healthy bowls, and Instagram-worthy plating. Higher food cost and more kitchen staff, but also higher average check (300-500 THB per person).

**Best locations:** Thonglor, Ekkamai, Sathorn, Phuket resort areas

### Kiosk / Takeaway Only

Minimal footprint (5-20 sqm), no seating, and a focus on takeaway drinks — lowest startup cost but also lowest revenue potential. Works in high-foot-traffic locations where rent per square meter is high but the absolute number is manageable.

**Best locations:** BTS/MRT station exits, office building lobbies, university campuses, inside malls

## Licensing: What You Need

The licensing requirements for a cafe in Thailand are the same as for a restaurant, with a few nuances.

**Company registration:** If you are a foreigner, you need a Thai limited company (51% Thai ownership). Registration costs 30,000-80,000 THB through a law firm. Full details in our [foreigner ownership guide](/blog/foreigner-opening-restaurant-thailand).

**Food and beverage license (Ror Ror 14):** Required for any establishment that prepares and sells food or drinks. Applied for at your local district office (amphoe). Cost: 2,000-5,000 THB. Processing time: 2-4 weeks.

**Health and safety certificate:** Issued after an inspection by the local public health office. They check food preparation areas, water supply, waste disposal, and staff hygiene. No fee, but you must pass the inspection before opening.

**FDA notification:** If you are selling packaged food items (bottled cold brew, packaged cookies) for retail, these must be registered with the Thai FDA. This applies to items sold outside your premises. Items prepared and consumed on-site are covered by your F&B license.

**Signage permit:** Any exterior signage in Bangkok requires a permit from the Bangkok Metropolitan Administration (BMA). Cost: 3,000-10,000 THB depending on sign size.

**Alcohol license:** Only if you plan to serve alcohol (some cafes serve wine or cocktails in the evening). This adds 30,000-60,000 THB in licensing costs and restricts your selling hours.

<Callout type="tip">
If you are opening a small cafe that only serves coffee and simple food (no alcohol, no tobacco), your licensing costs will be under 50,000 THB total. The process is straightforward — much simpler than a full restaurant with a liquor license.
</Callout>

## Location Strategy for Cafes

Cafe location decisions are different from restaurant location decisions.

**Cafes depend on habit.** A restaurant might attract customers from across the city for a special dinner, but a cafe's core customers visit 3-5 times per week because it is on their commute, near their office, or in their neighborhood. Your location needs to be embedded in someone's daily routine.

**Visibility matters more.** Restaurants can survive on side streets with good reviews. Cafes need to be seen. A ground-floor corner unit with glass frontage generates significantly more walk-in traffic than a second-floor unit around the corner.

**Proximity to other cafes is not always bad.** In Bangkok, cafe clusters (like the stretch of Ari between sois 1-4, or Ekkamai soi 10) draw more coffee drinkers overall. Being the only cafe in an area with no foot traffic is worse than being one of five cafes in a busy cafe district.

### Bangkok Neighborhood Guide for Cafes

**Ari** is Bangkok's unofficial cafe capital, with a high concentration of cafes, strong foot traffic, and relatively affordable rent (300-700 THB/sqm). Your competition is intense, but the customer base is established. Rent for a 60 sqm ground-floor unit: 25,000-45,000 THB/month.

**Thonglor/Ekkamai** draws customers with higher spending power and design-conscious taste, with rent at 500-1,200 THB/sqm. Customers expect polished interiors and specialty-grade coffee, and a 60 sqm unit runs 40,000-70,000 THB/month.

**Charoen Krung/Talad Noi** is the art district. Converted shophouses with character appeal to creative types and tourists, with moderate rent (200-500 THB/sqm), though spaces often need significant renovation. The area is growing fast.

**Silom/Sathorn** is the office district. Strong weekday morning and lunch traffic, dead on weekends. Works for takeaway-focused concepts near office towers.

**Lat Phrao/Ratchada** are emerging areas with lower rent and growing residential populations. Good for neighborhood cafes targeting Thai customers. Rent: 150-350 THB/sqm.

**Near universities (Siam, Sam Yan, Rangsit)** you get high volume but low average spend. Students order the cheapest drink and stay for hours. This works if your rent is very low and you accept the high turnover, low-margin model.

## Coffee Sourcing in Thailand

Thailand has an advantage that most cafe markets do not: the country produces excellent coffee, and you can source it locally.

### Thai-Grown Coffee

Thailand produces both arabica and robusta. The arabica-growing regions, Chiang Rai (Doi Tung, Doi Chang), Chiang Mai (Doi Suthep), and Nan, produce beans that compete with Central American and African origins.

Thai specialty-grade green beans run 300-800 THB/kg, depending on processing method and lot size. Washed process is most common; natural and honey process lots command higher prices.

For wholesale supply, look into Roots Coffee Roastery, Bluekoff, Gallery Drip Coffee, Brave Roasters, and Nana Coffee Roasters. They all supply cafes directly with minimum order quantities of 5-10 kg per order.

**Why source Thai coffee:**
- Lower shipping costs and faster delivery than imports
- Fresher beans (roasted-to-cup in days, not weeks)
- Strong marketing angle: "locally sourced" resonates with both Thai and international customers
- Supports Thai coffee farming communities

### Imported Coffee

If your concept requires specific origins (Ethiopian single-origin, Guatemalan micro-lots), Bangkok-based importers like Cerv Coffee, Pacamara, and Bluekoff can supply green or roasted beans. Import duties on coffee beans are 5%, and you will need an FDA import license for green beans.

### Roast Your Own vs. Buy Roasted

**Buy roasted (recommended for most new cafes):** Work with a local roaster who supplies roasted beans on a weekly or bi-weekly schedule. Lower startup cost (no roasting equipment), consistent quality, and one less thing to manage. Wholesale roasted beans cost 450-1,200 THB/kg depending on origin and quality.

**Roast in-house:** Requires a commercial roaster (300,000-1,500,000 THB), proper ventilation, training, and a consistent green bean supply chain. In-house roasting makes financial sense only if you sell 100+ kg per month. It is a strong differentiator but adds real complexity.

## Milk: The Hidden Cost Center

In a cafe, milk is your second-largest ingredient cost after coffee. Thailand's milk market is limited compared to Australia or the US.

**Full-cream dairy milk:** Meiji, Dutch Mill, or imported brands. 50-80 THB/liter. This is your default latte milk.

**Barista-specific milk:** Brands like Minor Figures, Bonsoy (imported), or Oatly are available in Bangkok but expensive: 80-150 THB/liter.

**Plant-based alternatives:** Oat milk, soy milk, almond milk. Growing demand, especially among health-conscious Thai customers and international visitors. Cost: 80-180 THB/liter.

For a cafe selling 100 lattes per day at 200ml of milk each, daily milk consumption is 20 liters. At 60 THB/liter, that is 1,200 THB/day or 36,000 THB/month just for milk. At premium pricing (100 THB/liter for barista oat milk), that jumps to 60,000 THB/month.

<Callout type="tip">
Charge extra for plant-based milk (20-40 THB surcharge per drink). Most customers expect this. It protects your margin on a product that costs 50-100% more than dairy. Clearly display the surcharge on your menu.
</Callout>

## Staffing a Cafe

A 30-seat cafe needs 3-5 staff members for daily operations.

**Head barista:** 18,000-30,000 THB/month. This person sets the quality standard. They should have at least 1-2 years of specialty coffee experience and the ability to train others.

**Baristas (2-3):** 13,000-18,000 THB/month each. Thai barista talent is growing fast. The specialty coffee community in Bangkok is strong and produces skilled young baristas.

**Kitchen staff (if serving food):** 12,000-16,000 THB/month. Even a simple food menu requires at least one dedicated kitchen person during peak hours.

**Monthly staffing cost: 56,000-112,000 THB** (including social security contributions of 5% employer share).

<Callout type="info">
Barista turnover in Bangkok is high — average tenure is 8-12 months. Retain good baristas by paying above market, offering training opportunities (latte art workshops, cupping sessions, SCA certifications), and creating a positive work environment. Replacing a trained barista costs 2-3 months of their salary in recruitment and training.
</Callout>

### Barista Training

Whether your staff has coffee experience or not, invest in training:

- **Basic barista course:** 2-3 days covering espresso extraction, milk steaming, drink preparation. Available from Roots Academy, Cerv Coffee, or individual trainers. Cost: 5,000-15,000 THB per person.
- **SCA (Specialty Coffee Association) certification:** Foundation and Intermediate levels. Available in Bangkok through accredited trainers. Cost: 8,000-25,000 THB per module. Worth it for head baristas.
- **In-house training program:** Document your recipes, extraction parameters, and service standards. Train every new hire on this system. Consistency is what keeps customers coming back.

## Menu Strategy

### Beverages: The Profit Engine

Your core menu needs:

- **Espresso-based drinks:** Espresso, americano, latte, cappuccino, flat white. These are 70-80% of revenue for most cafes.
- **Pour-over/filter coffee:** Single-origin offerings for the specialty crowd. Higher price point (150-220 THB), lower volume.
- **Cold drinks:** Iced coffee, cold brew, frappes. Essential in Thailand's climate. Cold brew is high-margin (batch-prepare overnight, serve all day).
- **Non-coffee beverages:** Matcha latte, chocolate, tea, fresh juice, smoothies. These serve non-coffee drinkers and expand your customer base. Do not neglect this category. It can be 20-30% of beverage sales.
- **Seasonal specials:** Rotate 1-2 specialty drinks monthly. Creates return visits and social media content.

### Food: Keep It Simple

The biggest mistake I see cafes make is trying to be a restaurant. Your food menu should be:

- **5-10 items maximum** at launch
- **Simple preparation** (toast, sandwiches, pastries, salads, rice bowls)
- **High margin** (65-75% gross margin)
- **Fast to serve** (customers in a cafe expect food within 5-10 minutes)

Menu items that work well in Thai cafes: croissants and pastries (buy from a wholesale bakery if not baking in-house), toast with toppings (avocado, egg, salmon), sandwiches and wraps, acai or smoothie bowls, rice dishes (simple Thai options for local customers).

Avoid anything requiring a full kitchen setup, anything with long preparation time, or complex plated dishes that slow down service.

## Monthly Financial Model

What does a typical 30-seat Bangkok cafe's monthly P&L look like once it is running at a sustainable level (months 6+)?

**Revenue:** 450,000-650,000 THB/month (150-200 drinks/day + food)
- Beverages: 70% of revenue
- Food: 30% of revenue

**Cost of goods sold:** 110,000-170,000 THB (COGS 25-28% blended)
- Coffee beans: 30,000-50,000 THB
- Milk: 25,000-40,000 THB
- Food ingredients: 40,000-60,000 THB
- Packaging/consumables: 15,000-20,000 THB

**Gross profit:** 340,000-480,000 THB (73-75%)

**Operating expenses:** 240,000-380,000 THB
- Rent: 30,000-70,000 THB
- Staff: 56,000-112,000 THB
- Utilities: 10,000-25,000 THB
- Marketing: 10,000-20,000 THB
- Accounting: 5,000-10,000 THB
- Equipment maintenance: 3,000-8,000 THB
- Supplies/cleaning: 5,000-10,000 THB
- Insurance: 2,000-5,000 THB
- Miscellaneous: 5,000-10,000 THB

**Net profit: 60,000-140,000 THB/month** (13-22% net margin)

This assumes you are past the initial ramp-up phase. Months 1-3 will be break-even at best, with full profitability typically arriving at months 4-8.

## Common Mistakes Specific to Cafes

**Overspending on aesthetics, underspending on equipment.** A beautiful interior with a cheap espresso machine produces bad coffee in a pretty room. Invest in the machine and grinder first, then allocate whatever is left to the fit-out.

**Not training staff on customer service.** Thai service culture defaults to polite but passive, and in a specialty cafe you want staff who can make menu recommendations, explain coffee origins, and guide customers toward higher-margin drinks. That requires active training.

**Ignoring delivery platforms.** Iced coffee and pastries travel well, and Grab and LINE MAN can add 20-30% to your revenue. Set up on delivery platforms from day one, but adjust your pricing to account for the 30-35% commission.

**Not selling retail beans.** Whole bean coffee sold retail (250g bags at 350-500 THB) has 50-60% margin and builds brand awareness. Every specialty cafe should offer beans to take home.

**Competing on price.** If your latte is 80 THB, you are competing with 7-Eleven and Amazon Cafe (the chain, not the website). You will lose. Charge 120-160 THB, use better ingredients, and create a better experience. The customers you want are willing to pay for quality.

## Timeline: From Concept to Opening

**Week 1-4:** Concept definition, financial modeling, location scouting, legal setup
**Week 5-8:** Sign lease, begin design, order equipment (espresso machines often have 4-6 week lead times)
**Week 9-14:** Renovation, equipment installation, menu development, staff hiring
**Week 15-16:** Barista training, soft opening with friends and family, social media launch
**Week 17:** Grand opening

**Total: 4-5 months** from "go" to opening day.

This is faster than a full restaurant because the build-out is simpler and the menu requires less development. The bottleneck is usually the renovation, so get your contractor booked early.

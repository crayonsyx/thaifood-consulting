"How much does it actually cost to open a restaurant in Bangkok?" I get this question more than any other. The answer depends on what you are building, but I can give you real ranges based on projects I have worked on.

This article breaks down costs by restaurant type, covering every major expense category. The numbers come from Bangkok market rates as of early 2026. Prices in other Thai cities (Chiang Mai, Phuket, Pattaya) run 20-40% lower across most categories.

<Callout type="info">
All figures are in Thai Baht (THB). At the time of writing, 1 USD is approximately 35.5 THB. I have included USD equivalents for reference, but always budget in the local currency to avoid exchange rate surprises.
</Callout>

## Cost Structure Overview

Every restaurant opening has six major cost buckets:

1. **Rent and deposits** — your biggest upfront commitment
2. **Renovation and fit-out** — transforming a raw space into a restaurant
3. **Equipment** — kitchen, bar, POS, furniture
4. **Licenses and legal** — company setup, permits, professional fees
5. **Pre-opening costs** — staff hiring, training, soft launch, marketing
6. **Working capital** — cash to cover losses during the ramp-up period

The single biggest variable is renovation. A simple repaint-and-furnish job costs 3,000-5,000 THB per square meter. A full gut renovation with custom design runs 15,000-30,000 THB per square meter. That difference alone can swing your total budget by millions of baht.

A small cafe or coffee shop is the lowest-cost entry point into Bangkok's F&B market. These concepts rely on high-margin beverages, simple food menus, and relatively small footprints.

### Where the Money Goes

In a typical 5 million THB casual dining opening, costs break down approximately:
- Renovation & design: 35%
- Equipment (kitchen + bar + POS): 22%
- Rent deposits & key money: 15%
- Working capital: 18%
- Licensing, legal, pre-opening: 10%

<Callout type="warning">
The renovation budget is where most projects go over budget. Contractors in Bangkok commonly underquote by 20-30% and then add costs during construction. Get at least three quotes, check references, and build a 15% contingency into your renovation budget.
</Callout>

## Hidden Costs Most People Miss

Beyond the major categories above, budget for these commonly overlooked expenses:

**Grease trap installation:** Required by Bangkok municipal code. Costs 30,000-80,000 THB depending on kitchen size. Many landlords require this as a condition of the lease.

**Fire suppression system:** Commercial kitchen hoods require integrated fire suppression (Ansul or equivalent). Budget 50,000-150,000 THB.

**Electrical upgrade:** Thai commercial spaces often have insufficient power for a full kitchen. A 3-phase electrical upgrade costs 30,000-100,000 THB.

**Pest control contract:** Monthly service from a reputable company costs 3,000-8,000 THB/month. This is not optional in Bangkok's climate.

**Insurance:** Property, liability, and business interruption insurance runs 20,000-60,000 THB/year for a typical restaurant. Surprisingly few Bangkok restaurants carry adequate coverage.

**Accounting fees:** Monthly bookkeeping and VAT filing costs 5,000-15,000 THB/month. Annual audit (required for companies) adds 20,000-50,000 THB.

**Delivery platform commissions:** If you use Grab, LINE MAN, or Foodpanda for delivery, expect to pay 30-35% of each order. This eats directly into your margin.

## Cost-Saving Strategies That Actually Work

Not all cost-cutting is smart. Here is what works and what does not.

### Worth Doing

**Buy used equipment.** Bangkok has a healthy secondary market for commercial kitchen equipment. Check Facebook groups, Pantip forums, and restaurant liquidation sales. You can save 40-60% on items like refrigerators, prep tables, and dishwashers.

**Negotiate rent.** Landlords in Bangkok will negotiate, especially for spaces that have been vacant for 3+ months. Ask for 1-2 months of rent-free fit-out period. This is standard practice.

**Start with a limited menu.** A 15-item menu requires less equipment, less inventory, and less training than a 40-item menu. Launch lean, then expand based on actual demand.

**Use a shared cloud kitchen to test.** Before committing to a full lease and build-out, test your concept from a shared kitchen for 3-6 months. Monthly cost: 15,000-40,000 THB for a station.

**Hire Thai chefs.** An experienced Thai head chef at 35,000 THB/month will outperform a foreign chef at 120,000 THB/month in most Bangkok kitchen environments. They understand local ingredients, supplier relationships, and staff dynamics.

### Not Worth Doing

**Skimping on ventilation.** Poor kitchen ventilation leads to health code violations, staff complaints, and food quality issues. Budget properly for exhaust hoods and HVAC.

**Cutting working capital.** If you budgeted 600,000 THB in working capital and spend it on a fancier fit-out instead, you will run out of cash before reaching profitability.

**Doing your own accounting.** Thai tax law is complex and frequently updated. A professional accountant at 8,000 THB/month prevents penalties that can exceed 100,000 THB.

**Choosing a location solely on rent price.** A cheap location with no foot traffic is more expensive than a pricier location that brings in customers. Always evaluate rent as a percentage of projected revenue, not as an absolute number.

## Monthly Operating Costs: What to Expect After Opening

Once open, your ongoing costs follow a predictable pattern:

**For a typical 60-seat casual dining restaurant in Bangkok:**

- **Rent:** 80,000-150,000 THB/month
- **Staff (12-18 people):** 200,000-350,000 THB/month (including social security)
- **Food costs:** 30-35% of food revenue
- **Beverage costs:** 20-25% of beverage revenue
- **Utilities:** 20,000-50,000 THB/month (electricity is the big one — kitchen AC and equipment)
- **Marketing:** 10,000-30,000 THB/month (social media, promotions)
- **Supplies & consumables:** 10,000-25,000 THB/month
- **Accounting & admin:** 8,000-15,000 THB/month
- **Maintenance & repairs:** 5,000-15,000 THB/month (budget for this — equipment breaks)

**Breakeven point for most casual restaurants: 800,000-1,200,000 THB/month in revenue** (roughly 25,000-40,000 THB/day).

<Callout type="warning">
Electricity costs in Bangkok are higher than most people expect. A restaurant with a full kitchen, walk-in cooler, and air conditioning can easily spend 30,000-50,000 THB/month on electricity alone. Request utility history from the landlord before signing your lease.
</Callout>

## Making Your Budget

Here is my recommended approach to building your restaurant budget:

1. **Start with your concept and location.** These determine everything else.
2. **Get three contractor quotes** for renovation before committing to a space.
3. **Price your menu** against actual Bangkok ingredient costs (visit Makro, Or Tor Kor, local markets).
4. **Build a monthly P&L projection** with conservative revenue assumptions (assume 50% capacity for month 1, growing 10% per month).
5. **Add 15-20% contingency** to your total budget for unexpected costs.
6. **Never invest more than you can afford to lose.** Restaurants are risky investments. Do not mortgage your house.

If your total budget falls below the minimum range for your concept type, you are not ready to open. It is far better to delay 6 months and save more capital than to open underfunded and close in 4 months.

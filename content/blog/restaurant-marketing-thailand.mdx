The best restaurant I ever watched fail had extraordinary food. A former Gaggan sous chef running a 30-seat modern Thai concept in Ari, plates that could have competed for a Michelin star, and a dining room that was empty four nights a week. He spent 12 million THB on the build-out and exactly zero on marketing. "The food speaks for itself," he told me. The food spoke to no one, because no one knew it existed.

He closed in month seven.

I bring this up because "build it and they will come" is still the default marketing strategy for roughly half the restaurant owners I meet. In a city with over 100,000 restaurants, that is not optimism -- it is a business plan with a hole in the middle. Bangkok diners have infinite choices, short attention spans, and a discovery process that runs almost entirely through their phones. If you are not showing up on those phones, you are invisible.

This guide covers every marketing channel that matters for restaurants in Thailand right now, with real costs, real timelines, and the specific tactics I have seen generate measurable returns. Whether you are planning a grand opening or trying to revive a restaurant that has gone quiet, everything here is built on what actually works in the Thai market -- not what works in New York or London.

## Understanding Thai Consumer Behavior First

Before spending a single baht on marketing, you need to understand how Thai consumers discover and choose restaurants. The process is fundamentally different from Western markets, and I have seen foreign owners waste hundreds of thousands of baht on channels that do not matter here.

### How Thais Find Restaurants

**Social media is the starting point, not a supplement.** A 2025 survey by the Electronic Transactions Development Agency (ETDA) found that 78% of Bangkok residents aged 18-45 discover new restaurants through social media. Not Google. Not walking past. Social media first, then everything else.

The platform hierarchy in Thailand for restaurant discovery looks like this:

**Instagram** is the number one discovery platform for restaurants in Bangkok, especially for the 18-35 demographic. Thai diners scroll Instagram looking for food, they save posts of restaurants they want to try, and they make decisions based on how a restaurant's feed looks. If your Instagram is dead or ugly, you are losing customers before they even look at your menu.

**TikTok** has exploded as a restaurant discovery tool since 2024. Short-form video reviews, "what I ate" content, and viral food clips drive enormous traffic, particularly for casual and street-level concepts. A single viral TikTok video can generate 500-2,000 walk-in visits over a two-week period, which is something I have seen happen to three of my clients.

**LINE** is Thailand's dominant messaging platform with over 53 million users, and its role in restaurant marketing is unique to this market. LINE Official Accounts function as a hybrid of email marketing and loyalty programs. Restaurants use LINE to send promotions, manage reservations, and communicate directly with repeat customers. If you are not on LINE, you are missing the single most effective retention channel in Thailand.

**Facebook** remains relevant for older demographics (35+) and for restaurant groups and community pages. It is less important for discovery than Instagram or TikTok but still matters for event promotion and local community engagement.

### Wongnai vs. Google Reviews

This is where Thailand diverges sharply from Western markets.

**Wongnai** is Thailand's largest restaurant review platform, with over 10 million monthly active users. Think of it as Thailand's Yelp, but more influential. Thai consumers check Wongnai ratings before visiting a restaurant the way Americans check Google reviews. A restaurant with a Wongnai rating below 4.0 (out of 5.0) will struggle to attract Thai customers, regardless of how good the food actually is.

**Google Reviews** matter for tourists and expats but carry less weight with Thai diners for restaurant decisions. That said, Google Maps is still how most people navigate to your restaurant, so your Google Business Profile needs to be complete and accurate with current hours, photos, and location pin.

<Callout type="tip">
Claim your Wongnai business listing immediately after opening. Respond to every review -- positive and negative -- within 24 hours. Wongnai's algorithm favors active businesses, and Thai consumers notice when owners engage with feedback. A personalized "thank you" reply in Thai to a positive review costs nothing and builds loyalty.
</Callout>

**TripAdvisor** still matters if your target audience includes tourists, particularly in Phuket, Chiang Mai, and tourist-heavy Bangkok areas like Khaosan, Silom, and Sukhumvit. For restaurants targeting primarily Thai customers, TripAdvisor is low priority.

### The Thai Decision Funnel

Here is how a typical Thai diner in Bangkok decides where to eat tonight:

1. **Discovery** -- sees a restaurant on Instagram, TikTok, or a friend's LINE message
2. **Validation** -- checks the Wongnai rating, scrolls the restaurant's Instagram feed, looks at recent photos
3. **Logistics** -- checks Google Maps for location, parking, BTS/MRT proximity, and opening hours
4. **Booking** -- calls, messages via LINE, or walks in
5. **Sharing** -- photographs the food, posts to Instagram or Stories, tags the restaurant, leaves a Wongnai review

Every stage of this funnel is a point where you either keep or lose the customer. Your marketing needs to cover all five stages, not just the first one.

## Digital Marketing Channels

### Instagram: Your Most Important Channel

I wrote a [complete guide to Instagram marketing for Bangkok restaurants](/blog/instagram-marketing-bangkok-restaurants) because the platform deserves that level of detail. But here is the strategic overview.

Instagram is where Bangkok restaurants are discovered, evaluated, and shared. Your Instagram feed is your second storefront. For many customers, it is the first one -- they see your feed before they ever see your restaurant.

**What to post:**
- **Food photography** (40-50% of posts) -- your best dishes, beautifully shot, with natural lighting or professional studio work
- **Behind-the-scenes** (20-25%) -- kitchen prep, ingredient sourcing, chef plating, team moments
- **Atmosphere and design** (15-20%) -- interior details, table settings, lighting, the view
- **Customer moments** (10-15%) -- reposted UGC, group shots, special celebrations

**Posting frequency:** 4-5 feed posts per week, daily Stories, 2-3 Reels per week. This sounds like a lot. It is. Instagram rewards consistency, and the algorithm punishes gaps. A restaurant that posts daily will outperform one posting twice a week by a wide margin in reach and discovery.

**Budget for Instagram:**
- Professional food photography session: 8,000-25,000 THB per session (typically 20-30 edited images)
- Monthly content creation (if outsourced): 15,000-40,000 THB/month
- Instagram advertising: 5,000-20,000 THB/month for targeted local reach
- Total Instagram investment: 20,000-60,000 THB/month for a serious effort

### TikTok: The Viral Discovery Engine

TikTok is the fastest-growing restaurant discovery channel in Thailand. The platform's algorithm is different from Instagram in a way that benefits restaurants: your content can reach massive audiences even with zero followers, because TikTok prioritizes content quality over account size.

**What works on TikTok for Thai restaurants:**
- **Process videos** -- a chef flambing a dish, pulling noodles, decorating a dessert. Movement and transformation perform well.
- **"What I got for X baht"** -- value-demonstration videos showing what a customer receives at a price point. These go viral regularly in Thailand.
- **First-person reviews** -- staff or the owner walking through the menu, explaining dishes with genuine enthusiasm. Polished production is not required; authenticity performs better.
- **Trending sounds** -- using popular Thai audio clips and music overlaid on food footage. Stay current with trends.

**TikTok costs:**
- In-house content (phone + basic editing): essentially free
- Outsourced TikTok content creation: 10,000-30,000 THB/month
- TikTok advertising: 5,000-15,000 THB/month (local targeting)

<Callout type="warning">
TikTok can send a rush of customers to your restaurant overnight. That sounds great until your kitchen cannot handle the volume, wait times hit 90 minutes, and those new customers leave negative reviews. Before running any viral-focused campaign, make sure your operations can absorb a 200-300% spike in covers. Scale your marketing to match your capacity.
</Callout>

### LINE Official Account: Retention and Loyalty

LINE is where you keep customers, not where you find them. The distinction matters. Instagram and TikTok are acquisition channels. LINE is a retention channel. And retention is where the real money is -- acquiring a new customer costs 5-10 times more than keeping an existing one.

**How to use LINE for your restaurant:**

**Rich menus.** LINE allows you to create a visual menu at the bottom of your chat that links to reservations, your menu, your location, promotions, and your loyalty program. Set this up properly and it becomes a mini-app for your restaurant.

**Broadcast messages.** Send promotions, new menu announcements, and event invitations directly to your followers. Thai consumers are accustomed to receiving LINE messages from businesses and respond well, provided you do not spam them. One to two messages per week is the sweet spot.

**Loyalty integration.** LINE's built-in loyalty card feature lets customers earn stamps for visits or spending thresholds. This is simpler and more effective than a standalone app, because everyone already has LINE on their phone.

**LINE costs:**
- LINE Official Account: free for up to 200 messages/month, 1,500 THB/month for up to 5,000 messages
- Rich menu design: 3,000-10,000 THB one-time
- LINE CRM integration (advanced): 5,000-15,000 THB/month

### Google Business Profile: Your Digital Shopfront

Even though Google Reviews matter less than Wongnai for Thai customers, your Google Business Profile is still how people find your address, phone number, hours, and directions. Treat it as essential infrastructure.

**Optimization checklist:**
- Complete all business information (hours, phone, website, menu link)
- Upload 20+ high-quality photos (interior, exterior, food, team)
- Post weekly updates (Google Business posts -- these are free and underused)
- Respond to every review within 48 hours
- Add your menu as a link or directly in the Google menu section
- Ensure your map pin is in the exact right location (I have seen wrong pins cost restaurants 20-30% of their walk-in traffic)

## Food Delivery Platforms as Marketing Channels

Here is something most restaurant owners miss: Grab Food, LINE MAN, and Robinhood are not just delivery services. They are marketing platforms with millions of daily active users browsing for food.

### Platform Marketing Tactics

**Grab Food** has the largest market share in Thailand. Their advertising tools include:
- **Search ads:** Pay to appear at the top of search results for relevant keywords. Cost: 2-5 THB per click.
- **Banner promotions:** Featured placement on the home screen. Cost: 5,000-30,000 THB/week depending on location targeting.
- **Discount campaigns:** Platform-subsidized discounts where Grab shares the cost. These are invitation-based for newer restaurants.

**LINE MAN** is Grab's primary competitor, with particular strength in Bangkok. Their marketing tools are similar -- search placement, promotional banners, and coupon campaigns.

**First-90-days strategy:** When you first join a delivery platform, the algorithm gives you a visibility boost. This is your window to generate reviews and order volume. Run a 20-30% discount on your top 3-5 items for the first 60-90 days, invest 10,000-20,000 THB/month in platform advertising, and respond to every review. The goal is to hit 100+ reviews with a 4.5+ rating, which creates a self-sustaining flywheel of organic visibility.

## Offline Marketing That Still Works

Digital channels dominate, but offline marketing is not dead in Thailand. Several offline tactics generate meaningful returns for restaurants.

### Pop-ups and Collaborations

Pop-up events are a powerful marketing tool in Bangkok for two reasons: they generate social media content (people post about pop-ups), and they expose your restaurant to a new audience through the host venue's customer base.

**Types of pop-ups that work:**
- **Chef collaborations** -- partner with another restaurant's chef for a one-night or weekend menu. Both restaurants promote to their respective audiences. Cost: typically shared, 20,000-50,000 THB per restaurant for ingredients and staff.
- **Market pop-ups** -- Bangkok's weekend markets (Chatuchak JJ Green, Artbox, Jodd Fairs) draw thousands of food-motivated visitors. A weekend market stall costs 3,000-15,000 THB and doubles as a marketing activation.
- **Brand collaborations** -- partner with alcohol brands (Chang, Singha, craft breweries), food brands, or lifestyle brands for co-hosted events. The brand often covers costs in exchange for venue access and social media exposure.

### Events and Themed Nights

Regular events give customers a reason to come back and something specific to share on social media.

**Monthly wine dinner:** Partner with a wine distributor. They provide the wine at cost or free, you provide the food and the venue. Charge 2,500-5,000 THB per person for a 5-course pairing dinner. These are high-margin, generate premium content, and attract a valuable customer segment. Wine distributors in Bangkok (Italasia, Bangkok Beer & Beverages, Wine Gallery) actively seek restaurant partners for these events.

**Cooking classes:** Off-peak cooking classes at 1,500-3,000 THB per person generate revenue and create loyal customers with a personal connection to your restaurant. Tourist-heavy areas (Silom, Sukhumvit, Old Town) see particular demand.

**Live music or DJ nights:** A weekly DJ set or live acoustic session costs 3,000-8,000 THB per night and can increase Friday/Saturday covers by 30-50%.

## PR Strategies for Thailand's Food Media

Getting media coverage in Thailand requires understanding which outlets matter and how they operate. The food media world here is smaller and more personal than in Western markets.

### Key Media Outlets

**BK Magazine (BK.asia-city.com)** -- Bangkok's most established English-language dining guide. A review or feature in BK carries significant weight with expats and English-speaking Thai diners. They accept press releases and conduct independent reviews.

**Timeout Bangkok** -- part of the global Time Out network, with a dedicated food section. Their "Best Restaurants" lists drive significant traffic. They run both editorial reviews and sponsored content.

**Eater (coverage via freelancers)** -- Eater occasionally covers Bangkok openings and trends, typically through freelance contributors based in the city. A feature on Eater reaches a global audience.

**Wongnai editorial** -- Wongnai publishes curated lists and editorial features alongside their user reviews. A spot on a "Top 10" Wongnai list drives substantial Thai customer traffic.

**Thai-language media** -- Sanook, Pantip (forum), and Thai food bloggers have enormous reach. If your primary audience is Thai, invest in Thai-language coverage first.

### How to Get Coverage

**Press kit.** Prepare a simple press kit: 5-10 high-resolution food and interior photos, a one-page concept summary, a sample menu, chef bio, and contact information. Send this to editors and food writers 2-3 weeks before opening.

**Media preview dinner.** Invite 10-15 food writers, bloggers, and editors for a complimentary dinner before your public opening. Budget 30,000-60,000 THB for food and beverage. This single event can generate coverage worth 10-20 times the cost.

**Personal relationships.** Bangkok's food media community is small. Build relationships with key writers and editors through genuine engagement -- comment on their coverage, attend their events, and respect their independence. Do not expect coverage in exchange for free meals, but do make yourself accessible and interesting.

**PR agency (optional):** A food-focused PR agency in Bangkok charges 25,000-60,000 THB/month on retainer and manages media relationships, press events, and ongoing coverage. This makes sense for fine dining and premium concepts. For casual dining, handling PR yourself or through a freelance publicist (10,000-25,000 THB/month) is more cost-effective.

## Influencer Marketing in Thailand

Influencer marketing is effective in Thailand, but the pricing, tiers, and expectations are different from Western markets. Here is how it actually works.

### Influencer Tiers and Costs

**Nano-influencers (1,000-10,000 followers):** These are regular food enthusiasts with small but engaged followings. Many will post in exchange for a complimentary meal (500-2,000 THB value). No cash fee. The engagement rate is typically 5-10%, higher than larger accounts. Invite 10-20 nano-influencers per month for steady, authentic content.

**Micro-influencers (10,000-50,000 followers):** This is the sweet spot for restaurant marketing in Bangkok. These accounts have engaged, local audiences and are trusted by their followers. Cost: 3,000-15,000 THB per post, or a complimentary meal plus 2,000-8,000 THB. Expect 1 feed post and 3-5 Stories.

**Mid-tier influencers (50,000-200,000 followers):** Broader reach but lower engagement rates (2-4%). Cost: 15,000-50,000 THB per campaign (1 Reel or TikTok video + Stories). Best for grand openings and major promotions.

**Macro-influencers (200,000-1,000,000 followers):** Celebrity-level food accounts. Cost: 50,000-200,000 THB per campaign. These make sense for grand openings of premium concepts, but the ROI per baht spent is typically lower than micro-influencers.

**Celebrity endorsements (1,000,000+ followers):** 200,000-1,000,000+ THB. Rarely cost-effective for standalone restaurants. More appropriate for chain launches or brands.

<Callout type="tip">
My recommendation for most Bangkok restaurants: spend 80% of your influencer budget on micro-influencers (10K-50K followers). They generate the most qualified foot traffic per baht. A single macro-influencer post at 100,000 THB could instead fund 10-15 micro-influencer visits that collectively reach a similar audience with higher credibility and engagement.
</Callout>

### Managing Influencer Relationships

**Set clear expectations.** Before inviting any influencer, agree on deliverables: number of posts, Stories, tags, hashtags, and timeline. Put this in writing, even a simple LINE message confirmation.

**Do not over-control the content.** Thai influencers produce better content when given creative freedom. Provide your key messages (concept, signature dishes, location) but let them shoot and edit in their own style. Over-scripted influencer content looks fake and performs poorly.

**Track performance.** Ask influencers for screenshot analytics of their posts (reach, impressions, saves, shares). Build a spreadsheet tracking each influencer's performance over time. Double down on the ones who drive measurable results.

## Grand Opening Strategies

Your grand opening is the single highest-impact marketing moment you will have. It is also the most common moment for restaurants to waste money on the wrong activities. Here is a proven sequence.

### Phase 1: Pre-Opening (6-4 Weeks Before)

**Launch Instagram and Facebook accounts** with a "coming soon" post and construction/renovation progress photos. Aim for 500-1,000 followers before opening day through organic posting and targeted ads (budget: 5,000-10,000 THB). For the full pre-opening timeline, see: [The Complete Guide to Opening a Restaurant in Thailand](/blog/how-to-open-restaurant-thailand).

**Set up your LINE Official Account** and start building your list through a QR code displayed in your window, on your Instagram bio, and shared with friends and neighbors.

**Contact food media** with your press kit. Schedule your media preview dinner for 1-2 weeks before the public opening.

### Phase 2: Soft Launch (2-1 Weeks Before)

**Friends and family nights** -- invite 50-100 people across 2-3 nights to stress-test your kitchen, service, and systems. These guests are your first social media ambassadors. Encourage posting.

**Media preview dinner** -- host 10-15 food writers and influencers. This generates your first wave of coverage and content.

**Soft launch period** -- open to the public at reduced capacity (50-70%) for 1-2 weeks. This builds word-of-mouth while allowing you to fix operational issues before full launch.

### Phase 3: Grand Opening (Week 1)

**Opening event or party** -- a dedicated launch night with a special menu, drinks promotions, and a curated guest list. Budget: 50,000-150,000 THB depending on concept and scale. Invite your best influencers, media contacts, neighboring business owners, and potential corporate clients.

**Opening promotions** -- offer a specific incentive for the first 1-2 weeks. Examples: free dessert with any main course, 20% off for LINE followers, a complimentary welcome drink. These give customers a reason to visit now rather than "sometime."

**Delivery platform launch** -- go live on Grab Food and LINE MAN simultaneously with your physical opening. Run first-90-day promotional pricing.

## Marketing Budget: What Percentage of Revenue?

This is the question everyone asks, and the answer depends on your stage.

**Pre-opening and first 6 months:** 8-12% of projected revenue. This is your heaviest marketing investment period. You are building awareness from zero, and every marketing baht works harder when you have no existing customer base to lean on.

**Months 7-18:** 5-8% of revenue. You should have a base of regular customers and organic word-of-mouth by now, so the budget shifts from acquisition to retention and seasonal campaigns.

**Year 2+:** 3-5% of revenue. A well-established restaurant with strong review scores, a solid LINE following, and consistent social media can maintain growth at this level.

## Seasonal Campaigns in Thailand

Thailand's calendar creates natural marketing moments that smart restaurants leverage. Planning your campaign calendar 3-6 months ahead allows you to prepare content, negotiate influencer rates, and order promotional materials.

### Key Seasons for Restaurant Marketing

**Chinese New Year (January/February):** Massive dining occasion, especially for Chinese-Thai families. Set menus for 8-10 people (auspicious numbers) at 8,888 or 16,888 THB perform well. Chinese-influenced restaurants see 40-60% revenue spikes. Start promoting 4 weeks ahead.

**Songkran (April 13-15):** Thailand's biggest holiday. Bangkok empties as residents return to their home provinces. Tourist restaurants thrive, but local neighborhood restaurants see a 30-50% dip. Plan accordingly -- run staff holidays, do deep cleaning, or target the tourist segment specifically.

**Valentine's Day (February 14):** Bangkok takes Valentine's Day seriously. Special couple set menus at 2,500-6,000 THB per couple sell out at popular restaurants. Book influencer coverage 3-4 weeks early. Create a dedicated Instagram-worthy table setup that customers will photograph and share.

**Loy Krathong (November):** A beautiful Thai festival with genuine warmth. Restaurants near rivers or with outdoor spaces can host krathong-making activities alongside dinner. Budget: 5,000-15,000 THB for krathong materials and decorations.

**Year-end holidays (December):** Corporate dining peaks with end-of-year parties and team dinners. Promote group packages, private dining options, and corporate catering starting in October. A single corporate booking for 30 people at 1,500 THB/head is 45,000 THB -- worth the outreach. If you are still in the concept phase, our guide on [restaurant concepts working in Bangkok right now](/blog/restaurant-concepts-bangkok-2026) can help you identify which formats lend themselves to seasonal marketing.

**Rainy season (June-October):** The quiet season for many restaurants. Counter this with comfort food promotions, delivery-focused campaigns, and loyalty program incentives to keep regulars coming in despite the weather.

<Callout type="tip">
Build an annual marketing calendar with all Thai holidays, school breaks, and seasonal events mapped out. Plan and budget campaigns quarterly. The restaurants that struggle with seasonality are the ones that react instead of plan -- they scramble to create a Valentine's Day promotion on February 10 instead of having it locked and promoted by January 20.
</Callout>

## Loyalty Programs That Work in Thailand

Customer retention is cheaper than acquisition, and loyalty programs are the most measurable way to drive repeat visits. But not all loyalty programs work equally well in the Thai context.

### What Works

**LINE stamp cards.** The simplest and most effective loyalty format in Thailand. Customers collect digital stamps for each visit or spending threshold, and earn a reward (free dish, discount, birthday perk) after a set number of stamps. LINE's built-in loyalty card feature makes this easy to set up and track. Zero app downloads required.

**Birthday rewards.** Thai consumers respond strongly to birthday promotions. A free cake or dessert platter for birthday celebrations drives group dining visits (average 4-6 people) and generates social media content. The cost of one cake (200-500 THB) generates a table of 4-6 people spending 3,000-8,000 THB total.

**Referral incentives.** "Bring a friend who has never visited, and you both get 15% off" is a simple mechanic that works. Track it through LINE or a unique code system.

### What Does Not Work

**Complicated point systems.** If customers need an app download, a separate login, and a calculator to figure out their rewards, they will not bother. Keep it simple.

**Discounts without a floor.** "10% off everything, all the time" is not a loyalty program. It is a margin reduction. Tie rewards to specific behaviors: visit frequency, spending thresholds, or referrals.

**Physical cards.** Punch cards get lost. Use LINE or a digital system.

## Measuring Marketing ROI

Most restaurants I audit have no idea which marketing activities are generating returns. Without measurement, you are guessing which half of your budget is wasted.

### Key Metrics to Track

**Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC).** Total marketing spend divided by new customers acquired. Track by asking new customers "how did you hear about us?" at the point of service. Target: under 150 THB per new customer for casual dining.

**LINE follower growth.** Your owned audience -- track weekly. A healthy restaurant should add 50-200 new LINE followers per month.

**Review volume and rating.** Track Wongnai and Google review counts and ratings weekly. Target: 10+ new Wongnai reviews per month with a 4.3+ average.

**Social media engagement rate.** Likes, comments, saves, and shares divided by followers. Target 3-5% for a restaurant Instagram account.

**Delivery platform metrics.** Order volume, average rating, and conversion rate (views to orders) on Grab and LINE MAN. Both platforms provide this data in their merchant dashboards.

**Repeat visit rate.** What percentage of customers come back within 30 days? Track through your POS or LINE loyalty program. Target: 25-35% for casual dining, 15-25% for fine dining.

<Callout type="info">
Install a simple POS system with customer tracking from day one. FoodStory, Ocha, and StoreHub all offer customer analytics that let you measure repeat visits, average spend per customer, and visit frequency. This data is worth more than any marketing campaign because it tells you what is working without guesswork.
</Callout>

## Common Marketing Mistakes

After 15 years of watching restaurants market themselves in Thailand, these are the mistakes I see most often.

**Spending everything on the opening, nothing on months 2-12.** A grand opening generates a burst of attention, but it fades in 2-3 weeks. The restaurants that thrive are the ones with a consistent, month-over-month marketing rhythm. Budget for the marathon, not just the sprint.

**Ignoring negative reviews.** A one-star Wongnai review that goes unanswered does more damage than no review at all. Respond promptly, professionally, and with a genuine attempt to resolve the issue. Other potential customers read your responses as much as they read the review.

**Posting food photos with bad lighting.** This sounds minor, but it is not. A plate of beautiful food photographed under yellow fluorescent light looks unappetizing. Invest in a single professional photography session (8,000-15,000 THB) to get 20-30 images that will serve you for months. Or learn basic food photography with natural light.

**Marketing to everyone.** A restaurant in Ari targeting Thai professionals aged 25-35 should not be running the same campaigns as a tourist restaurant on Khaosan Road. Define your audience narrowly and speak directly to them.

**Not tracking anything.** "I think our Instagram is doing well" is not data. Track followers, engagement, website clicks, and attribute customers to channels. Without data, you are guessing which half of your marketing budget is wasted.

**Copying competitors' marketing.** The restaurant across the street runs daily 30% discounts, so you should too? No. Constant discounting trains customers to wait for promotions and destroys your margin. Compete on value and experience, not on price cuts.

For more on why restaurants fail beyond marketing mistakes, see: [Why Restaurants Fail in Thailand: 12 Reasons](/blog/why-restaurants-fail-thailand)

## Building Your Marketing Plan

Here is the framework I give to every new client. It is not complicated, but it needs to be executed consistently.

**Before opening:**
1. Set up Instagram, Facebook, LINE Official Account, Google Business Profile
2. Create a content calendar with 4-6 weeks of pre-opening posts planned
3. Book a professional food photography session for opening week
4. Build your media contact list and prepare a press kit
5. Identify 20-30 micro-influencers in your area and begin outreach
6. Register on Wongnai, TripAdvisor (if tourist-relevant), and delivery platforms

**First 3 months:**
1. Execute your grand opening sequence (media dinner, soft launch, opening event)
2. Post daily on Instagram, 3-5 times/week on TikTok
3. Run delivery platform promotions and advertising
4. Invite 8-12 influencers per month
5. Respond to every review within 24 hours
6. Track all metrics weekly

**Months 4-12:**
1. Shift budget from acquisition to retention (increase LINE, reduce influencer spend)
2. Launch a loyalty program through LINE
3. Plan and execute seasonal campaigns
4. Review performance data monthly and double down on what works
5. Build corporate and group dining relationships
6. Explore collaborations and events

**Year 2+:**
1. Maintain 3-5% of revenue as marketing budget
2. Focus on loyalty, repeat visits, and customer lifetime value
3. Refresh your photography and content quarterly
4. Pursue media features and awards
5. Consider expansion marketing if opening additional locations

Your concept, location, and budget will determine the specifics, but this structure applies to almost every restaurant format in Thailand. The restaurants that follow it consistently outperform the ones that market reactively.

For guidance on developing a restaurant concept that gives you something worth marketing, see our [concept development service](/services/concept-development). A strong concept makes every marketing baht work harder, while a weak concept means you are spending money trying to convince people to visit a restaurant that does not have a clear identity.

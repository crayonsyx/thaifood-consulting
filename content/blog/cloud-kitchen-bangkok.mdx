Cloud kitchens (also called ghost kitchens, dark kitchens, or delivery-only kitchens) are the fastest-growing restaurant format in Bangkok. No dining room, no front-of-house staff, no expensive street-facing lease. Just a kitchen, a delivery platform connection, and a menu built for transport.

A cloud kitchen costs 500,000-2,000,000 THB to launch, compared to 3-8 million THB for a casual dining restaurant, and you can go from concept to first delivery in 4-8 weeks instead of 5-7 months. If the concept does not work, you shut down and relaunch a new brand without losing millions in renovation.

The economics, though, are tighter than most people realize. After delivery platform commissions (30-35%), food costs (30-35%), packaging (5-8%), and operating expenses, your net margin per order is 10-20%. The math only works at volume.

I have helped launch over 15 cloud kitchens in Bangkok since 2022. Here is what I have learned about which models work, which do not, and how to think about this format.

## Cloud Kitchen Models in Bangkok

### Model 1: Shared Kitchen Space

You rent a station in a multi-tenant commercial kitchen. The operator provides the space, base equipment, utilities, and usually handles licensing. You bring your own specialized equipment, ingredients, and staff.

**Major shared kitchen operators in Bangkok:**
- **CloudKitchens (Travis Kalanick's company):** Multiple locations across Bangkok. Monthly cost: 25,000-80,000 THB per station depending on size and location.
- **FoodStory Kitchen:** Several locations, integrated with the FoodStory POS system. Monthly: 20,000-50,000 THB.
- **Kitchen Hub:** Co-working kitchen model with flexible terms. Monthly: 15,000-40,000 THB.
- **Independent shared kitchens:** Smaller operators across the city. Monthly: 10,000-35,000 THB.

**Pros:** Lowest startup cost, fastest launch time (2-4 weeks), flexible terms (month-to-month or 6-month contracts), no licensing headaches (the operator holds the license).

**Cons:** Limited customization, shared equipment can cause bottlenecks during peak hours, no control over neighboring kitchen operations (smoke, odors), limited branding opportunities.

**Best for:** Testing a new concept, solo operators, first-time cloud kitchen owners, low-budget launches.

### Model 2: Standalone Kitchen

You lease and build out your own kitchen space, separate from any shared facility. This gives you complete control over equipment, layout, hours, and operations.

**Typical space:** 40-100 sqm in a commercial or industrial area.

**Monthly rent:** 15,000-40,000 THB in non-prime areas (Lat Phrao, Bang Na, Ram Intra), 30,000-80,000 THB near central Bangkok.

**Startup cost:** 1-3 million THB including equipment, build-out, and licensing.

**Pros:** Full control over operations, equipment, and food quality. Can run multiple brands simultaneously. No shared kitchen constraints.

**Cons:** Higher startup cost, longer setup time (2-4 months), full responsibility for licensing and compliance, less flexibility to exit.

**Best for:** Experienced operators, multi-brand strategies, concepts requiring specialized equipment.

### Model 3: Restaurant-Attached Cloud Kitchen

You already have a restaurant and add delivery-only brands that run from the same kitchen during off-peak hours or from dedicated stations within your existing kitchen.

**Additional startup cost:** 50,000-200,000 THB (packaging, additional ingredients, delivery platform setup).

**Pros:** Uses existing kitchen capacity and staff, no additional rent, immediate launch.

**Cons:** Can overload kitchen during peak hours if not managed carefully, staff must learn multiple menus, potential brand confusion.

**Best for:** Existing restaurant operators looking to increase revenue and kitchen utilization.

<Callout type="tip">
Model 3 is the lowest-risk way to enter the cloud kitchen space. If you already have a restaurant that is slow during certain hours (typically 2-5 PM), launching a delivery brand that targets that window generates revenue from otherwise idle capacity. Many of my restaurant clients run 2-3 delivery brands alongside their dine-in operation.
</Callout>

## Delivery Platforms: The Core of Your Business

In Bangkok, three delivery platforms dominate the market. Your relationship with these platforms will largely determine your cloud kitchen's success.

### Grab Food

**Market share in Bangkok:** ~40%
**Commission rate:** 30-35%
**Payment terms:** Weekly settlement
**Strengths:** Largest user base, strongest brand recognition, most reliable logistics
**Self-delivery option:** Available through GrabExpress, which reduces commission to ~15% but requires you to manage delivery

### LINE MAN Wongnai

**Market share in Bangkok:** ~35%
**Commission rate:** 28-32%
**Payment terms:** Weekly settlement
**Strengths:** Deep integration with LINE (Thailand's dominant messaging app), Wongnai reviews drive discovery, strong among Thai users
**Self-delivery option:** Available

### Foodpanda

**Market share in Bangkok:** ~15-20%
**Commission rate:** 30-35%
**Payment terms:** Bi-weekly settlement
**Strengths:** Aggressive promotions, strong in some outer Bangkok areas
**Self-delivery option:** Available

### Platform Strategy

**Be on all three platforms from day one.** Each platform has a different user base, and you are leaving money on the table by limiting yourself to one. The incremental cost of being on multiple platforms is nearly zero, just menu setup and ongoing management.

**Invest in platform advertising during months 1-3.** New listings get a short visibility boost, but it fades fast. Platforms offer in-app advertising (paid placement in search results) starting at 500-2,000 THB/day. During your first 3 months, allocate 15,000-30,000 THB/month to platform ads while that initial momentum is still working in your favor.

**Run promotions strategically.** Free delivery, percentage discounts, and bundle deals increase order volume. Platforms subsidize some promotions, so the actual cost to you may be lower than the face value. Run 2-3 promotional campaigns per month during slow periods (Monday-Wednesday, 2-5 PM).

**Monitor your ratings obsessively.** A 4.5-star rating on Grab gets you 3x the organic visibility of a 4.0-star rating. Respond to every negative review and fix recurring complaints immediately. A single string of negative reviews can crater your order volume for weeks.

<Callout type="warning">
Platform commissions of 30-35% eat directly into your margin. On a 200 THB order, you receive 130-140 THB. After food cost (60-70 THB) and packaging (10-15 THB), your gross profit per order is 55-70 THB. If your average order value is below 150 THB, the math becomes very difficult.
</Callout>

## Menu Design for Delivery

A delivery menu is different from a dine-in menu. Several factors matter that do not apply in a restaurant setting.

### Travel Time

Food sits in a delivery bag for 20-40 minutes between your kitchen and the customer. Design your menu around items that maintain quality during this window:

**Travels well:** Fried chicken, rice dishes, curries, pasta with sauce on the side, burgers (wrapped properly), stir-fries with rice, grain bowls, some desserts

**Travels poorly:** Crispy items that get soggy (tempura, spring rolls unless packaged carefully), delicate plated dishes, items that must be served immediately (souffles, delicate salads), anything temperature-sensitive

### Portion Engineering

Delivery customers expect full portions. Skimpy delivery portions generate negative reviews faster than anything else. But overly generous portions destroy your food cost.

**The sweet spot:** Delivery portions should look generous in the container (choose container sizes that portions fill completely) while hitting your food cost target. This is a packaging design problem as much as a portioning problem.

### Price Point Optimization

The average delivery order in Bangkok is 150-250 THB per person, so price your items to land in this range after the customer adds a drink or side. Your main dishes should be 130-200 THB, with add-ons at 30-60 THB.

Orders below 100 THB are not worth the delivery logistics, while orders above 300 THB per person are rare on delivery platforms since customers at that price point generally prefer dining in. Build your menu around the 150-250 THB sweet spot.

### Menu Size

Delivery menus should be smaller than dine-in menus. Aim for 12-20 items:

- 6-10 mains
- 2-4 sides
- 2-3 drinks
- 1-2 desserts

A focused menu reduces inventory, prep time, kitchen errors, and food waste, while also making your platform listing easier to browse — delivery customers make decisions in 30-60 seconds.

## Multi-Brand Strategy

The most profitable cloud kitchen operators in Bangkok run 2-4 brands from a single kitchen. The equipment and staff stay the same; what changes is the menu and branding on each platform listing.

### Why Multi-Brand Works

**More platform visibility.** Each brand is a separate listing on Grab, LINE MAN, and Foodpanda. Three brands means three listings, each appearing in different search categories.

**Different customer segments.** Brand A is a Thai rice and curry concept (100-150 THB average order). Brand B is a premium burger concept (200-300 THB average order). Brand C is a healthy bowl concept (150-200 THB average order). Each targets a different customer need.

**Higher kitchen utilization.** Instead of one brand's orders trickling in at 5-10 per hour, three brands combined might generate 15-30 orders per hour, keeping your kitchen busy and your staff productive.

### How to Execute Multi-Brand

**Share base ingredients across brands.** Your chicken goes into Brand A's rice dishes, Brand B's chicken burgers, and Brand C's grilled chicken bowls. Shared ingredients mean higher purchasing volume (better supplier pricing) and less total inventory.

**Differentiate through sauces and presentation.** The protein and base ingredients stay the same. What changes between brands is the sauce, the toppings, the packaging, and the name. This is not deceptive, it is efficient.

**Stagger launch dates.** Get Brand A running smoothly with ratings above 4.5 before launching Brand B two months later, then Brand C two months after that. Trying to launch three brands simultaneously almost always results in operational chaos.

**Use distinct branding.** Each brand needs its own name, logo, food photography, and platform profile — customers should not know your three brands share a kitchen. Invest 15,000-30,000 THB per brand in branding and photography.

## Packaging: The Underrated Detail

In a cloud kitchen, your packaging is your storefront. It is the only physical touchpoint between your brand and the customer.

**Quality matters.** Flimsy containers that leak, lids that do not seal, and bags that tear all generate negative reviews and lost customers. Spend the extra 2-3 THB per order on proper packaging.

**Insulation matters.** Hot food needs insulated containers or bags, and cold items need separate packaging. A hot curry next to a cold drink in the same bag ruins both.

**Branding matters.** Custom-printed containers, stickers, and bags cost 20-40% more than generic packaging, but they build brand recognition and encourage re-orders. At minimum, use branded stickers on generic containers.

**Sourcing in Bangkok:** Packaging suppliers on Charoen Krung Road (the "packaging district"), Mega Bangna Packaging Market, or online via Lnwshop and Shopee. Minimum orders are typically 500-1,000 units per item.

**Budget:** 8-15 THB per order for branded packaging (container, lid, cutlery, napkin, bag, sticker).

<Callout type="tip">
Include a small insert card in every order with your brand name, a QR code to your LINE or Instagram, and a "rate us 5 stars" prompt. This costs 0.50 THB per card and drives repeat orders and positive reviews.
</Callout>

## Licensing for Cloud Kitchens

The licensing requirements for a cloud kitchen are the same as for a regular restaurant. You need a company registration and a food and beverage license.

**If using a shared kitchen:** The shared kitchen operator typically holds the master F&B license. You may still need your own company registration and may operate under the shared kitchen's license. Confirm this with the operator before signing up.

**If operating a standalone kitchen:** You need your own F&B license, which requires an inspection of your kitchen by the local health authority. The kitchen must meet standards for ventilation, waste disposal, pest control, and food safety.

**For multi-brand operations:** Each brand does not need a separate license. Your single F&B license covers all food prepared in your licensed kitchen, regardless of how many brands you sell under on delivery platforms.

**Food safety certification:** While not legally required for all operators, Grab and LINE MAN are increasingly requiring it from cloud kitchen operators. Budget 5,000-15,000 THB for a basic food safety training and certification program.

## Common Cloud Kitchen Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Underestimating platform commission impact.** When a customer pays 200 THB for an order, you receive 130-140 THB. Your food cost, packaging, and labor must all come from that 130-140 THB. Price your menu based on what you actually receive, not the customer-facing price.

**Mistake 2: Ignoring peak hour capacity.** Lunch (11:30 AM-1:30 PM) and dinner (6-8 PM) account for 70% of daily orders. If your kitchen can only handle 15 orders per hour and you get 25 in an hour, service times spike, food quality drops, and ratings suffer. Design your kitchen workflow for peak capacity.

**Mistake 3: Not optimizing for delivery platform algorithms.** The platforms show users a curated list of restaurants based on rating, speed, proximity, and promotional activity. A new listing with no reviews, no promotions, and slow delivery times gets buried. Actively manage your presence on each platform.

**Mistake 4: Skipping food photography.** The biggest factor in a delivery customer's ordering decision is how the food looks in the app. Professional food photography costs 5,000-15,000 THB per brand (15-20 items) and pays for itself within a week in increased orders.

**Mistake 5: Not tracking per-order profitability.** Some menu items are profitable on delivery; others are loss-leaders or actually money-losing after commission. Run the math on every menu item: (price received after commission) - (food cost + packaging cost) = contribution margin. Cut or reprice items with margins below 30%.

## Getting Started: Step by Step

1. **Define your concept.** What cuisine? What price point? Who is the target customer? Test the concept with friends and family first.
2. **Choose your model.** Shared kitchen (fastest, cheapest) or standalone (more control, higher cost)?
3. **Set up the business.** Company registration if needed, food safety certification, licensing.
4. **Design the menu.** 12-20 items optimized for delivery travel and platform pricing sweet spots.
5. **Build or rent the kitchen.** For shared kitchens, this takes 1-2 weeks. For standalone, 1-3 months.
6. **Get professional food photography.** Budget 5,000-15,000 THB. This is not optional.
7. **Sign up on all three platforms.** Grab, LINE MAN, and Foodpanda. Complete profiles with photos and descriptions.
8. **Launch with promotions.** Run opening discounts for the first 2-4 weeks. Budget 15,000-30,000 THB for launch promotions and advertising.
9. **Monitor and optimize.** Track orders, ratings, delivery times, and per-item profitability daily.
10. **Scale to multi-brand** once your first brand is stable (4.5+ rating, 40+ orders/day).
